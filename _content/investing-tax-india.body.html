Why Tax Literacy Is Worth More Than One Extra Percent of Return

An investor who spends two hundred hours a year hunting for an extra percentage point of return, and zero hours understanding how that return is taxed, has the priorities backwards. The difference between a well-structured and a careless approach to the same portfolio is routinely two to four percentage points a year — larger than almost any selection edge, available with certainty rather than probability, and entirely within your control.

The Heads of Income Your Investing Touches

Indian income tax computes your liability under five heads. Investing touches three of them, and the same transaction can fall under different heads depending on facts and intention. Getting the head right is upstream of every rate, every deduction and every form — and it is the question most retail investors never consciously ask.

Capital gains versus business income — the classification that changes everything

The same purchase and sale of listed shares can be taxed as a capital gain or as business income, and the consequences are very different. There is no single statutory test; classification is a question of fact determined on the overall circumstances, and CBDT has issued circulars intended to reduce litigation on this point. Two things are settled, and one is not — verify current positionSettled by the nature of the transaction: intraday equity (bought and sold the same day, no delivery) is speculative business income; and F&O on recognised exchanges is non-speculative business income by virtue of the specific carve-out in the proviso to s.43(5). Not settled: delivery-based equity trading in the grey zone between "investor" and "trader." CBDT has issued circulars permitting a taxpayer to adopt a consistent position on listed shares held for longer periods, with the key condition being consistency year to year — you cannot classify as capital gains when gains are taxed favourably and as business income when losses need wider set-off. How to count a holding period preciselyThe holding period runs from the date of acquisition to the date of transfer. For listed shares bought on an exchange, the acquisition date is generally the trade date, not the settlement date — but practice and documentation vary, and your broker's and registrar's statements are the primary evidence. "More than 12 months" means strictly more: an asset acquired on 10 April 2025 and sold on 10 April 2026 has been held for exactly 12 months, not more. Selling on 11 April 2026 is the safe side of the line. When a sale is close to a threshold, check the specific dates on the contract note and consider waiting — the difference between 20% and 12.5% on a large gain can be substantial.

The switch trap, in rupeesMoving ₹20,00,000 (2,000,000 rupees) from a regular plan to a direct plan of the same scheme to save 1% a year in expense ratio is a redemption and a fresh purchase. If the holding has an embedded long-term gain of ₹7,00,000, the switch triggers approximately ₹71,875 of tax at 12.5% after the ₹1.25 lakh exemption, plus cess. Recovering that at 1% a year on ₹20,00,000 takes between three and four years — and the new holding restarts its holding-period clock. The switch may still be right; the point is that it must be computed, not assumed. Doing it in tranches across financial years, using the exemption each year, is usually the cheaper route. The Section 112A Exemption, Grandfathering, and Tax-Gain Harvesting

Three related mechanics govern long-term equity taxation in India: an annual exemption that resets every financial year and is lost if unused; a grandfathering rule that protects gains accrued up to 31 January 2018; and a harvesting technique that converts the annual exemption into a recurring, compounding tax saving. All three are simple, all three are widely misunderstood, and all three are worth real money.

The exemption — precise mechanics

  • The exemption is ₹1,25,000 (125,000 rupees) per financial year as at FY 2025-26, raised from ₹1,00,000 with effect from 23 July 2024. Verify the current figure.
  • It applies to the aggregate of long-term capital gains chargeable under s.112A — listed equity shares and equity-oriented mutual fund units together. It is not per scrip, not per scheme, not per demat account, not per folio.
  • It applies per person. A spouse with independent income and their own investments has their own exemption. But note s.64 clubbing — income from assets gifted to a spouse is clubbed back with the giver. Gifting money to a spouse to use their exemption generally does not work for this reason. Verify and take advice.
  • It is use-it-or-lose-it. Unused exemption does not carry forward.
  • It applies after setting off capital losses, not before.
  • It does not apply to short-term gains under s.111A, to debt fund gains, to gold, to property, to unlisted shares or to foreign shares.
  • Scrip-wise details of s.112A gains must be reported in the return — see the ITR schedules section.

Grandfathering of pre-31-January-2018 equity gains

Long-term capital gains on listed equity were exempt until 31 March 2018. When s.112A introduced the charge, Parliament protected gains accrued up to 31 January 2018 by deeming a higher cost of acquisition. The rule is in the proviso to s.55(2)(ac) and it is more subtle than most summaries suggest.

GRANDFATHERING - THE RULE AND A WORKED EXAMPLE
Applies to listed equity shares and equity-
oriented MF units ACQUIRED BEFORE 1 FEB 2018.
VERIFY current provisions.

THE DEEMED COST OF ACQUISITION IS:

  HIGHER OF
    (a) actual cost of acquisition
    (b) LOWER OF
         (i)  the fair market value as on
              31 January 2018
              - for listed shares: the HIGHEST
                quoted price on a recognised
                exchange on 31-Jan-2018
                (or the last trading day before
                it if there was no trading)
              - for MF units: the NAV on
                31-Jan-2018
         (ii) the full value of consideration
              received on transfer

prevents grandfathering from creating an
ARTIFICIAL LOSS when the asset later falls
below its 31-Jan-2018 value.

  Deemed cost = higher of (2,00,000 ; 5,60,000)

  (The Rs 3,60,000 of gain accrued up to

  been Rs 9,20,000.

Buyback Taxation After October 2024

Share buybacks were, until recently, a tax-efficient way for a company to return cash: the company paid a buyback distribution tax and the receipt was exempt in the shareholder's hands. That changed with effect from 1 October 2024, and the new treatment is materially worse for most shareholders. This is one of the changes most likely to catch an investor out, because it reverses the intuition.

Cost Basis Events: Bonus, Split, Rights and Demerger

Corporate actions do not generally create taxable income, but they do change your cost basis and sometimes your holding period. Getting these wrong produces returns that look wrong and tax computations that are wrong, and the errors persist for years because nobody recomputes historical cost basis.

DEMERGER - COST APPORTIONMENT
s.2(19AA), s.47(vid), s.49(2C), s.49(2D).
VERIFY.

A company demerges a division into a new
"resulting company." Shareholders receive
shares in the resulting company. Provided the
demerger meets the conditions of s.2(19AA),
this is NOT a transfer for the shareholder and
no tax arises at the time.

YOUR POSITION
  Original holding: 500 shares of the demerged
  company, purchased 08-Mar-2022,
  cost Rs 6,50,000 (Rs 1,300 per share)

  Demerger ratio: 1 share of the resulting
  company for every 2 shares held.
  You receive 250 shares of the resulting
  company.

THE APPORTIONMENT RULE
  s.49(2C): cost of the RESULTING company's
  shares
    = cost of original shares
      x (net book value of assets transferred
         in the demerger)
      / (net worth of the demerged company
         immediately before the demerger)

  s.49(2D): cost of the ORIGINAL (demerged
  company) shares
    = original cost MINUS the amount computed
      under s.49(2C)

  THE COMPANY IS REQUIRED TO PUBLISH THE RATIO.
  Look for it in the demerger scheme documents,
  the exchange filing, or the company's investor
  relations page. DO NOT GUESS IT.

WORKED, assuming the published ratio apportions
28% of cost to the resulting company:

  Cost apportioned to resulting company
    = Rs 6,50,000 x 0.28  = Rs 1,82,000
    across 250 shares      = Rs 728 per share
  Cost remaining with demerged company
    = Rs 6,50,000 - 1,82,000
                           = Rs 4,68,000
    across 500 shares      = Rs 936 per share

  Explanation to s.2(42A): the holding period of
  the RESULTING company's shares INCLUDES the
  period the original shares were held.
  So the 250 new shares are deemed held from
  08-Mar-2022, and are already long-term.
  This is a genuinely favourable rule and is
  often missed - investors assume the new shares
  start a fresh clock and sell late or
  misclassify the gain.

    Cost (250 x 728)         Rs 1,82,000
    LTCG                     Rs 1,73,000

The Full Transaction Cost and Tax Stack

Every trade carries six or seven separate charges, levied by five different bodies, computed on different bases, with GST layered on some of them. Brokers advertise the brokerage and bury the rest. On a long-term portfolio the stack is almost irrelevant; on an active one it is the difference between a profitable strategy and a losing one.

Turnover Computation and Tax Audit Applicability

"Turnover" for a derivatives trader is not the notional value of contracts traded. It is a specific computed figure, and it determines whether a tax audit under s.44AB applies. This is the single most confused area in retail trading taxation, and getting it wrong in either direction is expensive — unnecessary audit fees on one side, penalty exposure on the other.

The turnover computation for derivatives rests on ICAI guidance, not on statute — verify the current positionThe Income-tax Act does not define turnover for derivatives. The widely followed basis is the ICAI Guidance Note on Tax Audit under section 44AB, which has been revised — notably on the question of whether option premium received on sale is included in turnover. Earlier editions included it; a later revision reflects a different view. Different chartered accountants take different positions, and some brokers' tax reports compute it one way while your CA computes it another. Confirm which edition of the Guidance Note applies and which basis your CA is using, and keep the working.

Set-Off and Carry-Forward Rules in Full

Losses are an asset, but only if you handle them correctly. The rules are a hierarchy: set off within the same head first, then across heads where permitted, then carry forward what remains. Each category has its own carry-forward period and its own restriction on what it may be set against. And one procedural requirement overrides everything: a loss can only be carried forward if the return was filed by the due date.

Practical loss-management rules

  • File by the due date, every year, without exception — even in a year with nothing to report, if you have carry-forward losses from earlier years you must keep filing to preserve them.
  • Short-term capital losses are more valuable than long-term ones, because they can shelter both STCG (at 20%) and LTCG (at 12.5%). If you have a choice of which losing position to realise, realising the short-term one preserves more flexibility.
  • Set off against the highest-rate gain first where the law gives you a choice. Sheltering 20% STCG is worth more than sheltering 12.5% LTCG.
  • Do not harvest losses against gains that are already covered by the s.112A exemption. That wastes both.
  • Track carry-forward losses in a permanent schedule with the assessment year of origin and the expiry year. The return's Schedule CFL requires this and reconstructing it later is painful.
  • Remember that crypto losses set off against nothing, including other crypto gains. Compute crypto separately and do not expect any relief.
  • If you have a non-speculative business loss and salary income, note that it cannot touch the salary. Many traders assume a trading loss reduces their salary tax. It does not.

NRI Investors — the Differences That Matter

Residential status under the Income-tax Act is determined by physical presence tests, not by citizenship or by which passport you hold, and it is determined afresh every year. For someone who moves abroad mid-career, the tax position of the same portfolio can change substantially.

Residential status rules are intricate — verify and take adviceThe tests under s.6 involve day-counts (the 182-day test, and a 60-day test extended to 182 days in specified cases), the "resident but not ordinarily resident" (RNOR) intermediate category, and — since Finance Act 2020 — a deemed residence provision for Indian citizens with Indian income above a threshold who are not liable to tax in any other country. The rules interact with FEMA residency, which is a separate and different determination. Do not self-assess residential status for a year in which you moved. Take professional advice. Do this before you pay anyone anythingSEBI publishes a searchable list of registered intermediaries on sebi.gov.in, covering investment advisers, research analysts, brokers, portfolio managers, mutual funds and others. Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from Indian investors. These are general explanations as at FY 2025-26 / AY 2026-27, not advice, and every one of them should be confirmed with a chartered accountant against your actual facts and the current law.

I only have salary and some mutual fund SIPs. Do I need to do anything beyond ITR-1?
If you redeemed any units during the year, you have capital gains and ITR-1 is not available — you need ITR-2. SIP contributions alone create no tax event; only redemptions, switches and dividend receipts do. Note that a switch between schemes, or between regular and direct plans, counts as a redemption. If you did nothing but contribute, ITR-1 may remain available subject to the other conditions. Verify the current year's form conditions at incometax.gov.in.
I made one F&O trade for ₹800 of profit. Does that really change my return?
Yes. F&O is non-speculative business income regardless of size, which means ITR-3, the business schedules, and a different advance-tax position. There is no de-minimis exemption. This is worth knowing before you make the trade rather than after. If you have already done it, the return is still perfectly filable — it is just more work, and most people in this position use a chartered accountant.
How is my SIP taxed — as one investment or many?
Each instalment is a separate acquisition with its own date and its own cost. A 36-month SIP creates 36 lots. On redemption, the holding period is computed lot by lot, and Indian mutual funds generally redeem on a FIFO basis within a folio. So a partial redemption after four years sells the oldest instalments first, which are long-term. A full redemption after 14 months produces a mix: the first two instalments are long-term and the rest are short-term. The registrar's capital gains statement does this computation for you — check it rather than estimating.
What exactly is the ₹1.25 lakh exemption and how do I use it?
It is an exemption of ₹1,25,000 (125,000 rupees) per financial year on the aggregate of long-term capital gains chargeable under s.112A — listed equity shares and equity-oriented mutual fund units combined. It is per person, not per holding or per account, it resets annually and is lost if unused, and it applies after setting off capital losses. The practical use is tax-gain harvesting: each year, realise roughly ₹1,25,000 of long-term gain and immediately repurchase the same exposure, which resets your cost basis upward at no tax cost. Do it in early March, not on 31 March, and net of transaction costs. Verify the current limit.
Why did my broker's tax statement and the AIS show completely different numbers?
Almost certainly because the AIS reports gross sale consideration while your broker reports realised gain. ₹50,00,000 of sales producing ₹2,00,000 of gain shows as ₹50,00,000 in the AIS. This is expected and is not a discrepancy. Genuine discrepancies usually come from duplicate reporting (the same transaction reported by both the exchange and the depository), an old account wrongly linked to your PAN, or dividends reported gross versus received net. Use the AIS feedback mechanism for genuine errors and keep your reconciliation working.
I forgot to file last year and I had a big trading loss. Can I still carry it forward?
If the return was filed after the s.139(1) due date, no — business losses and capital losses cannot be carried forward from a belated return, under s.139(3) read with s.80. The loss is forfeited. There are limited condonation provisions in specific circumstances under s.119(2)(b), but they are discretionary and not a routine remedy. Take professional advice immediately rather than assuming nothing can be done, but set expectations accordingly. The lesson for every future year: file by the due date even when you owe nothing.
My employer gave me RSUs in a US-listed parent. What do I need to do?
Several things, and this is an area where a chartered accountant is not optional. In outline: the perquisite value at vesting is taxed as salary and your employer generally withholds on it; the capital gain on eventual sale is computed from the vest-date fair market value as cost, in rupees at the prescribed conversion rate, and is taxed as a foreign share — not under s.111A/112A, with a 24-month long-term threshold; any dividends are taxable in India with foreign tax credit available subject to Form 67; and the holding must be disclosed in Schedule FA every year while you hold it, regardless of value. The Schedule FA point is the one with disproportionate consequences if missed. Verify all of this against the current law with a professional.
Is switching from a regular plan to a direct plan worth the tax?
Compute it rather than assuming. A switch is a redemption plus a purchase, so it realises the embedded gain and restarts the holding period. The saving is the expense ratio difference — often 0.8–1.2 percentage points a year — applied to the whole balance going forward. On a holding with a small embedded gain the switch pays back in months; on one with a large long-held gain it can take several years. The usual sensible approach is to stop new contributions to the regular plan immediately (free) and switch the existing balance in tranches across financial years, using the s.112A exemption each year.
Can I use my spouse's ₹1.25 lakh exemption by gifting them shares?
Generally not for this purpose. Under s.64, income arising from assets transferred to a spouse without adequate consideration is clubbed with the transferor's income. Gifting securities to a spouse to realise gains in their hands therefore does not usually achieve the intended result. A spouse with their own independent income investing their own money has their own exemption, which is an entirely different thing. The clubbing provisions have exceptions and nuances — take professional advice rather than relying on a general statement.
What is "turnover" for an F&O trader and will I need a tax audit?
Turnover for derivatives is not the notional contract value. It is computed per the ICAI Guidance Note on tax audit — broadly the absolute sum of settlement profits and losses, with the treatment of premium received on sale of options depending on which edition of the Guidance Note is followed. That specific point was revised and is the main source of disagreement. On audit: the s.44AB thresholds (₹1 crore, extended to ₹10 crore where cash receipts and payments are each within 5%) interact with s.44AD in ways that can require an audit even for a loss-making trader. This is genuinely complicated. Engage a chartered accountant.
I invested in US stocks through an Indian platform. Do I need Schedule FA?
It depends on the structure, and you must determine which one you used. If you invested through an Indian mutual fund or fund-of-fund that invests overseas, you own units of an Indian scheme — you hold no foreign asset, and Schedule FA is not triggered by that holding. If you remitted under LRS to a foreign brokerage account and hold the shares abroad, you hold a foreign asset and a foreign custodial account, and Schedule FA applies regardless of value, every year, including years with no income. Many investors are unclear which they did. Check, and if in doubt have a chartered accountant check. The consequence of getting this wrong is disproportionate.
Why did my crypto loss not reduce my tax at all?
Because s.115BBH permits no set-off of VDA losses — not against other income, not against capital gains, and not even against other VDA gains. There is also no carry-forward. A year in which you gained on one coin and lost more on another produces tax on the gain and no relief for the loss. In addition, the only deduction permitted is the cost of acquisition — exchange fees and other costs are not deductible. Verify the current provisions, but this has been the position since the regime was introduced.
I got a notice saying my income does not match the AIS. What now?
Read it carefully to identify what kind of communication it is — an intimation under s.143(1) with an adjustment, a s.139(9) defective return notice, a s.142(1) enquiry and a s.148 reassessment notice are very different things with different deadlines and different responses. Then reconcile the specific line. The most common genuine causes are forgotten interest income, dividends reported gross versus recorded net, and transactions wrongly attributed to your PAN. Respond within the stated time through the e-filing portal. For anything beyond a simple s.143(1) adjustment, engage a chartered accountant — a poorly worded response can create problems that a well-worded one would not.
Should I choose the old or the new tax regime?
It is a computation, not a rule. The new regime under s.115BAC is the default, has wider slabs and lower rates, but most Chapter VI-A deductions are unavailable under it — including the common ones for specified investments, insurance premia, interest on certain loans, and donations, with a small number of exceptions. The old regime has narrower slabs and higher rates but permits those deductions. Which is better depends entirely on how much deduction you actually claim. The e-filing portal provides a comparison utility; run both. Note also that the choice interacts with business income — the ability to switch regimes year to year is restricted for taxpayers with business income. Verify the current slabs, rebate, standard deduction and switching rules.
What records should I keep, and for how long?
Keep contract notes, broker annual P&L and tax statements, registrar capital gains statements, demat and mutual fund account statements, bank statements, TDS certificates, advance tax challans, filed returns with acknowledgements, computation workings, corporate action documents, and — for anything foreign — broker tax statements, Form 67 and remittance documents. On retention: keep records relating to a capital asset for as long as you hold it plus the period during which the relevant assessment year can be reopened, which for serious cases can be many years. The practical answer for an individual investor is indefinitely, digitally; storage is free and reconstruction is impossible. Verify the current reassessment time limits under s.148/s.149, which were restructured.
