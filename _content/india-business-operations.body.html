Day Two — The Operating System of a Small Indian Company

Incorporation is day one. This guide is about day two onwards: the bank accounts, the invoices that get paid, the books that survive an audit, the payroll that runs on the 30th, the policies that keep you out of a labour tribunal, and the controls that stop ₹8 lakh (₹800,000) leaving on a forged email. It is written for an Indian software engineer running a consulting firm, a product company or a small SaaS business — the person who is simultaneously the CEO, the CFO, the Head of People and the person who fixes the printer.

This is educational material, not legal, tax, accounting or financial adviceIndian company law, tax law, exchange control, labour law and data protection law are statutory, fact-specific and change with every Union Budget, every notification and every state rule. Nothing here is advice on your facts. Before you structure a salary, classify a worker, claim an input tax credit, terminate an employee, sign a lease or process personal data, engage a qualified Chartered Accountant (CA), Company Secretary (CS), labour-law consultant or advocate and have them confirm the current position for your situation. Every rate, threshold, fee, form number and due date in this guide is stated as at FY 2025-26 / early 2026 and must be re-verified against the official portal before you act on it.
Five changes are in flight — verify current status before relying on anything here (1) Income-tax Act, 2025 is scheduled to replace the Income-tax Act, 1961 from 1 April 2026, introducing a unified "tax year" concept. Section references in this guide follow the 1961 Act; map them before relying on them — incometax.gov.in. (2) The four Labour Codes — Code on Wages 2019, Industrial Relations Code 2020, Code on Social Security 2020, Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code 2020 — were brought into force from late 2025, but state rules are still landing. The "wages" definition, gratuity eligibility for fixed-term employees, and leave entitlements all change. Your obligations depend on your state. (3) The DPDP Act 2023 Rules were notified in late 2025 with phased compliance deadlines. If you process any personal data — and you do — confirm which phase binds you at meity.gov.in. (4) GST rate rationalisation of September 2025 moved the structure to principally 5% and 18% slabs with a 40% demerit rate. Confirm the rate for your exact SAC code. (5) Revised MSME investment and turnover limits took effect 1 April 2025, changing who qualifies as micro/small/medium and therefore who benefits from the 45-day payment rule and from MSME credit schemes. Treat all five as "verify current status" items.

The single highest-leverage habitReconcile your bank account every Monday. Not monthly. Every transaction in the bank must map to an entry in the books that same week. It takes fifteen minutes when it is current and three days when it is six months stale — and almost every fraud, duplicate payment and missed receipt is caught in that fifteen minutes.

The four numbers a founder should know on any given day

Cash in the bank across all accounts. Net burn for the last three months. Accounts receivable and how much of it is over 60 days. Committed payroll and statutory dues for the next 30 days. If you cannot answer all four in under two minutes, the back office is not yet installed.

The four documents an enterprise customer will ask for

Your POSH policy and Internal Committee details. Your information security policy and a recent VAPT report. Your professional indemnity and cyber insurance certificates. Your privacy notice, sub-processor list and a signed Data Processing Agreement. Each one is a week of work and each one blocks a deal.

The three accounts that prevent a cash crisis

An operating account, a tax reserve account that GST and TDS are swept into the day money lands, and a payroll account funded on the 25th. The money you collected as GST and deducted as TDS was never yours; keeping it in a separate account is the cheapest discipline available.

The three filings that cost the most when missed

TDS deposit — interest plus 30% expense disallowance under Section 40(a)(ia). The income tax return within the Section 139(1) due date — miss it and you forfeit loss carry-forward. MCA annual filings — ₹100 per day per form with no cap, and director disqualification under Section 164(2).

Banking

Your bank is infrastructure. The wrong choice costs you in minimum balance charges, failed international wires, a relationship manager who changes every eight months, and a net banking portal that cannot do a bulk payment. The right choice is boring and invisible. Pick for operational capability, not for the opening offer.

Authorised signatories and payment controls

  1. Pass a proper board resolutionName the signatories, state the mode of operation and the monetary limits. Give the bank a certified copy. Update it whenever a director changes — and remove departed signatories the same week.
  2. Use maker-checker for every paymentOne person creates the payment, a different person approves it. Every serious bank's net banking supports this. In a two-person company, the two founders are maker and checker. This single control stops most internal fraud and all accidental double payments.
  3. Set a joint-approval thresholdBelow ₹50,000: single approval. ₹50,000–₹5,00,000: two approvals. Above ₹5,00,000: two approvals plus a verbal confirmation on a known phone number.
  4. Lock down beneficiary additionNew beneficiaries should require two approvals and a cooling-off period. Most invoice-interception fraud works by adding a new beneficiary with a slightly different name.
  5. Separate the person who reconciles from the person who paysEven in a ten-person company. If the same person raises the payment and reconciles the bank, no control exists.
  6. Review access quarterlyWho has net banking access, at what level, with which device bound? Remove ex-employees the day they leave, not the quarter they leave.

Bank reconciliation discipline

WEEKLY BANK RECONCILIATION - THE 15-MINUTE ROUTINE

 1. Download the bank statement for the week (CSV or Excel).
 2. Import into Zoho Books / Tally / your tool.
 3. For every bank line, confirm it matches a book entry:
       Receipt  -> matched to an invoice (full or part)
       Payment  -> matched to a bill or an expense claim
       Transfer -> matched to the other account
       Charge   -> booked to Bank Charges with GST noted
 4. INVESTIGATE, DO NOT IGNORE:
       [ ] Any receipt you cannot attribute to a customer
       [ ] Any payment you did not expect
       [ ] Any charge you do not recognise
       [ ] Any reversal or failed transaction
       [ ] Any round-figure payment to an unfamiliar beneficiary
 5. Book the entries. Clear the "unreconciled" list to ZERO.
 6. Record the closing balance in the weekly cash sheet.

MONTH-END ADDITIONALLY:
  [ ] Reconcile the book balance to the bank's closing balance
      and document every reconciling item (cheques issued but
      not presented, deposits in transit)
  [ ] Confirm every account - operating, tax, payroll, FD, EEFC
  [ ] Save the signed/initialled reconciliation as a PDF in the
      month's folder. An auditor will ask for 12 of these.

RED FLAGS THAT MEAN STOP AND INVESTIGATE TODAY
  - A payment to a beneficiary added in the last 30 days
  - A vendor's bank account that changed recently
  - Two payments of the same amount to the same vendor
  - A salary credit to an account that is not in your payroll file
  - Any transaction initiated outside working hours

SaaS company, Rs 2,40,00,000 annual collections through a gateway.
Mix: 55% UPI, 30% cards, 15% netbanking.

Assume (ILLUSTRATIVE - get your own quoted rates):
   UPI         0.00% + GST          (prevailing regime)
   Cards       2.00% + 18% GST  =   2.36% effective
   Netbanking  1.80% + 18% GST  =   2.12% effective

   UPI         1,32,00,000  x 0.00%  =  Rs        0
   Cards         72,00,000  x 2.36%  =  Rs 1,69,920
   Netbanking    36,00,000  x 2.12%  =  Rs   76,320
   ------------------------------------------------
   ANNUAL GATEWAY COST                  Rs 2,46,240

  1. SHIFT THE MIX. Every Rs 10 lakh moved from cards to UPI
     saves Rs 23,600. Offer a 1% discount for UPI payment -
     it costs Rs 10,000 and saves Rs 23,600. Net saving
     Rs 13,600 per Rs 10 lakh shifted.

     card MDR of 1.6-1.8% is achievable. On Rs 72 lakh of card
     volume, 2.00% -> 1.70% saves Rs 21,600 a year.
  3. CLAIM THE INPUT TAX CREDIT. The GST on the MDR is an
     input tax credit if you are registered. On Rs 2,46,240 of
     cost, roughly Rs 37,600 is GST and is recoverable. MANY
     SMALL COMPANIES NEVER CLAIM IT because the gateway
     invoice is never entered in the books.

CHARGEBACKS
   A chargeback is a card-network-initiated reversal. The gateway
   debits you, you contest with evidence (delivery proof, IP log,
   signed acceptance, communication trail). Budget 0.1-0.5% of
   card volume. Keep: timestamped delivery logs, the customer's

WHAT YOUR BANK WILL ASK, AND WHAT YOU MUST KEEP

  and other business/professional services. YOUR BANK WILL ASK
  YOU TO DECLARE IT, usually by email or in an inward remittance

  capital receipt or a personal transfer:
    - is not evidence of an export of services
    - breaks your GST zero-rating / refund claim
    - creates a FEMA question later
  VERIFY the correct purpose code for your exact service with
  your AD bank and against the RBI purpose code list.

FIRC / FIRA

  AD Category-I bank. It evidences that foreign currency was
  received, from whom, in what amount, and under what purpose
  code. You need it for:
    - GST zero-rated supply / refund claims
    - Income tax substantiation of export income
    - Any future FEMA or transfer pricing question
  Many banks now issue an e-FIRC/FIRA automatically; some charge

  Generated on the DGFT portal (dgft.gov.in) from bank-reported
  realisation data. Required for most export incentive schemes
  and sometimes requested in GST refund processing.
  Check that your bank is actually transmitting data to DGFT -
  many do not do it automatically for services exporters.

  Software export declarations under the FEMA export regulations.
  Applicability depends on the nature of the export and on whether
  you operate from an STPI/SEZ unit. VERIFY with your AD bank and

  period prescribed under FEMA (commonly 9 months from the date
  of export for most exporters - VERIFY the current period and

What actually makes customers payIn order of effectiveness: (1) a named individual on your side owning the relationship with a named individual in their AP team; (2) suspension of service, which is the only consequence most customers genuinely care about; (3) escalation to the business sponsor, who has a budget problem if you stop; (4) the MSME 45-day rule, which creates a tax problem for their finance team; (5) interest, which rarely gets paid but moves you up the queue; (6) legal notices, which are slow and relationship-ending. MSMED ACT, 2006
  Sec. 15  A buyer must pay a micro or small enterprise supplier
           within the period agreed in writing, and in any case
           NOT LATER THAN 45 DAYS from the day of acceptance or
           deemed acceptance.
  Sec. 16  If the buyer fails, it is liable to pay COMPOUND

  Sec. 18  The supplier may refer the dispute to the Micro and

  Sec. 19  A buyer appealing an MSEFC award must DEPOSIT 75% of

SECTION 43B(h), INCOME-TAX ACT 1961
  An expenditure payable to a MICRO or SMALL enterprise is
  deductible only in the year in which it is ACTUALLY PAID, if
  payment is made beyond the time limit specified in Sec. 15 of
  the MSMED Act. In other words:

  *** IF YOUR CUSTOMER DOES NOT PAY YOU WITHIN 45 DAYS, THEY
      CANNOT CLAIM YOUR INVOICE AS AN EXPENSE IN THAT YEAR. ***

  This hits their taxable profit directly. A Rs 8,26,000 invoice
  disallowed at a 25.17% effective rate costs them Rs 2,07,900
  of extra tax in that year. It is often cheaper for them to pay
  you than to delay.

HOW TO USE IT PROPERLY

     at udyamregistration.gov.in, and needs only PAN and Aadhaar.
     Without it you are not a "micro or small enterprise" for
     these purposes.
  2. PUT YOUR UDYAM NUMBER ON EVERY INVOICE, in the header, next
     to your GSTIN.
  3. Add a line to the invoice:
     "Acme Engineering Labs Private Limited is a registered Micro
      Enterprise under the MSMED Act, 2006 (Udyam Reg. No.
      UDYAM-KR-xx-xxxxxxx). Payment is due within 45 days of
      acceptance under Section 15 of the said Act."
  4. State it in the MSA too, with a representation that you will
     notify any change in status.
  5. Mention it in Reminder 3, not Reminder 1. It is a lever, not
     an opening gambit.

Verify your MSME classification — limits changed 1 April 2025The revised classification effective 1 April 2025 uses both investment in plant, machinery or equipment and turnover: Micro — investment up to ₹2.5 crore and turnover up to ₹10 crore; Small — investment up to ₹25 crore and turnover up to ₹100 crore; Medium — investment up to ₹125 crore and turnover up to ₹500 crore. Bounced cheques — Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act

  1. The cheque must be dishonouredReturned unpaid for insufficiency of funds or because it exceeds the arranged amount. Keep the bank's return memo — it is the foundation of the case.
  2. Serve a demand notice within 30 daysFrom the date you receive information of dishonour. The notice must demand payment of the cheque amount. Send by registered post with acknowledgement due and by email, to the correct registered address.
  3. Wait 15 daysThe drawer has 15 days from receipt of the notice to pay.
  4. File the complaint within 30 daysOf the expiry of that 15-day period, before the Magistrate having jurisdiction. Delay beyond this is generally fatal unless condoned.
  5. OutcomePunishable with imprisonment up to two years, or a fine up to twice the cheque amount, or both. In practice, most matters settle once the complaint is filed, because a criminal summons is taken seriously.
  6. Interim compensationCourts may direct interim compensation of up to 20% of the cheque amount during the trial under Section 143A, and up to 20% as a deposit on appeal under Section 148. Verify the current provisions.
Why a post-dated cheque is still worth asking forA plain unpaid invoice is a civil debt with a slow remedy. A dishonoured cheque is a criminal complaint under Section 138 with a defined and relatively fast process. For a customer with a history of delay, ask for a post-dated cheque or a security cheque at contract signing. Many will give one; the ones who refuse have told you something useful. Confirm the current procedure and limitation periods with an advocate — Section 138 practice is technical and strict on timelines.

Burn, runway and the working capital cycle

BURN AND RUNWAY

  GROSS BURN  = total cash operating outflows in a month
  NET BURN    = gross burn - cash receipts
  RUNWAY      = cash balance / average net burn over last 3 months

  Example (product company, pre-profit):
     Cash balance                      Rs 2,40,00,000
     Monthly gross burn                Rs   42,00,000
     Monthly cash receipts             Rs   19,00,000
     NET BURN                          Rs   23,00,000
     RUNWAY = 240 / 23                 =    10.4 months

  *** RULES ***
  - Compute runway on NET burn, using the trailing 3-month
    average, not on your best month.
  - Below 12 months: start raising, or start cutting.
  - Below 6 months: you have lost negotiating leverage. Cut now.
  - Model runway in the DOWNSIDE case too: what if the two
    largest customers churn?


THE WORKING CAPITAL CYCLE FOR A SERVICES BUSINESS

   Day 0    Engineer starts work on a project
   Day 30   Month ends; you accrue the revenue
   Day 33   You raise the invoice
   Day 63   Invoice due (Net 30)
   Day 78   Customer actually pays (DSO 62 from invoice date... )

   MEANWHILE:
   Day 30   You paid the engineer's salary for the month
   Day 37   You deposited the TDS on that salary
   Day 45   You paid PF and ESIC
   Day 50   You paid GST on the invoice you raised on day 33,
            even though the customer has not paid you

   *** YOU FINANCE ROUGHLY 78 DAYS OF DELIVERY COST. ***

   CASH REQUIRED = (monthly delivery cost) x (cycle in days / 30)
   For a company with Rs 9,00,000 of monthly delivery cost:
      9,00,000 x (78 / 30) = Rs 23,40,000 of permanent working
      capital just to stand still.

   GROWTH MAKES THIS WORSE. Doubling revenue doubles the working
   capital requirement BEFORE any of the new revenue arrives.
   This is why profitable services companies run out of cash
   while growing - and why a cash credit / overdraft facility
   is worth arranging when you do NOT need it.

   THE FOUR LEVERS, IN ORDER OF IMPACT
     1. ADVANCE BILLING. Moving from Net 30 arrears to 50%
        advance cuts the cycle by ~35 days.
     2. INVOICE ON DAY 1, NOT DAY 33. Weekly or fortnightly
        invoicing on T&M engagements cuts 15 days.
     3. COLLECT FASTER. Every 10 days of DSO reduction releases
        (annual revenue / 36.5) of cash.
     4. STRETCH PAYABLES - but NOT with micro/small vendors
        (Sec. 15 MSMED Act / Sec. 43B(h) cut both ways), and
        never with statutory dues.

The founder's cash discipline rules

  • Sweep GST collected and TDS deducted into the tax reserve account the day the money lands. Treat it as not yours, because it is not.
  • Fund the payroll account on the 25th for the whole month's payroll plus employer statutory contributions. Never let payroll compete with a vendor payment.
  • Keep a minimum cash floor equal to two months of fixed costs and treat breaching it as an emergency, not a nuisance.
  • Arrange an overdraft or cash credit facility when you do not need it. A bank will give ₹25–50 lakh to a profitable company with two years of filings and almost nothing to a company in distress.
  • Never take a loan to pay statutory dues. If you are there, the problem is upstream.
  • Review the 13-week forecast every Monday with whoever else signs payments. Twenty minutes.
  • Re-forecast the full year after any quarter that misses by more than 10%. A plan nobody believes is worse than no plan.
  • Keep three months of personal expenses outside the business. A founder who needs this month's salary makes bad decisions about this month's business.

Budgeting and Financial Planning

A budget is not a forecast. A forecast is what you think will happen; a budget is what you have authorised people to spend. Small Indian companies usually have neither, which is why the first time anyone notices that software subscriptions grew 140% is in the audit.

The annual operating plan

  1. Start in January for an April financial yearThree months is not too early. You need time to argue.
  2. Build revenue bottom-upNamed accounts, expected contract values, probability-weighted, plus a new-business number derived from pipeline and conversion rates — not a growth percentage applied to last year.
  3. Build headcount by role and monthEach role: title, function, start month, CTC band, and the specific outcome it is hired for. Apply a 1.10–1.15 loading factor for employer PF, gratuity, insurance and equipment.
  4. Build fixed costs from contractsRent, insurance, audit, subscriptions, cloud — from actual agreements, with the renewal date and the escalation clause noted.
  5. Build variable costs as a driverCloud as cost per customer, payment gateway as a percentage of collections, travel as cost per sales meeting.
  6. Lay in the statutory calendarAdvance tax in four instalments, annual audit fees, insurance renewals, GST refunds. These are cash events and they are predictable.
  7. Produce three scenariosBase, upside (+25% revenue) and downside (−30% revenue). For the downside, pre-decide the cuts: which hires are deferred, which spend is stopped, at what trigger.
  8. Set the triggers in advance"If Q2 revenue is below ₹X, we defer the two Q3 engineering hires and halve the marketing budget." Writing this down in March is a calm decision; making it in October is a panic.
  9. Lock it, then re-forecast quarterlyThe budget does not change. The forecast does. Keep both and always report actual against budget, with the forecast shown separately.

Compensation Structuring

Indian salary structuring is a legitimate exercise in allocating a fixed cost to the employee's benefit, within the law. It is also an area where the Labour Codes have changed the rules: the new definition of "wages" effectively forces basic pay to be at least 50% of total remuneration, which increases PF, gratuity and leave-encashment costs for companies that used to keep basic low.

The "wages" definition under the Code on Wages, 2019 — verify current state rulesThe Codes define wages as basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance, and provide that where the excluded components (HRA, conveyance, overtime, commission, bonus and so on) exceed 50% of total remuneration, the excess is deemed to be wages. The practical effect is that basic must be at least 50% of total remuneration for computing PF, gratuity, bonus and leave encashment. The Codes were brought into force from late 2025, but the operative rules are state-specific and are still being notified. GRATUITY COMPUTATION - WORKED

FORMULA (Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 - for establishments
covered by the Act)
   Gratuity = (Last drawn basic + DA) x 15/26 x completed years
   Service beyond 6 months in the final year counts as a full
   year; 6 months or less is ignored.

   Date of joining          1 July 2019
   Date of leaving         15 March 2026
   Service                  6 years 8 months -> 7 years
   Last drawn basic        Rs 1,10,000/month

            = 1,10,000 x 0.576923 x 7
            = Rs 4,44,231

   for employees covered by the Act. VERIFY the current ceiling

Performance management and lawful termination

  1. Set written goalsAt the start of the cycle, documented and acknowledged. Without them, "poor performance" is an opinion.
  2. Give contemporaneous feedbackIn writing, in the one-on-one notes. A termination defended on three years of unwritten dissatisfaction fails.
  3. Run a genuine Performance Improvement PlanSpecific, measurable objectives, a realistic period (30–90 days), scheduled check-ins, and support. A PIP designed to fail is evidence against you.
  4. Document everythingEach check-in: what was discussed, what was agreed, what improved and what did not, signed or at least emailed to the employee.
  5. For misconduct, follow natural justiceA written show-cause notice setting out specific allegations; a reasonable opportunity to respond; a domestic inquiry with an inquiry officer where the allegation is serious; the right to be heard; a reasoned decision. Shortcut any of these and an industrial tribunal will likely set the termination aside.
  6. Serve proper noticePer the appointment letter and the applicable state Shops & Establishments Act and the Industrial Relations Code, 2020. Pay in lieu if you want an immediate exit.
  7. Settle F&F within the statutory periodThe Code on Wages, 2019 provides for payment of wages on termination within two working days — verify your state's operative rule.
  8. Issue a relieving letterWithholding it is a common and poor practice that frequently ends in litigation and always ends in reputational damage.
"Workman" status matters more than you thinkThe Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 — now largely subsumed into the Industrial Relations Code, 2020 — gives significant protection to a "workman", including requirements for notice, retrenchment compensation, and in larger establishments prior government permission for retrenchment. Whether a software engineer is a "workman" is fact-specific and has been litigated repeatedly in India; designation alone does not decide it, and engineers performing technical rather than supervisory or managerial work have in several cases been held to be workmen. Before terminating anyone in a contentious situation, take specific legal advice. The cost of advice is a fraction of the cost of a reinstatement order with back wages.

Your ITC depends on your vendor's filings

THE GSTR-2B RECONCILIATION - DO THIS EVERY MONTH

WHY: Under Sec. 16(2)(aa) of the CGST Act, input tax credit is
available only if the invoice has been furnished by the SUPPLIER
in their GSTR-1 and appears in YOUR GSTR-2B. Your vendor's
non-filing becomes YOUR cash cost.

MONTHLY PROCESS
  1. Download GSTR-2B from gst.gov.in (auto-generated, usually
     available after the 14th)
  2. Export your purchase register from your accounting system
  3. Match on: GSTIN + invoice number + invoice date + taxable
     value + tax amount
  4. Categorise every difference:

     CATEGORY A - In books, NOT in 2B
        Cause: vendor has not filed GSTR-1, or filed with the
               wrong GSTIN, or reported it as B2C
        ACTION: email the vendor the SAME DAY with the invoice
               details. Withhold the GST portion of the payment
               if the invoice is not yet paid. DO NOT CLAIM
               the ITC in 3B this month.

     CATEGORY B - In 2B, NOT in books
        Cause: you have not received or recorded the invoice
        ACTION: get the invoice and book it. This is FREE MONEY
               you are leaving on the table - small companies
               routinely miss Rs 50,000-2,00,000 a year here.

     CATEGORY C - Value or tax mismatch
        ACTION: identify whether it is a rounding difference,
               a credit note, or an error. Correct one side.

     CATEGORY D - Blocked credit under Sec. 17(5)
        Motor vehicles (with exceptions), food and beverages,
        outdoor catering, health services, club memberships,
        rent-a-cab, works contract for immovable property,
        goods lost/stolen/written off, personal consumption.
        ACTION: do NOT claim. Book the GST as a cost.
        *** VERIFY the current Sec. 17(5) list - it has been
            amended. Note that food/beverage and health
            services became allowable where provision is
            OBLIGATORY under a law - and group health insurance
            for employees may qualify in some circumstances.
            Take advice. ***

  5. Claim only what is in 2B and not blocked
  6. Maintain the reconciliation as a document - it is the
     first thing asked for in a GST audit or notice

Lease clauses to negotiate

  1. Lock-in periodMarket is 12–36 months. You are liable for the full lock-in rent even if you leave. Negotiate the shortest lock-in and a right to assign or sublet to a reputable assignee with consent not unreasonably withheld.
  2. Security depositOften 6–10 months in Indian commercial leases. Negotiate down to 3–6 and insist on interest-free refundable within 30 days of handover, with a right to adjust the last months' rent against the deposit if it is not refunded.
  3. EscalationTypically 5% annually or 15% every three years. Push for 5% every two years, or a cap.
  4. Rent-free fit-out periodAsk for 45–90 days of rent-free time to complete fit-out. On a ₹5 lakh monthly rent, 60 days is ₹10 lakh.
  5. Maintenance charges (CAM)Get the rate per sq ft fixed with a cap on annual increase, and a list of what it includes. Open-ended CAM is a recurring surprise.
  6. What is chargeable areaIndian leases charge on super built-up area with a loading of 25–45% over carpet area. Confirm the carpet area and the loading factor in writing; the effective rate per usable square foot is what matters.
  7. Exit and noticeA right to terminate after the lock-in on 3–6 months' notice. Without it you are committed for the full term.
  8. Fit-out restorationLandlords often require restoration to bare shell at exit, which can cost ₹300–₹800 per sq ft. Negotiate "hand over in as-is condition, normal wear and tear excepted" or agree a fixed restoration sum upfront.
  9. Power backup, parking, air-conditioning hoursSpecify the DG backup capacity, the number of parking slots, and the HVAC operating hours — extended hours are usually chargeable.
  10. Registration and stamp dutyA lease over 11 months generally requires registration under the Registration Act, 1908. Stamp duty and registration charges are state-specific and significant; agree who pays, in writing.
  11. Force majeure and interruptionPost-2020, negotiate rent abatement if the premises are unusable for reasons beyond your control.

Data Protection and the DPDP Act, 2023

India now has a comprehensive personal data protection law. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 was passed in August 2023, the Rules were notified in late 2025 with phased compliance deadlines, and it applies to any company processing digital personal data in India — including a ten-person startup processing employee and customer data.

Verify the Rules, the phase-in timeline and every obligation at meity.gov.inThe DPDP Rules were notified in late 2025 with phased commencement: some provisions on notification, others after defined periods. The Data Protection Board of India is being constituted. Penalty amounts, the Significant Data Fiduciary criteria, the consent manager framework and the cross-border transfer restrictions are all set by the Act and the Rules and may be further specified by notification. Confirm the current status and your applicable deadline before building your compliance programme. This section describes the framework, not a legal opinion.

What the Act actually requires

  1. NoticeBefore or at the time of seeking consent, give a clear notice in plain language stating the personal data to be processed, the purpose, how to exercise rights, and how to complain to the Data Protection Board. The notice must be available in English and in the languages specified in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution — verify the current requirement.
  2. ConsentMust be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, with a clear affirmative action, and limited to the data necessary for the specified purpose. Pre-ticked boxes and bundled consent do not work. Consent must be as easy to withdraw as to give.
  3. Legitimate usesCertain processing is permitted without consent, including where the data principal voluntarily provides data for a specified purpose, for employment purposes, for compliance with law, for medical emergencies and for specified State functions. The employment-related legitimate use is the provision most relevant to a small company's HR data. Verify its exact scope.
  4. Purpose limitation and data minimisationProcess only for the stated purpose, only the data necessary, and erase it when the purpose is served or consent is withdrawn, unless retention is required by law.
  5. Accuracy and securityEnsure completeness and accuracy where the data is used to make a decision affecting the principal or is disclosed to another fiduciary. Implement reasonable security safeguards.
  6. Data principal rightsAccess to a summary of personal data processed and processing activities; correction, completion, updating and erasure; grievance redressal through the fiduciary before approaching the Board; and nomination of a person to exercise rights in the event of death or incapacity.
  7. Breach notificationNotify both the Data Protection Board and each affected data principal of a personal data breach, in the form and manner and within the timeline prescribed by the Rules. Verify the current timeline.
  8. Children's dataFor a data principal below 18 years, obtain verifiable consent of the parent or lawful guardian, and do not undertake tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted advertising directed at children. Certain classes of fiduciaries and purposes may be exempted by notification.
  9. Significant Data Fiduciary obligationsIf notified as one: appoint a Data Protection Officer based in India who reports to the board or governing body, appoint an independent data auditor, and conduct periodic Data Protection Impact Assessments and audits.
  10. Cross-border transferTransfer outside India is permitted except to countries restricted by the Central Government by notification. Sectoral laws imposing stricter localisation (for example RBI's payment data storage direction) continue to apply.
  11. Grievance redressalPublish the contact details of the Data Protection Officer or a designated person who can answer questions about processing.
PenaltiesThe Act provides for significant financial penalties imposed by the Data Protection Board, including for failure to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent a breach and for failure to notify a breach. The maximum penalties specified in the Schedule to the Act run to very large amounts relative to a small company's balance sheet. Verify the current Schedule and the Board's constitution and procedure at meity.gov.in. There is also a penalty for a data principal who files a false or frivolous complaint.

In a ten-person company, one person often raises the invoice, approves the payment, releases it and reconciles the bank. That is not a control environment; it is a trust arrangement. Frequently Asked Questions

I am a two-person company. Do I really need a separate current account, or can I keep using my savings account?
You need a current account, and the reasons are practical rather than theoretical. First, a private limited company is a separate legal person — it cannot bank in your personal name at all, and mixing the two creates a director's loan, potential Section 2(22)(e) deemed dividend exposure where the company has accumulated profits, and a governance finding in every audit. Second, banks' terms for savings accounts restrict business transactions, and high-volume business credits into a savings account get flagged and sometimes frozen. Third, you cannot receive foreign remittances cleanly without a current account and an AD Category-I relationship that will issue FIRCs. Fourth, payment gateways will not onboard you. If you are a sole proprietor rather than a company, a current account in the trade name is still the right answer, and you will need the GST certificate or Shops & Establishments registration to open it.
My biggest client always pays at 75–90 days against Net 30 terms. What can I actually do?
Work the levers in order. (1) Price it in. If they are costing you 50 extra days of working capital, a 4–6% price premium on renewal is rational and defensible — say so openly. (2) Change the billing rhythm. Move from monthly-in-arrears to fortnightly invoicing, or to a monthly retainer billed in advance. This shifts the whole cycle forward by 20–30 days without changing their payment behaviour at all. (3) Get Udyam registered and put your registration number and the MSMED Act Section 15 language on every invoice. Their finance team cares, because Section 43B(h) means they cannot deduct your invoice as an expense in the year if they pay beyond 45 days. (4) Find the actual bottleneck — it is usually a specific approval step or a vendor-portal upload that nobody told you about. Ask your sponsor to walk you through their AP process. (5) Put a suspension clause in the next contract. It is the only clause that reliably changes behaviour. Escalate to MSME Samadhaan only if the relationship is already lost.
Can I hire someone as a "consultant" to avoid PF and ESIC?
You can hire a genuine consultant, and you cannot relabel an employee. Indian authorities apply a substance test: control over how the work is done, fixed hours, your equipment, exclusivity, integration into the team, a fixed monthly payment regardless of output, and benefits like leave all point to employment regardless of what the contract says. If an EPFO inspection, an ESIC inspection, an income tax assessment or a labour tribunal concludes that your "consultants" are employees, the liability is retrospective: both halves of the PF contribution with interest under Section 7Q and damages under Section 14B, ESIC contributions with interest, gratuity where five years of continuous service were completed, the gap between Section 194J withholding at 10% and Section 192 at slab rates, and leave and notice entitlements. On a ₹18 lakh-a-year person over three years, that is realistically ₹8–15 lakh. Genuine consultants work from their own premises, set their own hours, have other clients, invoice against deliverables, and can send a substitute. If that does not describe the person, hire them properly and build the cost into the price you charge your customers.
How much cash should a small services company keep in the bank?
A minimum of two months of total fixed costs — payroll, employer statutory contributions, rent, insurance, subscriptions, professional fees — as an absolute floor, and three to four months as a target. Add to that an explicit provision for the next quarter's statutory payments, which is why a separate tax reserve account matters. Then test the number against your actual working capital cycle: if you finance 78 days of delivery cost before collecting, you need roughly 2.6 months of delivery cost permanently tied up before you have any buffer at all. Two practical additions: arrange an overdraft or cash credit facility while you are profitable and do not need it, because it costs almost nothing to hold and is unobtainable in distress; and keep any surplus above four months in a sweep-in fixed deposit so it earns something while staying liquid.
Our GSTR-2B does not match our purchase register and there is a ₹2.4 lakh gap. What do I do?
Split the gap into four buckets and treat each differently. In your books but not in 2B: the vendor has not filed GSTR-1, filed against the wrong GSTIN, or reported it as B2C. Email them the same day with the invoice details, and do not claim that credit in GSTR-3B — Section 16(2)(aa) makes credit conditional on it appearing in 2B. If the invoice is unpaid, withhold the GST portion until it appears. In 2B but not in your books: you have not recorded an invoice. Get it and book it; this bucket is usually free money small companies leave behind. Value mismatches: usually a credit note, a rounding difference, or a data-entry error — identify and correct one side. Blocked credits under Section 17(5): never claimable; book the GST as a cost. Then build the discipline: reconcile every month after the 14th, keep the reconciliation file as a document because it is the first thing a GST notice asks for, and add a clause to your vendor terms allowing you to withhold the GST component until the credit reflects. Verify the current Section 17(5) list and Section 16 conditions at gst.gov.in — both have been amended.
What is the minimum set of HR policies a ten-person company must actually have?
Five, and they are all enforceable obligations rather than nice-to-haves. (1) A POSH policy with a constituted Internal Committee, mandatory at ten or more employees, including an external member, with the IC details displayed and the annual report filed with the District Officer. (2) An appointment letter template containing confidentiality, IP assignment and non-solicitation — without this, your company may not own code written by your own employees. (3) A leave policy that meets or exceeds your state's Shops and Establishments Act entitlements. (4) An information security and acceptable use policy, because every enterprise customer's vendor onboarding asks for it. (5) A privacy notice and data protection policy under the DPDP Act, covering both employee and customer data. Everything else — performance management, travel, remote work — can start as a page in a handbook and grow. Note that the four Labour Codes brought into force in late 2025 change several underlying entitlements and the state rules are still landing; have a labour-law consultant review your policy pack against your state's current rules.
Group health insurance is quoting ₹9,000 per employee. Is that reasonable, and what should I check?
₹9,000 per employee for a ₹5 lakh family floater is in the normal range for a small Indian company in early 2026, but the premium tells you very little — the wording decides whether claims actually pay. Check six things. (1) Are all waiting periods waived, including the 30-day initial, the specified-ailment waiting and the pre-existing disease waiting? If not, the policy is much cheaper and much worse. (2) Is there a room rent cap? A "1% of sum insured per day" cap on ₹5 lakh is ₹5,000 a day, and exceeding it triggers a proportionate deduction on the entire bill — a ₹3 lakh claim can settle at ₹1.8 lakh. (3) Is there a co-pay? (4) Is maternity covered, with what sub-limit, and is the waiting period waived? This is the most frequently used benefit in a young workforce. (5) Who is the TPA and what is their claim turnaround? Ask other founders. (6) What is the monthly cut-off for adding joiners? Go through an IRDAI-registered broker and get three quotes with the wordings side by side; the broker costs you nothing.
I want to give employees a laptop allowance instead of buying laptops. Is that better?
Usually not, for three reasons. Tax: a cash allowance is fully taxable salary in the employee's hands, so a ₹1.5 lakh allowance costs the employee ₹45,000–₹58,000 in tax at higher slabs, while a company-provided laptop used for work is not a taxable perquisite. GST and depreciation: when the company buys, the invoice is in the company's name so you claim the input tax credit (18% on ₹1.5 lakh is ₹27,000) and you depreciate the asset at 40% WDV for tax purposes. With an allowance you get neither. Security and control: you cannot mandate full-disk encryption, MDM enrolment, patching or remote wipe on a device you do not own, and you cannot credibly answer an enterprise customer's security questionnaire. The combined value of the ITC and the employee's tax saving usually exceeds the administrative cost of owning the asset. Buy the laptops, put them in the fixed asset register, and keep a signed asset handover and return checklist.
We received a GST notice. What do I do first?
Do four things in order and do not ignore it. (1) Diarise the deadline immediately. Most GST notices carry a short response window — sometimes 7, 15 or 30 days — and an unanswered notice escalates to a best-judgment assessment under Section 62 or a demand under Sections 73/74, which is far harder and more expensive to unwind. (2) Identify exactly which notice it is and what it is about: ASMT-10 is a scrutiny of returns, DRC-01A is a pre-show-cause intimation, DRC-01 is a show cause notice, REG-17 is a cancellation notice, GSTR-3A is for non-filing. The form number tells you the gravity and the route. (3) Send it to your CA the same day with the underlying data — the relevant returns, the reconciliation, the invoices. (4) Respond on the portal within the deadline, in writing, with documents attached, even if the response is a request for extension. Separately, understand the root cause, because the same notice will recur next year otherwise: the three most common triggers for small companies are a GSTR-1 versus GSTR-3B mismatch, an ITC claim exceeding GSTR-2B, and a GSTR-3B versus e-way bill or e-invoice mismatch.
An employee resigned and is asking for their PF. What is my obligation?
Your obligation is to update their date of exit in the EPFO employer portal, and to do it promptly. Until the exit date is marked, the employee cannot withdraw or transfer their balance, and this is one of the most common reasons ex-employees chase former employers for years. The withdrawal or transfer itself is the employee's own action through the EPFO member portal using their UAN, with KYC (Aadhaar, PAN, bank account) seeded and verified. They can either transfer the balance to the new employer's account — which is the better option for them, since continuity of service matters for pension eligibility — or withdraw, subject to the withdrawal rules and the tax treatment, which depends on the period of continuous service. Also complete: ESIC exit update, deletion from the group insurance policy with the end date communicated to them, Form 16 for the year at the usual time, and the relieving and experience letters. Verify the current EPFO process and any changes to the exit-marking requirement at epfindia.gov.in.
Does the DPDP Act really apply to a ten-person startup?
Yes. The Act applies to the processing of digital personal data within India, and to processing outside India in connection with offering goods or services to data principals in India. A ten-person company processes employee KYC, PAN, Aadhaar, bank and salary data; candidate CVs and background verification reports; customer contact and billing data; and support tickets. That is all personal data and you are the Data Fiduciary for it. Separately, if you build software that handles your customers' end-user data, you are a Data Processor for that and your customer will require a Data Processing Agreement. The practical minimum for a small company: a record of processing activities, a published privacy notice, an employee privacy notice, a data retention schedule, DPAs with every vendor that touches personal data, a process for responding to data principal requests, a breach response plan, and annual training. The Rules were notified in late 2025 with phased deadlines, so confirm which phase applies to you at meity.gov.in. Small companies are not exempt; they are simply less likely to be notified as Significant Data Fiduciaries, which is a separate and heavier tier.
Should I use a neobank like RazorpayX or Open as my main business account?
Use one for workflow, not as your banking relationship. These platforms provide genuinely excellent payouts, approval chains, payroll integration and auto-reconciliation, and they will save you real hours each month. But they are not RBI-licensed banks — they sit on top of a partner bank's current account or a prepaid instrument, and several Indian fintech platforms have had to re-architect or suspend services following RBI directions on prepaid instruments and on specific partner banks. The practical approach: keep your primary current account and your main balance with a scheduled commercial bank where you also have the forex desk relationship, retain and periodically use the underlying net banking credentials, and use the neobank layer for vendor payouts and payroll workflow. Keep enough in the bank account that a disruption at the platform layer is an inconvenience rather than a crisis, and make sure at least two people know how to make a payment directly from the bank's own net banking.
What is the real annual cost of running compliance for a 25-person company?
Budget ₹5–10 lakh a year all in, excluding internal staff. A realistic breakdown for a ₹4 crore revenue, 25-person services company in early 2026: bookkeeping ₹2.4–3.6 lakh; GST returns ₹0.8–1.2 lakh; TDS returns ₹0.3–0.5 lakh; payroll processing ₹0.4–0.7 lakh; statutory audit ₹0.8–1.5 lakh; income tax return and computation plus tax audit if applicable ₹0.6–1.5 lakh; company secretarial retainer covering AOC-4, MGT-7, ADT-1, DIR-3 KYC, DPT-3, MSME-1, board and general meeting documentation ₹0.5–1 lakh; POSH external member and training ₹0.3–0.6 lakh; insurance ₹2–3.5 lakh depending on which policies you carry. On top of that, software: accounting, payroll, expense and document management typically ₹1.5–3 lakh. The number that matters more is founder time — if you are spending more than eight hours a week on this, a ₹6 lakh finance executive is cheaper than your attention.
How do I stop subscription spend from quietly growing 40% a year?
Four controls, in order of effectiveness. (1) One register, one owner. A single sheet listing every subscription with the tool, the internal owner, the cost, the billing cycle, the renewal date, licences purchased and licences actually active. Visibility alone typically surfaces 15–30% of waste. (2) A virtual card per vendor. Cancelling the card cancels the renewal, which removes the "we forgot to cancel and it auto-renewed for a year" problem entirely. (3) A 45-day renewal warning on the calendar, because every SaaS vendor will negotiate before renewal and none will refund after. (4) A quarterly usage review where each owner pulls active-seat data and justifies the licence count. Add two process rules: every new subscription requires approval per the delegation matrix with a named owner, and every employee exit triggers removal from every paid tool as a checklist item. Also register the company's GSTIN with every Indian vendor so you are claiming input tax credit — on ₹10 lakh of annual Indian SaaS spend that is ₹1.8 lakh you are otherwise donating.
We are about to sign our first enterprise customer and they have sent a 140-question security questionnaire. What is the fastest credible path?
Do not start a SOC 2 programme. For a first enterprise deal, the fastest credible path is a documented control set plus one piece of third-party evidence. Within two weeks: write and have the board approve an Information Security Policy, an Access Control Policy, an Incident Response Plan, a Backup and Recovery Procedure, and a Data Retention Schedule. Turn on SSO with mandatory MFA everywhere, enable full-disk encryption on every laptop with evidence, document a quarterly access review and actually run one, and complete a documented restore test from backup. Within four to six weeks: commission a third-party VAPT on your application and API — typically ₹1–4 lakh — and produce the report together with a remediation plan showing the high and critical findings closed. Sign DPAs with your sub-processors and publish the sub-processor list. That package clears the large majority of mid-market and many enterprise questionnaires. Start SOC 2 Type II or ISO 27001 only when a specific deal is explicitly blocked on it, because the first year costs ₹15–40 lakh and 6–12 months for SOC 2, or ₹5–15 lakh for ISO 27001 at small scale.
