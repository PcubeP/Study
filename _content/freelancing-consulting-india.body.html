The Opportunity and the Honest Trade-offs

An Indian software engineer with five to fifteen years of real delivery experience can, today, bill a US or European client at rates that are three to eight times what an Indian employer pays for the same hours. That arbitrage is real, it is legal, and it is the single largest financial lever available to most engineers in this country. It is also the most over-sold idea on the internet. This guide is the operating manual for the unglamorous half: the registrations, the tax computations, the LUT you forgot to renew, the invoice the bank will not release without a purpose code, the client who goes quiet at 60 days, and the rate card that keeps you solvent through a three-month dry spell.

This is educational material, not legal, tax or financial adviceIndian income tax, GST, exchange control (FEMA) and contract law are statutory, fact-specific and change with every Union Budget, notification and circular. Nothing here is advice on your facts. Before you choose an entity, elect presumptive taxation, file an LUT, sign a master services agreement, or decide not to register for GST, engage a qualified Chartered Accountant (CA) and, for contracts, an advocate, and have them confirm the current position for your situation and your state. Every rate, threshold, fee, form number and due date in this guide is stated as at FY 2025-26 / early 2026 and must be re-verified on the official portal before you act.

Why the arbitrage exists, and how long it lasts

A US company hiring a senior engineer in San Francisco carries a fully loaded cost — salary, payroll taxes, health insurance, 401(k) match, equity, office, recruiting amortisation — in the region of US$280,000–380,000 (₹2.46 crore–₹3.34 crore) a year. The same company engaging an independent contractor pays an hourly rate with none of that load and no severance exposure. At US$85/hour (₹7,480), a contractor working 1,500 billable hours costs them US$127,500 (₹1.12 crore) with zero benefits obligation and a 30-day termination clause. For the client that is cheap. For you, ₹1.12 crore of gross receipts is a life-changing number against an Indian cost of living.

The arbitrage is not charity and it is not permanent. It persists because of three things: a genuine shortage of senior engineers in the US and Western Europe, the normalisation of remote contracting after 2020, and the fact that Indian cost of living remains roughly one-fifth to one-eighth of coastal US cost of living. It compresses when: the client discovers an agency that will supply three people for your price; the exchange rate moves against you; or you are selling a commodity skill (basic CRUD web development, generic QA, first-line support) that is being automated or that fifty thousand other people also sell. The defence is specialisation, not effort.

Who should not do this

  • You have less than four years of experience and no identifiable specialism. You will compete on price with people who can survive on less.
  • You have no savings buffer. Six months of household expenses in a liquid fund is the floor, nine is sensible.
  • You are the sole earner with large fixed obligations — a ₹90,000 monthly EMI does not tolerate a 70-day payment delay.
  • You hate selling. Consulting is 30% engineering and 70% finding, scoping, pricing, reassuring and invoicing. If you will not do the 70%, you are looking for a remote job, which is a perfectly good thing to look for instead.
  • Your employment contract has a genuine, enforceable restriction and you have not yet exited. See the next section.
  • You need validation from a brand name. For the first two years nobody will have heard of you.

The three business models, and which one you are actually running

Staff augmentation

You bill hours into a client's team, attend their standups, use their Jira. Easy to sell, easy to start, commoditised, and dangerously close to disguised employment. Rate ceiling is low because the client is comparing you to a salary. Classification risk is highest here.

Project / outcome delivery

You take a defined scope, deliver it, get paid against milestones. Higher rate, higher risk, needs real scoping discipline and a contract with change control. This is where most independent engineers should aim.

Advisory / expertise

You are paid for judgement, not hours — architecture reviews, AI strategy, due diligence, fractional CTO. Highest rate per hour, lowest volume, requires demonstrable authority. Usually arrives after two or three years of the above.

Staff aug→Project delivery→Retainer advisory→Productised service→Small agency or product

Moonlighting and Employment Conflicts

Most Indian consultants start while employed. That is sensible — it de-risks the transition — and it is also where people blow up their careers, their references and occasionally their bank balances. The legal position in India is more nuanced than either "moonlighting is illegal" or "they can't stop me". What actually matters is your contract, your employer's policy, whose time and equipment you used, and whether there is any overlap with the employer's business.

Read your own contract before you read anything elseThere is no single Indian statute that makes moonlighting unlawful for a private-sector software employee. What binds you is (a) your employment contract, (b) your employer's code of conduct and IP policy, (c) your state Shops and Establishments Act (some state Acts contain "double employment" restrictions), and (d) general law on confidentiality, fiduciary duty and IP ownership. The analysis is document-specific. Have an advocate read your actual contract if there is real money at stake.

Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 — the thing everyone half-remembers"Every agreement by which any one is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business of any kind, is to that extent void." The settled Indian position is that this voids post-termination non-competes. It does not void restrictions that operate during the subsistence of employment — a court will treat an exclusivity clause in a live employment contract as a legitimate term of service. So "non-competes are void in India" is true and irrelevant while you are still on payroll.

Where the real exposure sits

In practice, Indian employers rarely sue a departing engineer for moonlighting. Litigation is slow, expensive and reputationally awkward. What they actually do is cheaper and more damaging to you:

  1. Termination for causeImmediate, no notice pay, no relieving letter. The relieving letter is the practical weapon — a large number of Indian employers and visa processes require it, and background verification agencies will flag "terminated for misconduct".
  2. Clawback of variable pay and unvested equityMost ESOP plans contain a "termination for cause" forfeiture trigger that takes vested-but-unexercised options as well.
  3. Negative background verificationA BGV agency calls HR. HR says "not eligible for rehire, separation for policy violation". That follows you for years.
  4. IP claim over what you builtThe genuinely expensive one. If your contract assigns everything created during employment and you built a product on weekends, the employer can assert ownership. This has killed startup fundraises at diligence.
  5. Injunction and damagesRare, but real where you took confidential information, solicited a named client, or used employer source code. The claim is then breach of confidence and copyright infringement, not "moonlighting".
  6. Tax and PF mismatch exposureTwo employers both treating you as the primary employer produces under-deducted TDS and a demand with interest under Sections 234B/234C, plus a visible paper trail in Form 26AS that your employer's payroll team can see if they look.
The two absolute red lines(1) Never use employer time, devices, networks, accounts or code for client work. MDM agents, DLP, SSO logs, VPN logs and GitHub audit logs make this trivially provable, and it converts a contractual dispute into a credible claim of breach of confidence and misuse of company property. (2) Never take on work for your employer's customer, competitor or active prospect. That single fact turns a technical policy breach into a demonstrable conflict of interest with quantifiable loss, which is the fact pattern employers actually litigate.

If you must moonlight, do it cleanly

  • Obtain the contract and the employee handbook and read the exclusivity, COI, IP and systems-use clauses. Save PDF copies now — you lose portal access on your last day.
  • Seek written consent where the contract allows it. A two-line email to your manager and HR, describing the engagement in general terms and confirming no overlap, is worth more than any argument later.
  • Separate everything physically: your own laptop, your own Google/Microsoft account, your own GitHub, your own phone number, your own VPN, your own password manager vault.
  • Work strictly outside contracted hours and outside any on-call window. Keep a simple log.
  • Choose work in a different sector or a different problem space from your employer's business. If you cannot explain in one sentence why there is no conflict, there is one.
  • Do not name your employer in your marketing. "Senior engineer at a large fintech" is fine; a logo on your website is not.
  • Do not reuse your employer's code, designs, architecture documents, prompts, datasets or customer lists. Not even "the general approach we used".
  • Keep the engagement small enough that it does not degrade your day job. The first visible symptom of moonlighting is missed deadlines, not an audit.
  • Get a PAN-linked trail right: your own invoices, your own bank account, declare the income in your ITR. Trying to hide it creates a tax problem on top of an employment problem.
  • Have a clean exit plan with a date. Moonlighting is a bridge, not a destination.

The special case: consulting during notice period or garden leave

You are still an employee during notice period. Exclusivity and IP clauses still bite. Garden leave — where the employer pays you but keeps you away from work — is contractually fine in India and the employment relationship subsists throughout, so the same restrictions apply. The day your employment actually terminates (check the relieving letter date, not your last working day), post-employment restraints collapse to: confidentiality (enforceable), non-solicit (partially), IP already assigned (binding), non-compete (void under Section 27).

My offer letter is silent on outside work. Can I freelance?
Probably, but check the employee handbook and code of conduct — they are almost always incorporated by reference ("the Employee shall comply with Company policies as amended from time to time") and that is where the moonlighting policy usually lives. Also check whether your state's Shops and Establishments Act contains a double-employment restriction. Even with no express clause, a conflict of interest or use of employer resources is still actionable.

Proprietorship: what you actually "register", because there is no registration

A sole proprietorship has no incorporation certificate because it has no separate existence. What you do is accumulate the artefacts that banks, clients and the tax department treat as proof of a business:

  1. PANYour existing individual PAN is the business PAN. No separate PAN exists for a proprietorship.
  2. Business nameYou may trade under any name that does not infringe a trade mark — "Rao Systems", "Nine Yards Consulting". No filing required, but consider a trade mark application (TM-A, ipindia.gov.in) if the name matters.
  3. Current accountBanks will ask for two proofs of business existence. The usual acceptable set: GST registration certificate, Udyam registration certificate, Shops & Establishment registration, professional tax enrolment certificate, or a certificate of practice. Collect at least two.
  4. GST registrationIf required (see the registrations section) or voluntarily.
  5. Udyam registrationFree, instant, Aadhaar-based, on udyamregistration.gov.in. Gives you MSME status, access to the 45-day payment rule under Section 15 of the MSMED Act 2006, and a document banks accept.
  6. Shops & Establishment registrationState law. Required in most states within 30 days of commencing business even from home, though enforcement against home-based solo consultants is light. Karnataka, Maharashtra, Delhi and Telangana all have online portals.
  7. Professional tax enrolmentState-specific; see the registrations section.

Total cost: typically ₹0 to ₹5,000. Total time: a week, mostly spent waiting for the bank.

When the OPC is genuinely the right answer

The One Person Company is widely mis-sold. It makes sense in a narrow band of cases:

  • You are reinvesting profit rather than extracting it — building a product, hiring a team — so the 25.17% corporate rate beats your 30%+surcharge personal rate and the dividend tax is deferred.
  • A client's procurement rules genuinely require an incorporated entity and you have no co-founder to satisfy the Pvt Ltd two-person requirement.
  • You want limited liability and your work carries real third-party risk that professional indemnity insurance will not cover.
  • You intend to convert to a Private Limited later; the conversion path exists and restrictions on conversion were relaxed in 2021.

Note the structural limits: only a natural person who is an Indian citizen may form an OPC; a nominee must be appointed in the memorandum; an OPC cannot be incorporated as or converted into a Section 8 company; it cannot carry out non-banking financial investment activity. Verify current OPC rules on mca.gov.in — residency and turnover-based conversion rules were amended in 2021 and may change again.

You can change your mind, and it is cheaper than you thinkStart as a proprietorship. If you cross ₹75 lakh, or you hire a second person, or a client blocks you at procurement, incorporate then and run both in parallel for a quarter — the proprietorship finishes its contracts, the company signs the new ones. Migration costs you a few weeks of paperwork and about ₹25,000. Starting with a Pvt Ltd "to be safe" costs you lakhs in unnecessary tax every year you stay small.

GST registration — the question that actually confuses everyone

Here is the sequence of provisions that determines whether a pure service exporter must register. Read it in order.

  1. Section 22(1), CGST Act 2017A supplier must register if "aggregate turnover" in a financial year exceeds ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh for special category states). Services do not get the ₹40 lakh goods threshold.
  2. Section 2(6), CGST Act"Aggregate turnover" includes exports and exempt supplies. So your ₹60 lakh of US billings absolutely counts towards the ₹20 lakh threshold. You cannot argue that exports are outside turnover.
  3. Section 7(5), IGST Act 2017Supply of services where the supplier is in India and the place of supply is outside India is treated as an inter-State supply.
  4. Section 24(i), CGST ActPersons making any inter-State taxable supply must register compulsorily, irrespective of turnover. Read literally, this would force every ₹3 lakh freelancer with one foreign client into GST registration.
  5. Notification No. 10/2017 – Integrated Tax, dated 13 October 2017This is the rescue. It exempts persons making inter-State supplies of taxable services with aggregate turnover below the Section 22 threshold from the compulsory registration requirement of Section 24(i). So a service exporter below ₹20 lakh of aggregate turnover is not obliged to register.
  6. ConclusionRegister when aggregate turnover (exports included) crosses ₹20 lakh, or earlier by choice. Verify both the notification's continued operation and the current threshold on gst.gov.in / cbic.gov.in — this is a notification-level position that can change.
The timing trapThe obligation to register arises when you cross the threshold, and you must apply within 30 days of becoming liable (Section 25(1)). Your registration is effective from the date you became liable if you apply within 30 days — otherwise from the date of grant. If you cross ₹20 lakh in November and apply in February, you have a gap during which you were liable and unregistered, you cannot have filed an LUT for that period, and the department's position may be that your exports in that window were not validly zero-rated. Track your rolling aggregate turnover monthly in a spreadsheet. This is a five-minute monthly task that prevents a six-figure problem.

IEC — the registration you probably do not need

The Importer Exporter Code is issued by DGFT under the Foreign Trade (Development and Regulation) Act 1992. It is mandatory for import and export of goods. For services, DGFT's position has been that IEC is required only where the service provider is taking benefits under the Foreign Trade Policy (for example, the erstwhile SEIS scheme) or where the service falls within a notified category. A software consultant receiving bank wires for consultancy generally does not need one.

Two practical caveats. First, some bank relationship managers ask for an IEC reflexively when a foreign remittance arrives, because their goods-export playbook says so. It costs ₹500 and takes a day, so if your bank is blocking a ₹8 lakh remittance over it, just get one rather than arguing. Second, if you ever need an e-BRC (electronic Bank Realisation Certificate) from the DGFT portal — occasionally demanded for government tenders or scheme claims — you need an IEC to generate it. Verify the current service-export position at dgft.gov.in.

Non-STPI registration and SOFTEX — the one nobody warns you about

Export of software, including software services delivered electronically, is a "software export" for exchange-control purposes. Under the RBI's Master Direction on Export of Goods and Services, software exports are declared in Form SOFTEX, which must be certified by a designated authority — in practice STPI, or the Development Commissioner for an SEZ unit. A consultant who is not in an STP or SEZ scheme registers as a non-STPI unit at stpi.in in order to get SOFTEX forms certified.

  1. Register as a non-STPI unitApply online at stpi.in with PAN, incorporation/registration proof, IEC (if held), a board resolution or proprietor declaration, address proof and a brief project profile. A registration certificate issues, usually valid for a defined period and renewable.
  2. File SOFTEX periodicallyTypically monthly, listing each export invoice with invoice number, date, value, currency, client details and the purpose code. Many jurisdictions allow a consolidated monthly statement rather than one form per invoice.
  3. Get it certifiedSTPI validates and returns certified SOFTEX. Service charges are slab-based on annual export value and payable annually.
  4. Bank closes the EDPMS entryYour authorised dealer bank matches the certified SOFTEX against the inward remittance in the Export Data Processing and Monitoring System and closes the shipping-bill-equivalent entry.
  5. Realisation timelineExport proceeds must be realised and repatriated within the period prescribed in the RBI Master Direction (commonly cited as nine months from the date of export for non-SEZ exporters). Extensions are possible through the AD bank. Verify the current period at rbi.org.in.
Ask your bank before you spend money on thisPractice varies enormously. Some banks process software-consultancy inward remittances with nothing more than a purpose code and a self-declaration, especially at smaller values. Others will not release funds, or will keep the EDPMS entry open and start sending you reminders, until certified SOFTEX is produced. The economical approach: ask your relationship manager in writing, "For inward remittances under purpose code P0802 of approximately US$X per month, will you require certified SOFTEX, and at what value threshold?" Act on the written answer. Thresholds, waivers and the Master Direction itself change — verify current status at rbi.org.in and stpi.in.

Income Tax for the Independent Professional

Section 44ADA of the Income-tax Act 1961 is the most valuable provision in Indian law for a software consultant, and it is routinely misunderstood. It lets a qualifying professional declare 50% of gross receipts as income, pay tax on that, keep no books, face no audit, and pay advance tax in a single instalment. On ₹50 lakh of receipts that is a ₹25 lakh deduction you did not have to spend or document. This section explains exactly who qualifies, exactly what the limits are, what happens when you cross them, and when regular taxation is actually better.

Every figure in this section is as at FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27)Slab rates, the rebate under Section 87A, surcharge thresholds, the Section 44ADA ceilings and the audit thresholds under Section 44AB are all set annually by the Finance Act. Verify on incometax.gov.in. Note further that the Income-tax Act, 2025 is scheduled to replace the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026 with a unified "tax year" concept and renumbered provisions — the substance of presumptive taxation is expected to carry over, but the section numbers used throughout this guide will change. Always cross-check against the new section map.

Section 44ADA, precisely

Who is eligibleA person resident in India being an individual or a partnership firm other than a limited liability partnership, engaged in a profession referred to in Section 44AA(1).A proprietor consultant: yes. An LLP: expressly excluded. A company: not eligible. A non-resident: not eligible.

Residential status — the precondition everyone skips

Section 44ADA requires you to be resident in India. Residence under Section 6 turns on days of physical presence: broadly, 182 days or more in the previous year, or 60 days or more in the previous year together with 365 days or more in the preceding four years (with relaxations to 182 days for citizens leaving for employment and, in certain cases, 120 days for visiting Indian citizens/PIOs with Indian income above ₹15 lakh). A consultant who spends eight months a year with a client in Dubai may become non-resident and lose 44ADA entirely, while gaining a different and more complex set of problems. If you travel heavily, track your days and get a residency opinion annually. Verify Section 6 as currently in force on incometax.gov.in.

Deductions and Expenses Without Presumptive Taxation

If you are above the 44ADA ceiling, or your real cost base exceeds 50%, you compute professional income the ordinary way: receipts minus expenses "wholly and exclusively" incurred for the profession, under Sections 30 to 37 of the Income-tax Act 1961. That phrase — wholly and exclusively — is where most disputes live. This section covers what is allowable, at what rate, what documentation survives scrutiny, and what the assessing officer will disallow.

The allowability test

Wholly and exclusively

Section 37(1). The expense must be for the purpose of the profession, not personal. Mixed-use items (home, car, phone) must be apportioned on a defensible basis and the basis documented contemporaneously.

Not capital in nature

A laptop is capital — you claim depreciation, not the full cost. A ₹2,400 monthly SaaS subscription is revenue — fully deductible. The line matters for cash flow and for the written-down value block.

Not specifically disallowed

Section 40(a)(ia) disallows 30% of a payment to a resident where TDS was deductible and not deducted. Section 40A(3) disallows cash payments above ₹10,000 to a person in a day. Section 43B(h) disallows unpaid MSE dues.

The regime election — a rule that bites professionals specifically

Under Section 115BAC as it now operates, the new regime is the default. A person with income from business or profession who wishes to opt out into the old regime must furnish Form 10-IEA on or before the due date for filing the return under Section 139(1). Critically, a business/professional assessee may switch out of the new regime and back only once in a lifetime — having opted out and then returned to the new regime, you cannot opt out again. A salaried person may choose afresh each year; a professional cannot. Model both regimes before your first Form 10-IEA, and verify the current rule and form number on incometax.gov.in.

Record-keeping that survives an assessment

  • A separate current account used only for the profession. Mixing personal and business transactions is the single most damaging habit, because it makes every expense arguable.
  • Every expense paid by bank transfer, UPI from the business account, or a business credit card. Nothing in cash above ₹10,000 and preferably nothing in cash at all.
  • A monthly folder structure: 2025-26/09-Sep/invoices-in/, invoices-out/, bank/, contracts/. Name files YYYY-MM-DD_vendor_amount.pdf.
  • A fixed asset register with date of purchase, date put to use, cost, block, and WDV carried forward — maintained even on 44ADA.
  • A TDS register for every payment you make to a sub-contractor or professional, with challan numbers and Form 16A issued.
  • Contemporaneous notes for apportioned expenses: a one-page memo setting out the home-office area percentage and the phone business-use percentage, dated at the start of the year.
  • Retention: Rule 6F requires specified professionals to keep books for six years from the end of the relevant assessment year. Keep seven, digitally, in two places.
  • Download Form 26AS and the Annual Information Statement (AIS) every quarter and reconcile them against your own records. Mismatches are the number one trigger for an automated notice under Section 143(1)(a).

Advance Tax and the Quarterly Rhythm

A salaried engineer never thinks about advance tax because payroll deducts TDS every month. A consultant receiving gross wires from a US client has no TDS at all, which means the entire year's tax is yours to compute, fund and deposit on a statutory schedule. Miss it and you pay 1% a month under Sections 234B and 234C — not catastrophic, but entirely avoidable, and the interest is not deductible.

GST for Service Exporters, In Depth

Export of services from India is zero-rated — you charge no GST, and you recover the GST you paid on inputs. But "export of services" is a defined term with five cumulative conditions, and the place-of-supply rules in Section 13 of the IGST Act contain a trap (the "intermediary" rule) that has caught a great many consultants and agencies. This section goes through the mechanics in the order you will meet them.

GST law as at early 2026 — verify everythingThe provisions cited are from the CGST Act 2017 and IGST Act 2017 as amended, plus notifications. The rate rationalisation of September 2025 changed the slab structure; the intermediary provision in Section 13(8)(b) has been the subject of sustained constitutional litigation and GST Council recommendations for omission. Confirm the current text of Sections 2(6), 13 and 16, the applicable rate for your SAC code, and the status of Section 13(8)(b) at gst.gov.in and cbic.gov.in before relying on this.

The intermediary trap, explained properly

Section 2(13) of the IGST Act defines an intermediary as "a broker, an agent or any other person, by whatever name called, who arranges or facilitates the supply of goods or services or both, or securities, between two or more persons, but does not include a person who supplies such goods or services or both or securities on his own account."

You are NOT an intermediary (good)

You contract directly with the foreign client. You perform the work yourself or through people you employ and pay. You bear the delivery risk. You invoice your own fee for your own service. This is supply "on your own account" — Section 13(2) applies, place of supply is the client's location, it is an export, zero-rated.

You MAY be an intermediary (bad)

You introduce a foreign client to an Indian development shop and take a 15% commission. You run a marketplace matching foreign buyers with Indian engineers. You act as a sourcing agent. You are paid a "finder's fee" or "referral commission". Here you arrange a supply between two other persons — Section 13(8)(b) puts the place of supply in India and you must charge 18%.

THE THREE-PARTY TEST (CBIC Circular 159/15/2021-GST guidance)

An intermediary arrangement requires:
  (a) MINIMUM THREE PARTIES — two principals and the intermediary
  (b) TWO DISTINCT SUPPLIES — the main supply between the
      principals, and the ancillary facilitation supply
  (c) The intermediary does NOT supply the main service
      on its own account
  (d) SUB-CONTRACTING IS EXPRESSLY NOT INTERMEDIATION — a person
      who sub-contracts the work but supplies it in their own
      name, on their own account, to the client is NOT an
      intermediary

Route B — paying IGST and claiming refund

If you have no LUT, you charge IGST on the export invoice at the applicable rate (18% for most IT and professional services; verify your SAC rate post-September-2025 rationalisation), pay it in cash or from credit, and then claim refund of the IGST paid under Section 54 read with Rule 96. For goods the refund is largely automated against shipping bills; for services it is not — you file RFD-01 with a statement of invoices and the FIRC/BRC evidence.

Two practical problems make this the inferior route for a consultant. First, your client either pays the extra 18% (they will object, and it is not creditable to them) or you absorb it, which is a direct 18% hit until refunded. Second, service refunds require officer processing and documentary scrutiny, and the money is gone for 4–10 weeks. Use Route A.

Invoice numbering, credit notes and corrections

  • One unbroken series per financial year. Rule 46 requires a consecutive serial number unique for a financial year. Gaps invite questions; duplicates are worse.
  • Separate prefixes for separate streams: EXP/25-26/nnn for exports, DOM/25-26/nnn for domestic, CN/25-26/nnn for credit notes. Each series must itself be consecutive.
  • Never delete or renumber an issued invoice. If an invoice was wrong, issue a credit note (Section 34, CGST Act) referencing the original invoice number and date, then issue a fresh invoice.
  • Credit notes reducing tax liability must be declared in GSTR-1 by the 30 November following the end of the financial year, or the date of filing the annual return, whichever is earlier. Beyond that, you may still issue a commercial credit note but cannot reduce your output tax. Verify the current cut-off.
  • For export invoices under LUT where tax is nil, a credit note has no tax effect but still corrects the reported turnover in Table 6A — which matters for your refund formula.
  • Keep a register: invoice number, date, client, currency, amount, INR at CBIC rate, due date, date paid, INR received, FIRA reference, SOFTEX reference. One spreadsheet. It answers every question anyone will ever ask you.

Choosing your primary rail — a decision rule

  1. Invoices above US$10,000Use a direct SWIFT wire and negotiate the FX rate with your bank's treasury desk. Above roughly US$10,000 per wire, most Indian banks will improve the card rate by 15–40 paise if you call before the conversion. That is ₹1,500–₹4,000 per wire for one phone call.
  2. Invoices US$1,000–US$10,000Wise Business is usually cheapest and fastest, with mid-market FX. Confirm FIRA availability with Wise and with your CA before you make it your only rail.
  3. Invoices below US$1,000Wise or Payoneer. Do not use SWIFT — the fixed charges dominate.
  4. Clients who insist on their own platformDeel, Remote, Upwork. Accept it, read who the contracting party is, and check how you will evidence receipt in convertible foreign exchange.
  5. AlwaysRun two rails. Platforms freeze accounts, banks have compliance queries, and a single point of failure in your cash collection is a business risk, not an inconvenience.
FIRA availability is the deciding factor if you are GST-registeredTo claim a GST refund on zero-rated exports you must evidence receipt of payment in convertible foreign exchange. Before you move all your billing onto any platform, get written confirmation of what documentation they will issue and show it to your CA. RBI and FEMA Compliance for Inward Remittance

Every dollar that enters India does so under the Foreign Exchange Management Act 1999, through an Authorised Dealer bank, against a purpose code, and — for software exports — with an export declaration trail. You will not be sent to jail for getting this wrong. You will, however, have remittances held, EDPMS entries left open, periodic nagging letters from your bank, and potentially a problem with your GST refund. Do it properly from the first invoice.

FEMA positions change by Master Direction amendment, not by statuteThe governing instruments are the Foreign Exchange Management Act 1999, the Foreign Exchange Management (Export of Goods and Services) Regulations, and the RBI's consolidated Master Direction on Export of Goods and Services, all at rbi.org.in. Purpose codes are published by the RBI for balance-of-payments reporting. Realisation periods, SOFTEX thresholds and EEFC rules have all been amended multiple times. Verify everything in this section against the current Master Direction.

Realisation, EDPMS and what happens when a client pays late

  1. The obligationSection 7 of FEMA 1999 and the Export Regulations require an exporter to furnish a declaration and to realise and repatriate the full export value within the prescribed period. For software exports, this operates through SOFTEX and EDPMS.
  2. The periodCommonly stated as nine months from the date of export for non-SEZ exporters, having been varied by the RBI from time to time. Verify the period currently in force at rbi.org.in.
  3. What an open entry looks likeYour AD bank's EDPMS shows an export declaration with no matching realisation. The bank sends you a reminder, then a stronger reminder, then escalates. Repeated open entries can affect your ability to transact.
  4. ExtensionAD banks are empowered to extend the realisation period in defined circumstances on written application, supported by evidence — the client's email confirming a revised payment date, a dispute note, a restructured payment plan. Apply before the period expires, not after.
  5. Write-offWhere the amount is genuinely unrecoverable, the Regulations and Master Direction permit self-write-off up to specified percentages of the previous year's export realisations, and bank-approved write-off beyond that, subject to conditions and evidence of recovery efforts. Verify the current limits and conditions.
  6. Short realisationWhere the client deducts bank charges or a disputed amount, your realisation will be less than the declared export value. Document the reason and ask your bank how to reconcile the EDPMS entry — most banks accept a reconciliation statement with the MT103 showing correspondent deductions.
Contraventions of FEMA are compoundable, not criminal — but they are not freeSection 13 of FEMA 1999 provides for a penalty of up to three times the sum involved where the amount is quantifiable. In practice, genuine delays by a consultant with documentation are resolved administratively through the AD bank, and the RBI's compounding mechanism exists for real contraventions. Double Taxation and Foreign Withholding

The single most common question an Indian consultant receives from a new US client is "we need a W-9 for tax" — which is the wrong form, because you are not a US person. The second is "our finance team says we have to withhold 30%" — which is usually wrong too. Knowing precisely why, and having the right form ready, is worth thousands of dollars per engagement and makes you look like a professional rather than a hobbyist.

Treaty positions and forms as at early 2026The India–US Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement, Form W-8BEN/W-8BEN-E and the US withholding rules under Chapter 3 of the Internal Revenue Code are foreign law. The Indian-side mechanics — Tax Residency Certificate under Section 90(4), Form 10F, foreign tax credit under Rule 128 and Form 67 — are at incometax.gov.in. Verify both sides. For any engagement above a few lakh rupees, pay a cross-border tax adviser for an hour of their time.

How a DTAA actually works

  1. Two countries both claim the right to tax the same income.India taxes you because you are resident here (Section 5, worldwide income). The US may claim a right because the income has a US source.
  2. The treaty allocates the right.Under Section 90(2) of the Income-tax Act, you may choose the more beneficial of the Act or the treaty. The treaty's distributive articles decide who taxes what.
  3. For business profits (Article 7), the source state may tax only if there is a Permanent Establishment there.A consultant in Bengaluru with no US office, no US employees, no dependent agent and no fixed place of business in the US has no US PE. The US therefore has no right to tax the business profits.
  4. For independent personal services (Article 15 of the India–US treaty), the source state may tax only if there is a fixed base regularly available, or presence exceeding the specified number of days.Again, not you.
  5. The trap is Article 12 — Royalties and Fees for Included Services.If your service is "fees for included services", the US may tax it at a reduced treaty rate even without a PE. The definition requires that the service either is ancillary to the application of a right/property for which a royalty is paid, or "makes available" technical knowledge, experience, skill, know-how or processes, or consists of the development and transfer of a technical plan or design.
  6. Where tax is nevertheless withheld, India gives credit.Article 25 (relief from double taxation) plus Section 90/91 read with Rule 128 and Form 67.
The "make available" test is the one to understandRoutine software development, running a pipeline, maintaining infrastructure or delivering a report does not normally "make available" technical knowledge — the client cannot independently apply the technology after you leave. Training the client's engineers so they can thereafter perform the service themselves, or transferring a technical plan or design, may. The distinction is fact-specific and has produced a large body of Indian case law on inbound payments. For outbound services from India to the US, the practical outcome is that most consulting engagements are Article 7/15 business or professional income taxable only in India. Take advice for your specific deliverables.

The equalisation levy — officially gone

The equalisation levy was introduced in 2016 as a 6% charge on payments to non-residents for online advertisement services, and extended in 2020 as a 2% charge on consideration received by non-resident e-commerce operators for e-commerce supply or services. Both have been withdrawn: the 2% e-commerce levy with effect from 1 August 2024, and the 6% advertisement levy with effect from 1 April 2025. For an Indian consultant this matters in two directions: you no longer need to consider whether payments you make to foreign digital platforms attract the 6% levy, and foreign clients who were told to gross up for Indian levies no longer need to. Residual compliance for earlier periods — Form 1 annual statements, interest and penalties for past years — may still be live. Verify at incometax.gov.in.

Pricing

Most Indian consultants price by taking their last salary, dividing by 2,000 hours, adding a bit, and then discounting when the client hesitates. That method guarantees you earn less than you did as an employee while carrying all the risk. The geography discount question

Clients sometimes argue that because you live in India your rate should be lower. The correct answer is that you sell outcomes, not cost-of-living.   1. Decide the number and the date before you talk to anyone.A 10–20% annual increase is normal and expected. A 60% jump needs a story.

Positioning and niching

The single highest-leverage change most Indian consultants can make is to stop describing themselves as a "full-stack developer with 9 years of experience" and start describing a specific problem for a specific kind of company. Compare:

Generic (low rate, high competition)

"Senior software engineer with 9 years of experience in Python, React, AWS, Docker, Kubernetes and machine learning. Available for freelance projects."

Competes with 400,000 people. Buyer cannot tell you apart. Priced by the hour against the cheapest adequate option.

Positioned (high rate, low competition)

"I take RAG systems that work in a demo and make them survive production at Series B SaaS companies — evaluation harnesses, retrieval quality, cost and latency. Four shipped; two were rescues."

Competes with perhaps 50 people globally. Buyer self-identifies instantly. Priced against the cost of failure.

Proposals and Closing

A proposal is not a document that describes what you will do. It is a document that lets a buyer justify a decision they have already half-made, to people who were not on the call. Write it for the person who has to approve the spend and was not in the room.

The discovery call, structured

  1. Minutes 0–3: frame"Thanks for the time. I'd like to spend most of this understanding what's actually going on, then tell you honestly whether I'm the right person. If I'm not, I'll say so and suggest who is. Does that work?" This earns permission to ask hard questions.
  2. Minutes 3–20: the situationWhat are you trying to achieve? What have you already tried? Who is working on it today? What does the current system look like? When did this become a priority? Shut up and let them talk. Most consultants talk 70% of a discovery call and lose it.
  3. Minutes 20–30: the cost of inactionThis is the part everyone skips. "If this is still broken in six months, what happens?" "What has it cost so far?" "What is the revenue or risk exposure attached to it?" You cannot price value you have not quantified, and the client often has not quantified it either — doing so for them is itself consulting.
  4. Minutes 30–38: the decision processWho else needs to agree? What is the budget range you are working within? Is there a procurement process? What is the timeline? Has anything like this been bought before, and from whom? If you do not ask this, you will send a proposal into a void.
  5. Minutes 38–45: your honest readReflect back what you heard. Say what you would do and roughly what it would cost — give a range verbally on the call. "Something in the US$15,000–25,000 range over four to six weeks" lets them react while you can see their face. Then agree the next step with a date.
  6. Within 24 hours: written recapFive bullets of what you understood, the range you discussed, and what happens next. This single email closes more deals than any proposal template.
Never send a proposal without a budget conversationIf you do not know the range, you are guessing, and a guess is wrong in one of two expensive ways. Ask directly: "So I size this sensibly — are we talking about something closer to US$10,000 or closer to US$60,000?" Buyers answer this far more often than consultants expect. If they genuinely cannot say, give them three options at different price points and let the choice reveal the budget.

Fixed-price risk, quantifiedOn a fixed-price engagement your effective hourly rate is the fee divided by actual hours. A US$18,000 project estimated at 140 hours pays US$129/hour. Fixed price is only safe where (a) you have done this exact work before, (b) the acceptance criteria are objectively testable, and (c) there is a genuine change-control clause you are willing to use.

Getting Paid on Time and Dealing With Non-payment

Every consultant eventually meets the client who goes quiet. The defence is almost entirely structural and almost entirely applied before the work starts: deposits, milestones, short terms, and control of something the client needs. Once an invoice is 90 days old your realistic recovery probability is well under 50%, and legal action across borders rarely makes economic sense below roughly ₹15–20 lakh.

When to write it off

  1. Do the arithmetic honestly.Recovery value = (amount owed) × (probability of recovery) − (cost of pursuit) − (value of your time at your own hourly rate). A ₹4 lakh debt with a 35% recovery probability and ₹1.5 lakh of pursuit cost plus 40 hours of your time at ₹5,000 is worth ₹1.4 lakh − ₹1.5 lakh − ₹2 lakh = negative ₹2.1 lakh. Write it off.
  2. Write off formally.In your books, record it as a bad debt. Under Section 36(1)(vii) of the Income-tax Act a bad debt written off as irrecoverable in the accounts is deductible, subject to Section 36(2) — broadly, the debt must have been taken into account in computing income. On 44ADA you have no such claim, because you are taxed on receipts, so an unpaid invoice simply never becomes a receipt. Confirm the treatment with your CA.
  3. Deal with the FEMA side.An unrealised export receipt leaves an open EDPMS entry. Apply to your AD bank for write-off under the Master Direction, with evidence of your recovery efforts — the dunning emails, the legal notice, the collections agency report. Keep that file.
  4. Deal with the GST side.If you exported under LUT and were never paid, the export condition in Section 2(6)(iv) is not satisfied. Discuss with your CA whether any reversal or adjustment is required, particularly if you claimed an ITC refund attributable to that turnover.
  5. Extract the lesson, in writing.What was the earliest signal you ignored? Almost always there was one: a missed kickoff, a vague answer about who approves spend, a procurement process that started after work did, a founder who was evasive about funding. Write it in a one-page "client red flags" note and read it before every new engagement.
  6. Move on fast.The opportunity cost of a six-month collection obsession is usually larger than the debt. Your best recovery of a bad debt is a good new client.

Scaling Beyond Yourself

A solo consultant's revenue is capped by hours. There are four ways past the cap — sub-contract, build an agency, productise, or shift to retainers — and each changes what your job is. Choose deliberately, because the version of you that enjoys deep technical work may actively hate running a 12-person services firm.

The four routes are not a ladder. They are alternatives with different lifestyles attached.

Sub-contracting — the mechanics

  1. Contract back-to-back.Your agreement with the associate should mirror the obligations you owe the client — confidentiality, IP assignment to you (so you can assign onward), warranties, and a liability position you can live with. Never promise the client more than you have secured from the associate.
  2. Deduct TDS.Payments to a resident professional attract TDS under Section 194J — 10% for professional services, 2% for technical services — above the annual threshold per payee (raised to ₹50,000 by the Finance Act 2025; verify). Deposit by the 7th of the following month, file Form 26Q quarterly, issue Form 16A. Failure means a 30% disallowance under Section 40(a)(ia) plus interest.
  3. Watch the GST position.If your associate is GST-registered they charge you 18%, which you claim as ITC and recover through your export refund. If they are unregistered, there is no GST on their invoice and nothing to claim.
  4. Do not become an intermediary.Contract with the client in your own name, on your own account, bearing the delivery risk. See the GST section.
  5. Price the margin properly.If you pay ₹3,000/hour and bill US$95 (₹8,360), your gross margin is 64% — but you are carrying delivery risk, payment risk, review time and reputation risk. A 30–45% net margin after your own review time is realistic.
  6. Review everything before it leaves.Your name is on it. Budget 15–20% of the associate's hours for your own review, and include that in the price.
  7. Disclose or do not, but be consistent.Most clients are relaxed about sub-contracting if they know. Almost none are relaxed about discovering it. Check your MSA — many require prior written consent.

When to incorporate a Private Limited

  • You have a co-founder.Shared ownership needs a cap table, and a cap table needs a company. Do it on day one of the partnership, not later.
  • You want to hire employees with ESOPs.Options require a company and a Section 62(1)(b) scheme. A proprietorship cannot grant equity.
  • You are raising external investment.No institutional investor will fund a proprietorship or an LLP.
  • Gross receipts are consistently above ₹1 crore and you are reinvesting rather than extracting.The 22% rate under Section 115BAA plus deferral of the dividend tax starts to beat the 30%+surcharge personal rate.
  • A material client genuinely cannot contract with an individual.Verify this with their procurement before spending the money; it is often assumed rather than true.
  • You are building a product with a view to a sale or acquisition.Buyers acquire shares, not sole traders.
  • Your work carries liability that insurance will not cover and limited liability is a real protection rather than a comfort blanket.
Reasons that are NOT good enough to incorporate"It looks more professional." "Someone on LinkedIn said so." "I want a .com with a company name" (you can trade under a business name as a proprietor). "For tax savings" (at consulting receipts below ₹1 crore, incorporating usually increases your total tax). "To separate personal and business" (a separate current account does that). Model the tax outcome with your CA before incorporating. The worked comparisons earlier in this guide show the gap is often ₹3–9 lakh a year against you.

Frequently Asked Questions

I earn ₹42 lakh a year from one US client. Do I need to register for GST?
Yes. "Aggregate turnover" under Section 2(6) of the CGST Act 2017 includes exports, so your ₹42 lakh crosses the ₹20 lakh services threshold in Section 22. You must apply within 30 days of becoming liable. Once registered, file Form GST RFD-11 (LUT) for the financial year so you can export without paying IGST, then file GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B every period — including nil returns. You will also be able to claim refund of unutilised input tax credit on your cloud, co-working, hardware and professional fees, which is usually worth ₹1–2 lakh a year. Verify the current threshold and the continued operation of Notification 10/2017-Integrated Tax at gst.gov.in.
My gross receipts will be about ₹68 lakh this year. Can I still use Section 44ADA?
Yes, provided your cash receipts do not exceed 5% of total gross receipts — the enhanced ceiling is ₹75 lakh (₹7,500,000) where that condition is met, against a base ceiling of ₹50 lakh. Bank wires, UPI, NEFT and account-payee cheques are not "cash" for this test. Two cautions. First, the test applies to receipts for the whole previous year, so a late client payment in March can push you over retrospectively and remove 44ADA for the entire year — track a rolling total weekly. Second, at ₹68 lakh you are close enough to the edge that you should maintain basic books anyway, so that a crossing does not leave you with a tax audit and no ledgers. Verify the current limits on incometax.gov.in.
My US client's finance team says they must withhold 30%. Are they right?
Almost certainly not, if you perform all services in India and have no US presence. Compensation for personal services is generally sourced to where the services are performed; services performed outside the United States by a non-resident alien are generally foreign-source and not subject to US Chapter 3 withholding. Separately, Articles 7 and 15 of the India–US treaty reserve taxing rights over business profits and independent personal services to India absent a US permanent establishment or fixed base. Send a signed Form W-8BEN (not a W-9 — that is for US persons) with a short covering note. Where their counsel still insists, the watch-point is Article 12(4) "fees for included services" and the "make available" test, which can be engaged if you transfer a technical plan or design or train their staff to perform the service themselves. Get cross-border tax advice for any engagement above roughly ₹20 lakh; this is US law and it depends on your exact facts.
Do I need an IEC to receive payments for software consulting?
Usually no. The Importer Exporter Code is mandatory for import and export of goods; for services, DGFT's position has been that IEC is required only where you claim benefits under the Foreign Trade Policy or fall within a notified category. In practice some bank relationship managers ask for one reflexively on an inward remittance. It costs ₹500 and takes a day on dgft.gov.in, so if a remittance is being held over it, obtain one rather than argue. You will also need an IEC if you ever need to generate an e-BRC. Verify the current service-export position at dgft.gov.in.
Do I have to file SOFTEX? My bank has never mentioned it.
It depends entirely on your bank and your values. Software exports are declared in Form SOFTEX under the RBI's Master Direction on Export of Goods and Services, certified by STPI for a non-STPI registered unit, and matched by your AD bank against the inward remittance in EDPMS. Some banks process consultancy remittances under purpose code P0802 with nothing more than an invoice; others will not close the EDPMS entry without certified SOFTEX and will send you escalating reminders. Ask your bank's trade desk in writing: "At what value, if any, will you require certified SOFTEX for inward remittances under P0802?" Act on the written answer. Registration with STPI costs a few thousand rupees plus slab-based annual service charges on export value. Verify current requirements at rbi.org.in and stpi.in.
Should I incorporate a Private Limited company to look more credible?
Not for credibility alone. At ₹20 lakh of receipts, the worked comparison in this guide shows a proprietorship using Section 44ADA keeping roughly ₹3.7 lakh a year more than an OPC, because corporate profit is taxed at about 25.17% and then taxed again at your slab rate when you take a dividend out, on top of mandatory statutory audit and ROC filings. Incorporate when there is a structural reason: a co-founder, ESOPs, external investment, a client whose procurement genuinely blocks individuals, or sustained receipts above ₹1 crore with profit being reinvested. Credibility is cheaper to buy with a business name, your own domain, a professional indemnity certificate and two published case studies. Run the numbers with your CA for your own figures before deciding.
A client wants to pay me in USDT because their bank is slow. Is that fine?
No. Three separate problems. For GST, Section 2(6)(iv) of the IGST Act requires payment in convertible foreign exchange (or INR where the RBI permits) — a stablecoin is neither, so your supply may fail the export test and become taxable at 18% out of your own pocket. For FEMA, there is no authorised dealer, no purpose code, no FIRA and no EDPMS entry, so the export proceeds are not realised through the prescribed channel. For income tax, virtual digital assets are taxed under Section 115BBH at a flat 30% on gains with no deductions other than cost of acquisition and no loss set-off, plus 1% TDS under Section 194S on transfer. Ask the client to use Wise instead — it is usually faster than their bank anyway. Verify the current VDA tax treatment at incometax.gov.in.
I am still employed. Can I take a small consulting project on weekends?
Read your employment contract and the employee handbook first — exclusivity ("whole time and attention"), conflict of interest, IP assignment and systems-use clauses are what govern, and handbooks are usually incorporated by reference. If the contract permits outside work with prior written consent, ask for it in writing with a description of the engagement and a confirmation of no conflict. Then: use only your own laptop, accounts and network; work only outside contracted hours and on-call; pick a sector unrelated to your employer's business, customers and prospects; never reuse employer code, documents, prompts or customer information; and declare the income in your ITR. The legal risk is usually smaller than the practical risk — termination for cause, a withheld relieving letter, forfeited ESOPs and a negative background verification. A post-employment non-compete is void under Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, but that gives you no protection while you are still on payroll.
What should I charge a US client as a senior engineer with nine years of experience?
Build it from your target income rather than from a benchmark. Take the post-tax income you need, gross it up for tax, add real business costs, divide by realistic billable hours (45 working weeks × 45 hours × 67% billable ≈ 1,357 hours), then divide again by an expected utilisation of 70–80% in your first two years. For a ₹72 lakh receipts target that calculation lands at roughly US$80–85 per hour. As an observational cross-check, senior Indian independents with a clear specialism commonly sit in the US$50–90/hour band for engineering and higher for advisory, with AI/ML work currently carrying a premium. Publish a three-tier rate card rather than a single number, and expect to raise it 10–20% annually. These bands are observational and will vary by niche; validate against three peers in your specific area.
A client has not paid an invoice that is 70 days overdue. What now?
Work the sequence. Day +3: friendly note assuming an administrative slip, asking who in AP owns it. Day +14: copy AP, ask for a specific payment-run date, offer to supply anything missing, mention the contractual interest without invoicing it. Day +32: formal notice citing your suspension clause and your conditional IP-assignment clause, with a deadline, and suspend if unpaid. Day +55: final notice naming exactly what you will do and when. Alongside that, check your leverage: do you hold anything they need, and does your contract allow you to withhold it? For an Indian client, file free on MSME Samadhaan if you are Udyam-registered — Section 16 of the MSMED Act gives compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate and Section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act denies them the deduction. For a foreign client below roughly US$50,000, an international commercial collections agency on 15–35% contingency is usually better economics than arbitration. Above all, do the arithmetic before you spend six months on it.
Can I claim my rent, laptop and internet if I use Section 44ADA?
No — not separately. Section 44ADA deems 50% of gross receipts to be your income and treats all deductions under Sections 30 to 38, including depreciation, as already allowed. You cannot deduct rent, equipment, internet or software on top of the 50%. The written-down value of your assets is also deemed to have been reduced as if depreciation had been claimed, so keep a fixed asset register even on 44ADA — you will need the correct opening WDV the year you exit. What you can still claim separately are Chapter VI-A deductions (80C, 80D, 80CCD(1B)), but only if you are in the old regime, which for most 44ADA consultants produces a worse outcome than the new regime. Run both in the ITR utility.
What is an EEFC account and should I open one?
An Exchange Earners' Foreign Currency account is a non-interest-bearing current account in India denominated in a foreign currency, into which eligible foreign exchange earnings may be credited, and from which permitted foreign-currency payments may be made without a round-trip conversion. It pays off only if you have meaningful and regular foreign-currency outgo — AWS billed in USD, AI API spend, overseas travel, foreign advisers. The worked example in this guide shows a saving of roughly ₹38,000 a year on US$28,000 of annual foreign spend against US$150,000 of receipts. Below about US$10,000 of annual foreign spend, the administration and the crystallisation discipline are not worth it. Note that balances must be converted to rupees on or before the last day of the month succeeding the month of credit, subject to the current rule. Verify crediting percentages and crystallisation rules at rbi.org.in.
My client's contract says I assign all IP "conceived during the term". Should I sign?
Not as drafted. That wording reaches your unrelated side projects, your reusable libraries and your writing. Push for two changes. First, narrow the assignment to "the deliverables created specifically for the Client under a Statement of Work". Second, add an express Background IP carve-out with a schedule listing your pre-existing tools, libraries, templates and methodologies, coupled with a perpetual non-exclusive licence to the client to use that Background IP as embedded in the deliverables. Also make the assignment conditional on payment in full — it is market-standard and it is the single strongest anti-non-payment clause available to you. Separately, note that Section 19 of the Copyright Act 1957 requires an assignment to be in writing and signed; US-style "work made for hire" language alone may not effect a valid assignment under Indian law, so insist on an express assignment clause.
How much should I hold back from each payment for tax?
Set a fixed percentage and transfer it to a separate account the day the money lands, with no decision involved. On Section 44ADA with deemed income around ₹25 lakh the effective tax on receipts is roughly 7%, so reserve 10%. At deemed income around ₹37 lakh it is roughly 9.5%, so reserve 13%. On regular computation at ₹85 lakh of professional income the effective rate on receipts is roughly 24%, so reserve 28%. Always round up — a surplus on 31 March is a bonus, a shortfall is 1% a month under Sections 234B and 234C. If you are GST-registered and billing Indian clients, the GST you collect is additional and is never your money. Remember that under Section 44ADA/44AD the entire advance tax is payable in a single instalment by 15 March, so the reserve must be fully funded by then.
What is the one thing most new Indian consultants get wrong?
Underpricing, caused by dividing a target annual number by 2,000 hours. The honest divisor is closer to 1,000–1,350 billable hours, because 25–35% of your week goes to sales, admin, compliance and marketing, and because you will not be fully utilised in your first two years. That single arithmetic error produces a rate roughly half what it should be, which then forces 60-hour weeks to hit the number, which eliminates the time needed for business development, which guarantees the gaps that caused the under-utilisation in the first place. The second most common error is treating gross receipts as income and discovering in March that the tax was never set aside.

Onboarding a new client — the checklist

  • Confirm the contracting entity. Which legal entity signs, and which entity pays? If it is an Indian subsidiary, the supply is domestic at 18% GST with 10% TDS, not a zero-rated export. Ask before you quote.
  • Confirm the billing currency and the payment rail. SWIFT, Wise, their platform? Who bears bank charges — insist on "OUR".
  • Get the AP contact name and email. Not the project manager. The person who runs the payment file.
  • Get a PO number if the client is above about 200 people. "No PO, no pay" is a real policy.
  • Signed MSA or short-form consulting agreement with your eight protective clauses intact.
  • Signed SOW with objective acceptance criteria, deemed acceptance after 5 business days, an out-of-scope list, an assumptions register and a milestone payment schedule.
  • Mutual NDA if not already covered by the MSA confidentiality clause.
