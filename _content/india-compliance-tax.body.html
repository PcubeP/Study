How to Use This Guide

This is a working reference for running an Indian business once it exists — the GST, income tax, TDS, ROC and payroll obligations that arrive every month whether or not you remembered them. It is written for a software engineer running a consulting practice, a SaaS product company or a small services firm, and it assumes you will do a meaningful amount of this yourself and hand the rest to a CA. Every process names the form, the portal, the due date, the penalty and the mistake that triggers a notice.

This is educational material, not legal, tax or accounting adviceIndian tax law is amended by every Finance Act and by hundreds of notifications, circulars and instructions each year. Nothing here is advice on your facts. Rates, thresholds, due dates, form numbers and penalties are stated as at FY 2025-26 / early 2026 and must be re-verified against the official portal before you file, claim, deduct or pay. Engage a qualified Chartered Accountant for your returns and a Company Secretary for your ROC filings.
Four large changes are in flight — verify current status before relying on anything here (1) Income-tax Act, 2025 is scheduled to replace the Income-tax Act, 1961 from 1 April 2026, introducing a unified "tax year" concept in place of the "previous year" / "assessment year" pair. All section numbers in this guide follow the 1961 Act; map them to the new Act before use. (2) GST rate rationalisation of September 2025 restructured the slabs to principally 5% and 18% with a 40% demerit rate. Confirm the rate applicable to your exact HSN or SAC code. (3) The four Labour Codes — Code on Wages 2019, Industrial Relations Code 2020, Code on Social Security 2020, and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code 2020 — were brought into force from late 2025, but state rules are still being notified. The Codes change the statutory definition of "wages", which changes PF, gratuity and bonus computations and therefore CTC structuring. (4) The DPDP Act 2023 Rules were notified in late 2025 with phased compliance deadlines. Treat all four as "verify current status".

GST Fundamentals

Goods and Services Tax is a destination-based, multi-stage, value-added tax on the supply of goods and services, with credit available for tax paid on inputs. It replaced a dozen central and state levies in July 2017. For a services business the practical mechanics reduce to four questions: is it a supply, where is the place of supply, when is the time of supply, and what rate applies.

What is a "supply"

Section 7 of the CGST Act defines supply to include all forms of supply of goods or services — sale, transfer, barter, exchange, licence, rental, lease or disposal — made or agreed to be made for a consideration in the course or furtherance of business. Schedule I lists activities treated as supply even without consideration, which catches a number of situations a software business will encounter.

The "intermediary" trap for Indian consultantsIf your contract makes you an agent or broker arranging a supply between your overseas principal and a third party — rather than supplying the service on your own account — you may be an "intermediary". Historically, the place of supply for intermediary services has been the supplier's location, which makes the transaction a domestic supply taxable at 18% rather than a zero-rated export. Indian marketing representatives, sales agents and some BPO arrangements have faced large demands on this basis. Draft your contract to make clear you supply services on a principal-to-principal basis, and get a written opinion. Verify the current statutory position and case law, which has moved.

The rate structure

GST rates were rationalised in September 2025 — verify your exact rateThe rate structure moved to principally 5% and 18% slabs, with a 40% demerit rate for specified goods, alongside nil-rated and exempt categories and special rates for certain items. The earlier 12% and 28% slabs were substantially reorganised. This changed rates for a large number of HSN codes. Do not assume the rate you used last year still applies. Check the rate finder on gst.gov.in or the current rate notification under Section 9 of the CGST Act for your exact HSN or SAC. Most IT and software services have historically been at 18%; confirm this for your SAC.

Exempt is not the same as zero-ratedOn an exempt supply you charge nothing and you cannot claim input tax credit on the inputs used — the tax sticks to you as a cost. On a zero-rated supply you also charge nothing, but you retain full entitlement to input tax credit and can claim a refund of it. Export of services is zero-rated, which is why an Indian software exporter can recover GST paid on cloud bills, rent and professional fees as a cash refund. Confusing the two is one of the most expensive conceptual errors in Indian indirect tax.

GST Registration

Registration under GST is mandatory above a turnover threshold, mandatory from the first rupee in a list of cases under Section 24 regardless of turnover, and available voluntarily to anyone. For a software consultant the real decision is usually whether to register voluntarily before the threshold, and the answer is usually yes.

Aggregate turnover is defined in Section 2(6) and includes, computed on an all-India PAN basis: taxable supplies, exempt supplies, exports, and inter-State supplies of persons with the same PAN — but excludes CGST, SGST, IGST, cess, and the value of inward supplies on which tax is payable under reverse charge. The all-India PAN basis matters: if you have two business lines under one PAN, their turnovers add together.

The reverse-charge trap that catches almost every software startupIf you pay for a US SaaS tool, an overseas freelancer, a foreign cloud bill, or advertising invoiced by an entity outside India, that is an import of services. A pre-revenue startup paying $200 a month for cloud and tooling can therefore be required to register from day one. The REG-01 registration process

  1. Part A — generate a TRNOn gst.gov.in, go to Services → Registration → New Registration. Enter the legal name exactly as per PAN, the PAN, state, district, email and mobile. Validate both OTPs. You receive a 15-digit Temporary Reference Number valid for 15 days.
  2. Part B — the substantive applicationLog in with the TRN and complete ten tabs: Business Details, Promoter/Partners, Authorised Signatory, Authorised Representative, Principal Place of Business, Additional Places of Business, Goods and Services (HSN/SAC), Bank Accounts, State Specific Information, and Verification.
  3. Upload documentsSee the table below. Scans must be legible and within the portal's file size limits.
  4. Aadhaar authenticationThe authorised signatory and promoters can authenticate via Aadhaar OTP. Completing Aadhaar authentication substantially reduces the chance of being selected for physical verification and shortens the processing timeline. Declining it makes site verification near-certain.
  5. Biometric verification where applicableIn notified states, applicants selected by risk parameters must attend a GST Suvidha Kendra in person for biometric Aadhaar authentication and document verification. This has been rolled out progressively state by state — check whether it applies in your state.
  6. Submit with DSC or EVCCompanies and LLPs must use a DSC. Proprietorships and partnerships may use EVC (Aadhaar OTP).
  7. ProcessingThe officer either approves, raises a query in Form REG-03 (you respond in REG-04 within 7 working days), or rejects in REG-05. If approved, you receive the registration certificate in Form REG-06 with your GSTIN.
  8. Physical verification if orderedThe officer visits the principal place of business and uploads a report in Form REG-30. Have the name board up, a desk, the rent agreement and the electricity bill ready.

GST Invoicing

Issued where the taxable value or tax charged in the original invoice was less than it should have been. A debit note increases your output liability. Input Tax Credit

Input tax credit is the mechanism that makes GST a value-added tax rather than a cascading turnover tax. It is also the single largest source of disputes, notices and cash loss for small businesses, because the conditions in Section 16 are cumulative, the blocked-credit list in Section 17(5) is long, and the time limit in Section 16(4) is absolute.

Section 16 — the four conditions, all of which must be satisfied

  1. You possess a tax invoice or debit noteIssued by a registered supplier, containing all Rule 46 particulars. A proforma invoice, a quotation or a bank statement is not enough.
  2. You have received the goods or servicesIncluding the "bill-to ship-to" deeming provision where goods are delivered to a third party on your direction.
  3. The tax has actually been paid to the GovernmentBy the supplier, in cash or through their own credit. This is the condition you cannot verify directly and cannot control — which is why GSTR-2B matching matters.
  4. The supplier has furnished the returnUnder Section 39. And, under Section 16(2)(aa), the invoice must have been furnished by the supplier in their GSTR-1 and communicated to you in GSTR-2B.
Section 16(2)(aa) shifted the risk onto youBefore this provision, you could claim credit on an invoice you held even if the supplier had not filed. Now, ITC is available only if the invoice appears in your auto-generated GSTR-2B. If your vendor does not file GSTR-1, or files it with your GSTIN typed wrongly, you lose the credit — even though you paid the tax to them. The practical consequence: reconcile GSTR-2B every single month and chase defaulting vendors before you pay them. Consider a contractual clause withholding the GST component until the credit reflects in your 2B.

The 180-day payment rule — second proviso to Section 16(2)

THE RULE
If you claim ITC on an invoice and then FAIL to pay the supplier
the value of supply PLUS the tax within 180 DAYS from the invoice
date, you must:
  (a) add the ITC claimed to your output tax liability, and
  (b) pay interest from the date of claiming the ITC.

You can RE-CLAIM the credit when you eventually pay the supplier.
The re-claim is not subject to the Section 16(4) time limit.

Reversal under Rules 42 and 43 — mixed use

Where inputs are used partly for taxable (including zero-rated) supplies and partly for exempt supplies or non-business purposes, the common credit must be apportioned and the exempt portion reversed. Rule 42 covers inputs and input services; Rule 43 covers capital goods, spread over 60 months.

RULE 42 REVERSAL - simplified worked example

Facts for the month:
  Total common input tax credit (C2)            Rs 2,00,000
  Total turnover in the State                   Rs 50,00,000
    of which EXEMPT turnover (E)                Rs  5,00,000
    of which TAXABLE + ZERO-RATED               Rs 45,00,000

  Note: ZERO-RATED (export) supplies are treated as TAXABLE
  for this computation, not exempt. This matters a great deal
  to a services exporter.

Step 1  Identify credit exclusively for exempt supplies (D1 input)
          -> reverse in full

Step 2  Apportion the common credit:
          Reversal = C2 x (E / F)
                   = 2,00,000 x (5,00,000 / 50,00,000)
                   = 2,00,000 x 0.10
                   = Rs 20,000

Step 3  Add a further 5% of common credit treated as used for
        non-business purposes, where applicable (D2)
                   = 2,00,000 x 5%  = Rs 10,000

Step 4  Total reversal for the month = Rs 30,000, added to
        output tax liability in GSTR-3B Table 4(B).

Step 5  ANNUAL RECOMPUTATION
        Redo the calculation for the whole financial year before
        the due date of the September return of the following
        year. If the annual reversal exceeds the sum of monthly
        reversals, pay the difference with interest. If it is
        less, claim the excess back as credit.

Rule 43 applies the same logic to CAPITAL GOODS, but spreads the
useful life over 60 months and reverses monthly.

Section 16(4) — the absolute time limit

ITC that is not claimed in time is lost permanentlyUnder Section 16(4), input tax credit on an invoice or debit note for a financial year cannot be taken after the 30th day of November following the end of that financial year, or the date of filing the annual return for that year, whichever is earlier. There is no condonation, no appeal on equity, and no mechanism to recover it. For a company with ₹60 lakh of annual GST-bearing expenses, forgetting to claim credit on ₹10 lakh of invoices is ₹1.8 lakh of permanent cash loss. Reverse Charge Mechanism

Under reverse charge, the liability to pay GST shifts from the supplier to the recipient. You pay the tax on a purchase, in cash, and then claim it back as input tax credit if it is otherwise eligible. It is cash-flow negative, administratively fiddly, and it is the single most commonly missed GST obligation in small Indian companies — because the expense looks like an ordinary purchase in the books.

The cash-flow effect

RCM must be paid in cash — you cannot use your credit balanceSection 49(4) permits the electronic credit ledger to be used only for payment of output tax. Reverse charge liability is not output tax in that sense and must be discharged from the electronic cash ledger. So a startup with a large accumulated ITC balance and heavy overseas SaaS spend still writes a cheque to the Government every month, and then adds an equal amount to a credit balance it may not be able to use. For a pre-revenue company with no domestic output tax, this is a pure cash drain until it can claim a refund — and refund of ITC accumulated purely from RCM, where there are no zero-rated supplies and no inverted duty structure, is generally not available. Composition Scheme

The composition scheme under Section 10 of the CGST Act lets a small taxpayer pay a flat percentage of turnover instead of running the full output-tax-minus-input-credit machinery. Presumptive Taxation — Sections 44AD and 44ADA

Presumptive taxation lets eligible small taxpayers declare a deemed percentage of turnover as income, skip detailed books of account, and avoid a tax audit. For a solo software consultant it is the single largest administrative and often financial advantage available in Indian tax law — and it is unavailable to an LLP or a company, which is a strong argument for staying a proprietorship longer than instinct suggests.

Is a software developer a "profession" under 44ADA or a "business" under 44AD?This matters enormously — 50% deemed income versus 6%. The CBDT notification under Section 44AA(1) includes "technical consultancy" and notified information technology professionals. any of the FIVE succeeding years you declare income NOT in

An owner-manager chooses how to extract money from their own business, and the choice has a large tax consequence.     Shares exercised                            5,000

    which creates a real cash problem: you owe tax on paper
    gains before you have sold anything.

    VERIFY current eligibility and the deferral period.

  Capital gain = Sale consideration
                 MINUS FMV already taxed as perquisite

    Sale consideration                     Rs 45,00,000

  1. Exercising early in a high-FMV year and owing lakhs in

  3. Forgetting that the employer deducts TDS at exercise,

     than expected.
  4. Not getting a fresh valuation, so the FMV used is
     indefensible.

Section 195 — the overseas payment trap

Paying an overseas vendor without considering Section 195 is a frequent and expensive errorSection 195 requires deduction of tax at source on any sum payable to a non-resident that is chargeable to tax in India — with no threshold. Whether a particular payment is chargeable depends on the Income-tax Act, the relevant Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement, and the concepts of business connection, permanent establishment, royalty and fees for technical services. Payments for standard off-the-shelf software have been litigated extensively. Before remitting, your banker will require Form 15CA (your declaration) and, in most cases, Form 15CB (a Chartered Accountant's certificate) certifying the taxability and the rate. Budget for the 15CB fee and build it into your overseas procurement process. If you under-deduct, the demand lands on you, grossed up.

Statutory registers — the ongoing obligation

Maintaining registers is not a one-time act at incorporation. The Register of Members must be updated on every transfer; the Register of Directors on every change; MBP-4 on every contract in which a director is interested; and the Register of Charges on every secured borrowing. These registers are the first thing a due-diligence lawyer asks for, and reconstructing three years of them under time pressure is a standard and expensive startup experience.

The Labour Codes' redefinition of "wages"

This is the change that will alter your payroll cost — verify the position for your stateThe Code on Wages, 2019 introduces a single, uniform definition of "wages" across PF, gratuity, bonus and other social security computations. The definition includes basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance, and excludes a specified list of allowances — but with a critical proviso: if the excluded allowances exceed a prescribed percentage (commonly stated as 50%) of total remuneration, the excess is added back to wages. The practical effect is that employers can no longer keep "basic" at 20–30% of CTC and load the rest into allowances to minimise PF and gratuity. Confirm the exact proviso, the percentage, and your state's rules before restructuring any salary.

Digital records and electronic maintenance

  • Electronic maintenance of books is permitted under the Companies (Accounts) Rules 2014, provided the records remain accessible in India at all times, are retained completely and unaltered, are capable of being displayed in legible form, and a proper system for storage, retrieval and display exists
  • A backup of the books maintained in electronic mode must be kept on servers physically located in India, and the company must intimate prescribed particulars to the ROC annually with AOC-4
  • Audit trail (edit log) functionality in accounting software is mandatory for companies, with the feature enabled throughout the year and not disabled — verify the current applicability date and scope on mca.gov.in; this has been deferred more than once
  • GST records may be maintained electronically; each entry must be authenticated by digital signature and no entry may be erased — incorrect entries must be scored out and corrected, with a log of every edit
  • Invoices may be issued electronically; a digital signature is not required where issued through a system complying with the Information Technology Act

How to respond to a notice — the standard method

  1. Read it the day it arrivesIdentify the statute, the section, the assessment year or tax period, the exact issue, and the response deadline. Put the deadline in the calendar immediately.
  2. Verify it is genuineUse the "Authenticate Notice/Order issued by ITD" utility on incometax.gov.in or check the reference number on gst.gov.in. There are phishing emails that mimic tax notices very well.
  3. Build the reconciliation before you write a wordMost notices are mismatches. Produce a table reconciling the department's figure to yours, line by line, with the reason for each difference.
  4. Decide the position with your CAThree outcomes: we are right and can prove it; we are wrong and should pay; it is arguable and we need to weigh the cost.
  5. Write a short, factual replyState the issue, state your position, attach the reconciliation and the supporting documents as a single indexed PDF. Do not argue emotionally and do not volunteer unrelated information.
  6. File within the deadline, through the portalKeep the acknowledgement. If you need more time, request an adjournment before the deadline, with a reason.
  7. If you lose and the amount is small, payA ₹60,000 demand contested through two levels of appeal costs more in fees and time than it saves, even if you win.
  8. If the amount is large or the principle recurs, appealA characterisation issue that will repeat every year — intermediary status, a classification dispute, a recurring disallowance — is worth fighting once.

Frequently Asked Questions

Specific answers to the compliance questions Indian technology founders ask most. Every answer is stated as at FY 2025-26 and should be confirmed against the official source named before you act on it.

I am a freelance developer with only US clients. Do I need to register for GST at all?
Strictly, registration is required under Section 22 once aggregate turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh (₹2,000,000) for services, and exports count towards aggregate turnover — export of services is a zero-rated supply, not an exempt one. Separately, if you pay any overseas vendor for services — a US cloud bill, an offshore contractor, foreign tooling — you are liable under reverse charge, and Section 24(iii) then makes registration compulsory regardless of turnover. In practice most services exporters register voluntarily even below the threshold, because it lets you file an LUT in Form RFD-11 and export without IGST, claim refund of input tax credit on your Indian expenses, and give your bank a clean documentary trail for FIRC and e-BRC. The cost is twenty-four returns a year. Verify your exact position with a CA and on gst.gov.in.
What happens if I forget to file the LUT in April?
You lose the right to export without paying IGST for the period until you file it. Every export invoice raised in the gap must carry IGST, which you pay out of cash and then reclaim as a refund under Rule 96 by filing RFD-01 with FIRC or BRC evidence. On ₹50 lakh of quarterly exports that is ₹9 lakh of working capital blocked for the weeks or months the refund takes. The LUT is valid for exactly one financial year, does not auto-renew, takes about ten minutes on gst.gov.in, needs two witnesses' details and a DSC or EVC, and costs nothing. Put "File LUT" in the calendar for the first working day of April, every year, permanently. If you have already missed it, file immediately — the LUT applies prospectively from filing, so the longer you wait the more invoices are caught.
I paid a freelancer ₹8 lakh last year and did not deduct TDS. How bad is it?
Expensive but recoverable. Under Section 194J you should have deducted 10%, i.e. ₹80,000. The consequences are: 30% of the expense disallowed under Section 40(a)(ia), i.e. ₹2.4 lakh added back to your income (a tax cost of about ₹60,000 at the 25.17% company rate); you are an assessee-in-default for the ₹80,000 under Section 201(1); interest at 1% per month from the date it was deductible under Section 201(1A); a late-filing fee of ₹200 per day for the unfiled 26Q under Section 234E, capped at the TDS amount; and a possible penalty under Section 271C. The mitigation: if the freelancer has already included the income in their return and paid tax on it, you can obtain a certificate from their CA in Form 26A, which relieves you of the Section 201(1) demand and restores the Section 40(a)(ia) deduction. Chase that certificate now. Then fix the process: no payment leaves without a TDS check.
Section 44AD or 44ADA — which applies to a software developer?
This matters enormously, because 44ADA deems 50% of receipts to be income while 44AD deems only 6% (for digital receipts). Section 44ADA applies to professions listed in Section 44AA(1), which includes "technical consultancy" and notified information technology professionals. A consultant providing bespoke technical advisory and development work is commonly treated as covered by 44ADA. A business that resells licences, operates a SaaS subscription product, or runs a development agency with salaried staff looks far more like a business under 44AD, where the gross receipts limit is ₹2 crore (₹3 crore with ≤5% cash) rather than ₹50 lakh (₹75 lakh with ≤5% cash). There is genuine ambiguity and the characterisation has been litigated. Take a written view from your CA for your facts, document the reasoning, and be consistent year on year. Also remember 44AD carries a five-year lock-in on opting out; 44ADA does not.
My client deducted TDS but it is not showing in my Form 26AS. What do I do?
You cannot claim credit for TDS that is not reflected in Form 26AS or the AIS, because the credit is granted from the department's own records. The usual causes are: the client deducted but has not yet filed the quarterly return; they filed but entered your PAN incorrectly; or they deducted and did not deposit. Steps: ask the client for the TDS certificate (Form 16A) downloaded from TRACES, which proves they filed and in what quarter; if the PAN is wrong, ask them to file a correction statement on TRACES — this is routine and takes them a few days; if they deducted and did not deposit, you have a commercial problem, because the department will pursue them but will not give you credit until it is deposited. Do not file your return claiming credit that is not in 26AS; you will receive a Section 143(1) adjustment. If the timing is tight, file on time without the credit and file a rectification under Section 154 once it appears.
We are a two-person company with no revenue yet. What do we still have to file?
More than you expect, and the fact of zero revenue reduces almost none of it. You must: hold board meetings (two a year if you are a small company, four otherwise); hold an AGM within six months of the financial year end; get a statutory audit done — mandatory for every company at any turnover; file AOC-4 within 30 days of the AGM and MGT-7A within 60 days; file ADT-1 for the auditor; file DIR-3 KYC for every director by 30 September; file DPT-3 by 30 June (director loans and share application money are reportable even if not deposits); file a nil income tax return in ITR-6; file nil GST returns every month if registered; and file INC-20A if you are within 180 days of incorporation. Realistic annual cost: ₹45,000 to ₹80,000. If the company will stay dormant, consider applying for dormant status in Form MSC-1 under Section 455, which reduces this materially.
Do I really have to pay GST under reverse charge on my AWS and SaaS bills?
If the invoice is issued by a foreign entity with no Indian GSTIN, it is an import of services and you are liable under reverse charge at 18%, payable in cash — you cannot use your input credit balance for it. You must also issue a self-invoice under Section 31(3)(f) and a payment voucher under Rule 52. You then claim the tax back as input tax credit if the service is an eligible business input, which cloud and tooling normally are. Check each vendor's invoice carefully: many large providers now bill Indian customers from an Indian entity with an Indian GSTIN, in which case it is ordinary forward charge and no RCM arises. The ones that still bill from offshore are the ones to watch. For a pre-revenue startup with heavy overseas tooling spend, this creates real cash outflow against credit you may not be able to use — model it before signing a large contract. Verify the current reverse-charge notification on gst.gov.in.
Should I pay myself salary or dividend from my own company?
Salary, in almost every case, because it is deductible to the company so the money is taxed once, whereas dividend is paid from post-tax profit and taxed again in your hands — an effective combined burden that can approach 43% at higher slabs versus about 26% for salary. Three constraints. First, the salary must be commercially justifiable for the work actually done; Section 40A(2)(b) lets the officer disallow excessive payments to related parties. Second, salary attracts PF if the company is covered, professional tax, and gratuity accrual. Third, if you want to retain profit inside the company for growth, paying it all out as salary defeats that. A blended structure — salary plus market-rate rent if you own the premises plus a modest dividend — is often optimal, and rent carries a 30% standard deduction under the house property head. Model it with your CA against both the old and new personal regimes.
What is the difference between a tax audit and a statutory audit, and do I need both?
They are different statutes with different triggers. A statutory audit is required by Sections 139–148 of the Companies Act for every company, every year, from the first financial year, regardless of turnover, profit or activity. A tax audit is required by Section 44AB of the Income-tax Act only when turnover exceeds ₹1 crore for a business (raised to ₹10 crore where cash receipts and cash payments are each ≤5% of the total — which is true of almost every software business) or ₹50 lakh for a profession (₹75 lakh under the same cash condition). So: a company with ₹5 lakh of revenue needs a statutory audit and no tax audit. A proprietorship with ₹3 crore of digital-only turnover needs neither, because the ₹10 crore relaxation applies and there is no Companies Act. A company with ₹12 crore of turnover needs both — and the tax audit report is then filed in Form 3CA plus 3CD rather than 3CB plus 3CD.
I received an ASMT-10 notice about a GSTR-3B versus GSTR-2B mismatch. How worried should I be?
Not very, if you respond properly and on time. ASMT-10 is a scrutiny notice under Section 61 pointing out a discrepancy; it is not a demand. The usual cause is that you claimed input tax credit on invoices that had not appeared in your GSTR-2B at the time, because a vendor filed late. Build a clear reconciliation table: for each disputed invoice, show the vendor GSTIN, invoice number and date, the amount, the month you claimed it, and the month it subsequently appeared in your 2B. Attach the invoices. File your reply in ASMT-11 within 30 days. If the officer accepts it, you receive ASMT-12 and the matter closes. If you ignore it, proceedings escalate to a show-cause notice under Section 73 or, worse, Section 74 with a 100% penalty. The real lesson is procedural: claim only what is in 2B in the month it is in 2B, and carry the rest forward with a tracking list.
How much should I budget for compliance in year one?
For a proprietorship with GST registration and presumptive taxation: ₹12,000 to ₹45,000 a year — GST returns, an ITR, light bookkeeping, no audit. For an LLP below the audit thresholds: ₹25,000 to ₹70,000 — add Form 8 and Form 11 and a heavier bookkeeping requirement. For a Private Limited Company: ₹55,000 to ₹1,50,000, dominated by the mandatory statutory audit (₹20,000–₹60,000) and the six annual ROC forms. Once you hire, add payroll processing at roughly ₹150–₹400 per employee per month plus PF, ESIC and professional tax filings. Once you have any overseas group entity, add a transfer pricing study and Form 3CEB at ₹50,000 to ₹2.5 lakh. These are metro rates; tier-2 cities are often 40–60% of them. Agree scope and fees in writing before engaging anyone.
My company has outstanding payments to a small vendor beyond 45 days. What is the actual consequence?
Two separate consequences, both real. First, under Section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act, a payment due to a registered micro or small enterprise is deductible only in the year of actual payment, unless paid within the time limit under Section 15 of the MSMED Act 2006 — 45 days where there is a written agreement, 15 days where there is not. So the expense is disallowed in the current year and allowed only when you pay, which can push a loss-making year into a taxable one. Second, under Section 405 of the Companies Act you must file Form MSME-1 half-yearly, by 30 April and 31 October, disclosing all amounts outstanding to micro and small enterprises for more than 45 days, with reasons. The vendor can also file on MSME Samadhaan and recover compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate. Collect Udyam numbers from every vendor at onboarding and flag micro and small suppliers in your payables ageing.
Can I claim input tax credit on my team's lunch, employee health insurance and the company car?
Generally no, on all three. Section 17(5) blocks credit on food and beverages and outdoor catering under clause (b)(i), on motor vehicles for transport of persons with seating capacity up to 13 under clause (a) and on their insurance, servicing and maintenance under clause (ab), and on club and fitness memberships under clause (b)(ii). There is one meaningful exception: where the employer is obligated under any law for the time being in force to provide the benefit to employees, credit is available — but this exception is narrow and should not be assumed without a written view. Health insurance for employees was blocked for most of GST's life; the position where it is legally mandated has been argued. Confirm the current text of Section 17(5) and the applicable circulars with your CA. What you can claim: cloud and hosting, Indian-billed software subscriptions, commercial office rent, laptops and hardware, professional fees, recruitment fees, Indian-billed advertising, telecom and internet.
What single compliance failure causes the most damage to small Indian companies?
Not filing ROC annual returns. Missing AOC-4 and MGT-7 accrues additional fees at ₹100 per day per form with no upper cap — roughly ₹73,000 per form over two years — and at three continuous financial years of non-filing, Section 164(2)(a) disqualifies every director for five years across all companies, deactivating their DIN. Founders discover this when they try to incorporate their next venture and cannot. The second most damaging is TDS: failure to deduct disallows 30% of the expense under Section 40(a)(ia) (100% for non-residents under Section 40(a)(i)) and, where tax was deducted but not deposited, Section 276B carries rigorous imprisonment from three months to seven years. The third is losing input tax credit to the Section 16(4) deadline, which is pure, unrecoverable cash. All three are calendar problems, not knowledge problems.
The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from April 2026. What should I actually do about it?
Four practical things. One: ask your CA, in writing, for a mapping of the provisions you rely on — 44ADA, 115BAA, 194J, 40(a)(ia), 43B(h), 80JJAA — to their equivalents in the new Act, and keep it with your tax file. Two: understand the "tax year" concept, which replaces the previous-year and assessment-year pair with a single unified period; this changes how returns, due dates and notices are referenced, and will affect your compliance calendar labels and your accounting software's tax configuration. Three: re-verify any irrevocable election you have made or plan to make, particularly Section 115BAA, and how it carries over. Four: do not assume continuity of thresholds, rates or forms; re-check every number in your tax model against the new Act and the first Finance Act under it before you file. Authoritative source: incometax.gov.in. Treat everything in this guide that cites a 1961 Act section as needing that mapping.

Monthly closing checklist

Books and bank

  • Bank statements downloaded for every account and reconciled to the ledger, with every unreconciled item explained
  • All sales invoices for the month recorded; invoice series verified as consecutive with no gaps; any cancelled numbers recorded as cancelled
  • All purchase bills recorded and filed in the shared drive with the standard naming convention
  • Petty cash counted and reconciled; cash payments confirmed to be within Section 40A(3) limits
  • Credit card and expense claims reconciled and approved
  • Foreign currency transactions recorded at the correct rate with the basis documented
  • Prepaid expenses, accruals and provisions passed
  • Depreciation entry passed for the month
  • Inter-company and director-account balances reviewed and agreed

GST

  • Output tax computed and agreed to the sales register, split correctly between CGST+SGST and IGST by place of supply
  • Zero-rated export invoices tagged, with the LUT reference quoted on each
  • Reverse charge liability identified on every overseas vendor invoice; self-invoices and payment vouchers issued
  • GSTR-2B downloaded and reconciled line by line to the purchase register
  • Vendors missing from 2B listed and chased, with payment withheld where the contract permits
  • Blocked credits under Section 17(5) identified and excluded
  • Rule 42/43 reversal computed where there are exempt supplies
  • 180-day ageing reviewed on unpaid purchase invoices on which ITC was claimed
  • Invoices approaching the Section 16(4) deadline flagged and escalated
  • GSTR-1 filed by the 11th; GSTR-3B filed and paid by the 20th; RCM paid in cash

TDS and payroll

  • Every vendor payment reviewed for the applicable TDS section and rate before release
  • PAN collected and validated for every payee; Section 206AA higher rate applied where PAN is absent
  • Section 195 and Form 15CA/15CB considered for every overseas remittance
  • TDS deposited by the 7th with correct section codes and assessment year on the challan
  • Payroll processed; payslips issued; Section 192 TDS computed on the annual estimate
  • EPF ECR uploaded and challan paid by the 15th; exit dates marked for leavers; UAN created for joiners
  • ESIC contribution paid by the 15th
  • Professional tax deducted, deposited and returned per state requirement

Review and governance

  • Management P&L and cash flow reviewed against budget
  • Receivables ageing reviewed; anything beyond 45 days escalated
  • Payables ageing reviewed; MSME vendors beyond 45 days flagged for Section 43B(h) and MSME-1
  • Advance tax position reviewed against year-to-date profit, annualised
  • Compliance calendar reviewed for the next 60 days; owners confirmed
  • Notices inbox checked; every open proceeding has a tracked next date
  • Board meeting held and minuted if due; statutory registers updated for any change
  • Any share allotment, director change, address change or charge creation filed within its 15- or 30-day window
