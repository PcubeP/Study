Why an Engineer Must Be Able to Read a Contract

You will sign more contracts in your career than you write lines of production code that survive five years. An employment agreement, an ESOP grant, an NDA before every pitch, a consulting MSA, a SaaS order form, a sub-contractor agreement, a founders' agreement. Each one allocates risk and ownership, and almost all of the pain comes from the same five clauses. You do not need a law degree. You need to be able to find those five clauses in forty minutes, know what "market" looks like, and know when to stop and call an advocate.

This is educational material, not legal adviceIndian contract, intellectual property, employment, data protection and arbitration law is statutory, fact-specific and evolves through amendment and judicial decision. Nothing here is advice on your facts or your documents. Before you sign, assign IP, agree an indemnity, file a trade mark or patent, terminate a contract or respond to a legal notice, engage a qualified advocate — and for anything touching tax, a Chartered Accountant. Every fee, statutory limit, time period, form number and procedural step in this guide is stated as at early 2026 and must be re-verified at the official source before you rely on it.
Four things are in flight — verify current status (1) The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the Income-tax Act, 1961 from 1 April 2026, introducing a unified "tax year" concept. Any tax section cited in a contract (TDS under Section 194J, withholding gross-ups, Section 40(a)(ia) clauses) will need renumbering. Cross-check the new section map at incometax.gov.in. (2) The GST rate rationalisation of September 2025 restructured slabs to principally 5% and 18% with a 40% demerit rate. Contracts with hard-coded GST rates need review. Confirm your SAC rate at gst.gov.in. (3) The DPDP Act 2023 Rules were notified in late 2025 with phased compliance deadlines. Data processing addenda written before that need updating. Confirm which phase binds you at meity.gov.in. (4) Arbitration law reform. A substantial overhaul of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 has been under active consideration. Before drafting an arbitration clause, confirm the provisions in force at legislative.gov.in and indiacode.nic.in.

Three habits that prevent most contract disasters

  1. Never sign the same day you receive it.Nothing commercially sensible is lost by taking 48 hours. Urgency on the other side is a negotiating technique far more often than it is a real constraint.
  2. Read the definitions before the clauses.Most dangerous drafting hides in a definition. "Deliverables means all work product, materials, inventions and developments created, conceived or reduced to practice by the Supplier during the Term" is an IP assignment clause wearing a definition's clothing.
  3. Write down your three walk-away points before you negotiate.If you decide in the moment, you will concede in the moment. Deciding in advance converts a negotiation into an execution.

Indian Contract Act 1872 — Fundamentals

The Indian Contract Act 1872 is short, old, and still the backbone of every commercial agreement governed by Indian law. You need about fifteen sections of it. The two that come up most often in a technology practice are Section 27, which makes restraints of trade void and therefore kills post-employment non-competes, and Section 74, which converts penalty clauses into claims for reasonable compensation.

Section references below are to the Indian Contract Act 1872 as in force in early 2026Read the current text at indiacode.nic.in. Judicial interpretation matters as much as the text; the cases mentioned are illustrative of settled positions, not a substitute for an advocate's advice on your facts.

What makes an agreement a contract

Section 10: "All agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not hereby expressly declared to be void."

ElementSectionThe ruleHow it fails in practice
Offer and acceptance2(a), 2(b), 3–9A proposal communicated, and acceptance that is absolute and unqualified, communicated in a prescribed or usual manner. A counter-offer rejects the original offer."Looks good, just change the payment terms to net 30" is a counter-offer, not an acceptance. There is no contract until the counter is accepted.
Consideration2(d), 23, 25Something given, done or promised at the promisor's desire. An agreement without consideration is void, with narrow exceptions in Section 25 (written and registered promise made out of natural love and affection; compensation for past voluntary service; written promise to pay a time-barred debt).A "letter of intent" with no consideration flowing either way is usually not enforceable as a contract. Free pilots and unpaid proofs of concept frequently fall here.
Capacity11, 12Must be of the age of majority (18 under the Indian Majority Act 1875), of sound mind, and not disqualified by law. A minor's agreement is void ab initio.A 17-year-old co-founder cannot validly sign. For companies, capacity turns on the memorandum, the board resolution and the authorised signatory — see the next section.
Free consent13–22Consent is free when not caused by coercion (15), undue influence (16), fraud (17), misrepresentation (18) or mistake (20–22). Consent caused by the first four makes the contract voidable at the option of the aggrieved party; bilateral mistake of fact as to a matter essential to the agreement makes it void.Material misstatements in a due-diligence questionnaire, or an inflated capability claim in a proposal relied upon by the client, can amount to misrepresentation.
Lawful object and consideration23, 24Unlawful if forbidden by law, defeats the provisions of any law, is fraudulent, involves injury to person or property, or is immoral or opposed to public policy.An agreement to circumvent exchange control, to pay an undisclosed commission to a government official, or to share fees in a manner a professional regulator forbids.
Certainty29Agreements the meaning of which is not certain, or capable of being made certain, are void."Fees to be mutually agreed later" is an agreement to agree and is unenforceable. Put a mechanism in, not a hope.

Void versus voidable — the distinction that decides your remedy

Void agreement

Section 2(g): not enforceable by law, from the outset. A minor's contract, an agreement in restraint of trade, an agreement with an unlawful object, an uncertain agreement. No rights arise for anyone. Section 65 may require restoration of any advantage received.

Voidable contract

Section 2(i): enforceable at the option of one party. Induced by coercion, undue influence, fraud or misrepresentation. The aggrieved party may affirm or rescind. If they affirm, or delay unreasonably, the right to rescind can be lost.

Void ab initio vs becomes void

A contract can be valid when made and become void later — Section 56, impossibility or unlawfulness arising after formation (frustration). The commercial consequence differs: work done before frustration is treated differently from a contract that never existed.

Section 27 — restraint of trade, and what it means for non-competes

TEXT
SECTION 27, INDIAN CONTRACT ACT 1872

"Every agreement by which any one is restrained from
exercising a lawful profession, trade or business of any
kind, is to that extent void.

EXCEPTION 1 - SAVING OF AGREEMENT NOT TO CARRY ON BUSINESS
OF WHICH GOODWILL IS SOLD. One who sells the goodwill of a

on a similar business, within specified local limits, so

goodwill from him, carries on a like business therein,

regard being had to the nature of the business."

WHAT THIS MEANS IN PRACTICE
--------------------------------------------------------------
  VOID (post-employment / post-engagement restraints)
    "For 24 months after termination you shall not work for
     any competitor of the Company."
    "You shall not provide services to any company in the
     fintech sector for 12 months after this Agreement ends."
    >>> Indian courts have consistently declined to enforce
        restraints that operate AFTER the relationship ends,
        regardless of how reasonable they look, because
        Section 27 contains no "reasonableness" test for
        them - unlike English law.

  ENFORCEABLE (restraints DURING the relationship)
    "During your employment you shall devote your whole time
     and attention to the Company and shall not engage in any
     other business."
    "During the Term the Consultant shall not provide
     services to the named competitors in Schedule 3."
    >>> A negative covenant operating during the subsistence
        of the contract is treated as a legitimate term of
        service, not a restraint of trade.

  ENFORCEABLE (sale of goodwill, within Exception 1)
    Founders selling a business can be restrained from
    competing, within reasonable local limits, for as long
    as the buyer carries on a like business.

  PARTIALLY ENFORCEABLE
    Non-solicitation of CLIENTS and EMPLOYEES - Indian courts
    have granted relief where the restraint is narrow,
    limited in time, connected to confidential information
    or to clients the person actually dealt with, and does
    not amount to a general bar on working. Outcomes are
    fact-specific.

  ALWAYS ENFORCEABLE
    Confidentiality obligations. These do not restrain the
    exercise of a profession; they restrain the use of the
    other party's information. This is why a well-drafted
    confidentiality clause is worth more to a client than a
    non-compete they cannot enforce.

Section 27 does not protect you if the contract is governed by another lawA consulting agreement with a US client governed by the law of Delaware or California, with a Delaware forum, will be analysed under that law — and some US states enforce reasonable non-competes. California does not; many others do. The Indian position gives you no comfort in a foreign forum. If you cannot get the non-compete removed, get the governing law and seat changed, or narrow the restraint to named competitors for a short period.

Section 28 — restraint of legal proceedings

Section 28 voids agreements that absolutely restrict a party from enforcing their rights through ordinary legal proceedings, or that limit the time within which rights may be enforced, or that extinguish rights or discharge liability on the expiry of a specified period. Two exceptions preserve arbitration agreements. A further exception, introduced by amendment, preserves certain provisions in bank guarantees and similar instruments. Verify the current text at indiacode.nic.in.

ClauseLikely position under Section 28
"Any dispute shall be referred to arbitration"Valid — expressly saved
"No claim shall be brought more than 6 months after the event giving rise to it"Problematic. A clause that curtails the limitation period, or extinguishes the right after a short period, engages Section 28. Clients frequently insert these; they may not survive under Indian law.
"The Supplier waives all rights to bring any claim"Void as an absolute restraint
"Claims shall be brought exclusively in the courts of Mumbai"Generally valid as an exclusive jurisdiction clause between two courts that would otherwise both have jurisdiction — it channels proceedings rather than barring them
"The parties shall first attempt mediation for 30 days before commencing proceedings"Generally valid as a procedural pre-condition, not an absolute bar

Section 74 — penalty versus liquidated damages

This is one of the sharpest divergences between Indian law and English law, and it matters every time you see a clause fixing a sum payable on breach — a service-level credit, a delay penalty, a termination fee.

TEXT
SECTION 74, INDIAN CONTRACT ACT 1872 (summarised)

"When a contract has been broken, if a sum is named in the
contract as the amount to be paid in case of such breach, or
if the contract contains any other stipulation by way of
penalty, the party complaining of the breach is entitled,
whether or not actual damage or loss is proved to have been
caused thereby, to receive from the party who has broken the
contract REASONABLE COMPENSATION NOT EXCEEDING THE AMOUNT SO
NAMED or, as the case may be, the penalty stipulated for."

THE PRACTICAL EFFECT
  1. Indian law does NOT distinguish between an enforceable
     "liquidated damages" clause and an unenforceable
     "penalty" clause the way English law does. Both are
     treated under Section 74.
  2. The named sum operates as a CEILING, not an
     entitlement. You cannot recover more than the figure
     named, however large your actual loss.
  3. The claimant must still establish that loss was
     suffered, and the court awards REASONABLE compensation,
     which may be far less than the named sum.
     - Fateh Chand v Balkishan Das (1963) established the
       ceiling principle.
     - ONGC v Saw Pipes (2003) allowed recovery of the named
       sum where it was a genuine pre-estimate and loss was
       difficult to prove.
     - Kailash Nath Associates v DDA (2015) reaffirmed that
       compensation is payable only where loss is shown,
       except where loss is impossible to prove.

WHAT THIS MEANS WHEN YOU DRAFT
  - If you are the party PROTECTED by the clause, draft it
    as a genuine pre-estimate of loss, record in a recital
    WHY loss would be difficult to quantify, and keep
    evidence of the estimate. "The parties acknowledge that
    loss arising from a Service Level failure is difficult
    to quantify and that the Service Credits represent a
    genuine pre-estimate of such loss."
  - If you are the party EXPOSED, remember the sum is a
    ceiling and the other side must still show reasonable
    compensation. A ₹50 lakh "penalty" is not automatically
    a ₹50 lakh liability.
  - A clause stating that service credits are the SOLE AND
    EXCLUSIVE REMEDY converts a floor into a cap in your
    favour. That is usually the more valuable drafting.

The Specific Relief (Amendment) Act 2018

Before 2018, specific performance in India was a discretionary remedy granted only where damages were inadequate. The 2018 amendment changed the architecture, which matters for technology contracts where damages are often a poor substitute for the thing itself — a source-code escrow release, a data export on termination, an IP assignment the counterparty refuses to execute.

ProvisionChangeWhy it matters to you
Section 10Specific performance shall be enforced, subject to Sections 11(2), 14 and 16 — it is no longer purely discretionaryA refusal to execute an assignment, hand over data, or transfer a domain is more realistically enforceable
Section 14Lists contracts not specifically enforceable, including those involving continuous supervision by the court and contracts dependent on personal qualificationsA court will not order a consultant to keep consulting. Specific performance is for transfers and deliverables, not for compelling ongoing services.
Section 20Introduced substituted performance — the aggrieved party may get the contract performed by a third party or its own agency and recover the cost from the defaulter, after giving at least 30 days' written noticeIf a vendor abandons a project, you can engage a replacement and claim the cost — provided you serve the notice. Put a reference to substituted performance in your termination clause.
Section 20ABars injunctions that would delay the progress or completion of a notified infrastructure projectRelevant if you contract into infrastructure programmes
Section 20B / 20CSpecial courts; suits under the Act to be disposed of within twelve months from service of summons, extendable by six monthsA statutory timeline, which Indian commercial litigation badly needed. Performance against it varies.
Section 14ACourt may appoint experts to assist on technical issuesUseful in software disputes where the judge needs help on what the code does
Drafting consequenceBecause specific performance is now the rule rather than the exception, it is worth stating expressly in your contract that damages would be an inadequate remedy for breach of the confidentiality, IP assignment and data-return obligations, and that the aggrieved party is entitled to seek injunctive relief and specific performance without proving special damage. It does not bind a court, but it removes an argument.

Stamp duty

Stamp duty is a state subject for most instruments. The Indian Stamp Act 1899 applies at the Union level and in some states; most states have their own Stamp Act with their own Schedule of rates. An agreement that is not duly stamped is not void, but it is inadmissible in evidence under Section 35 of the Indian Stamp Act — which means you cannot rely on it in court until the defect is cured by paying the duty plus a penalty, which can be a multiple of the duty.

ARBITRATION AGREEMENTS AND STAMPING
  A seven-judge bench of the Supreme Court held in
  "In Re: Interplay Between Arbitration Agreements under

Counterparts, version control and the signed-copy problem

  • Include a counterparts clause: "This Agreement may be executed in any number of counterparts, including by electronic signature or by exchange of signed PDF copies, each of which is an original and all of which together constitute one agreement." Without it, a purist can argue that a contract signed on two separate documents is not one instrument.
  • Use a document control footer on every page: document name, version, date, and page X of Y. Disputes about which version was signed are depressingly common.
  • Circulate negotiation drafts as Word with tracked changes and the final as PDF. Never send an untracked redraft; it is read as an attempt to slip something past.
  • Name files unambiguously: Northwind-MSA-v4-RaoSystems-markup-2026-02-18.docx. Never "final", "final2", "final-FINAL".
  • Store the fully executed copy — with all signature pages and all schedules — in one PDF, in your contracts folder, the day it is signed. A contract you cannot find is a contract you do not have.
  • Keep the negotiation trail: the email in which each change was agreed. Under Section 92 of the Indian Evidence Act, extrinsic evidence is generally not admissible to contradict a written contract, but the trail is invaluable for commercial discussions and for proving what was and was not agreed on ambiguous points.
  • Where schedules, policies or URLs are incorporated by reference, take a dated copy. "The Supplier shall comply with the Customer's Security Policy available at customer.com/security" means you have agreed to a document the other side can change unilaterally. Attach the version you saw, or add "as at the Effective Date".

Definitions and interpretation

The definition trick you must learn to spotCompare these two definitions of the same term:
(A) "Deliverables means the items expressly identified as deliverables in the relevant Statement of Work."
(B) "Deliverables means all work product, materials, documentation, software, inventions, discoveries, improvements and developments conceived, created, authored, developed or reduced to practice by the Supplier, alone or with others, during the Term, whether or not in connection with the Services."
Definition (B), combined with a one-line clause saying "all Deliverables shall vest in the Customer", assigns your entire year of creative output — including your unrelated weekend project. Always read the definitions of "Deliverables", "Work Product", "Developments", "Confidential Information", "Affiliate", "Losses" and "Services" before anything else.

The NDA

You will sign more NDAs than any other document. Most are harmless, a few are badly drafted, and one or two will contain a clause that quietly gives away the thing the NDA is supposed to protect. Learn to read one in six minutes.

Mutual versus one-way

Mutual NDA

Both parties disclose and both receive. Obligations are symmetrical. This is appropriate for almost every commercial conversation — a sales call where you discuss your methodology and they discuss their architecture. Default to mutual and say so.

One-way NDA

Only one party discloses; only the other has obligations. Appropriate where genuinely one-sided — an investor receiving a data room, an employee receiving company information. If a client hands you a one-way NDA for a two-way conversation, ask for mutual. It costs them nothing and tells you how they negotiate.

The seven things to check in any NDA

#ElementWhat good looks likeRed flag
1Definition of Confidential InformationInformation disclosed in connection with the Purpose that is marked confidential, or that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential given its nature and the circumstances"All information disclosed, in any form, whether or not marked" with no reasonableness qualifier — this makes casual conversation confidential and is unworkable
2ExclusionsPublic domain through no breach; already lawfully known to the recipient; independently developed without use of the discloser's information; lawfully received from a third party without restriction; required to be disclosed by law or a competent authorityMissing exclusions. An NDA with no "independently developed" carve-out means you can be accused of breach for building something you would have built anyway.
3Purpose limitationA narrow, defined Purpose — "evaluating a potential engagement for retrieval system remediation"No purpose stated, or a purpose so broad it permits the discloser to argue any use was outside it
4Permitted disclosuresTo employees, contractors and professional advisers with a need to know, who are bound by equivalent obligations, with the recipient remaining responsible for their complianceNo permitted disclosure at all — you cannot then show the document to your own lawyer or accountant
5Term versus survivalA term for disclosures (e.g. 2 years) and a separate survival period for obligations (e.g. 3–5 years from disclosure, indefinite for trade secrets)"Perpetual" obligations over all information. Unbounded confidentiality for ordinary business information is unreasonable and, in practice, unmanageable.
6Residual knowledgeEither absent, or carefully boundedSee the warning below. A broad residuals clause can hollow out the entire NDA.
7Return or destructionOn written request, return or destroy, with an exception for one archival copy retained for legal or audit purposes and for copies in routine backups that are not readily accessibleAn absolute destruction obligation you cannot honestly comply with, because immutable backups exist
The residual knowledge clause — read this one twiceA typical residuals clause reads: "Notwithstanding anything to the contrary, the Receiving Party may use Residuals, where 'Residuals' means information retained in the unaided memory of individuals who have had access to Confidential Information." On its face that is reasonable — you cannot un-know things. In practice a broad residuals clause can gut the NDA, because almost anything can be characterised as retained in memory. If you are the discloser (you are showing a client your methodology), push to delete it or narrow it to exclude any information that is a trade secret, any specifically identified confidential information, and any use that would infringe IP rights. If you are the receiver, a residuals clause is genuinely useful protection against an accusation that your next engagement used their ideas. Know which side you are on before you negotiate it.

Intellectual Property Law in India for Software People

Four statutes and one gap. Copyright protects your code automatically. Patents protect inventions but specifically exclude computer programmes "per se". Trade marks protect your brand. Designs protect appearance. And trade secrets have no Indian statute at all — they are protected only by contract and by controls you put in place yourself.

All fees, timelines and procedural steps are as at early 2026Official fees, the Patent Rules, the Trade Marks Rules and the Copyright Rules are amended periodically. Verify every fee and timeline at ipindia.gov.in and copyright.gov.in before filing. Fee concessions for natural persons, startups, small entities and educational institutions are a significant discount and are subject to eligibility conditions and documentary proof.
Four regimes plus a statutory gap. Most software value sits in copyright and trade secrets.

IP in Contracts — The Single Most Important Commercial Issue

For a consultant, every rupee of long-term enterprise value is in what you keep. For a client, every rupee of value is in what they get. A good IP clause resolves that without either side feeling robbed: the client owns what they paid for, you keep the tools you brought with you, and the client gets a licence to the tools to the extent they are embedded in what you delivered.

Background IP versus Foreground IP

Background IP (yours)

Everything you owned or licensed before the engagement, plus anything you develop independently of it. Your evaluation harness library, your scaffolding scripts, your architecture review checklist, your prompt templates, your deployment patterns, your general methodology and know-how. You keep it. You grant a licence.

Foreground IP (theirs, on payment)

What you created specifically for this client under this engagement — their pipeline configuration, the code written against their schema, the report about their system, the model fine-tuned on their data. They own it, once they have paid.

If you do not define Background IP, you do not have anyA clause that assigns "all work product created during the engagement" with no background carve-out assigns your reusable library the first time you drop it into a client repository. The fix is two sentences plus a schedule: define Background IP, exclude it from the assignment, and list the specific items in a schedule attached to the agreement. Maintain that schedule as a living document. Add to it every time you build a reusable tool.

  unsettled in India and elsewhere - authorship under the
  Copyright Act 1957 contemplates a human author. If a

  meaningful human authorship, it may not attract copyright

  client does not get exclusivity. VERIFY the current

Employment bonds and training-cost recovery

An "employment bond" requiring an employee to serve a minimum period or repay a sum is common in Indian IT services. The position is nuanced.

  • A bond that is simply a penalty for leaving is likely to be read as a restraint and, in any event, is subject to Section 74 — the stated sum is a ceiling, and the employer must show reasonable compensation.
  • A bond that seeks genuine recovery of quantifiable training costs actually incurred by the employer — a specific certification programme, an overseas deputation, a paid course — is more likely to be enforced, to the extent of the proven cost, and typically on a reducing balance over the bond period.
  • A bond requiring deposit of original educational certificates is widely considered improper and has attracted judicial criticism.
  • A bond disproportionate to the training provided, or applied to ordinary on-the-job experience, is unlikely to survive.
  • If you are drafting one: state the actual cost incurred, attach evidence, provide pro-rata reduction over the service period, and keep the amount genuinely compensatory.
  • If you are signing one: get the actual training cost in writing, confirm pro-rata reduction, and confirm that no original documents will be retained.
Verify before acting on a bondThe enforceability of service bonds in India is fact-specific and the case law is not uniform. Take advice on the specific bond before you sign it or before you enforce it.

NOTE THE PHRASE "WHETHER DIRECT OR INDIRECT". Without it,
a court can find that loss of profit was a DIRECT loss on
the facts and therefore not caught by an exclusion of
"indirect loss including loss of profit". This is one of
the most consequential six words in commercial drafting.

"cost of restoring from backup" carve-back is a reasonable
and well-understood compromise.

Data Protection in Contracts

Every software contract now carries a data protection layer. In India that is the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and the Rules notified under it. If you serve EU customers, it is also the GDPR, which reaches you as a processor even though you are in Bengaluru. Both push a defined set of obligations into your contracts, and procurement teams will not sign without them.

The DPDP Rules are phasing in — verify which obligations bind you nowThe DPDP Act 2023 was enacted in August 2023 but its substantive obligations depend on the Rules, which were notified in late 2025 with phased commencement — different provisions taking effect at different intervals. Your actual compliance deadline depends on which phase applies. Check the current notified Rules and commencement dates at meity.gov.in before relying on any statement about what is or is not yet in force.

Seat versus venue — the distinction that decides everything

Seat

The legal home of the arbitration. It determines the procedural law governing the arbitration, which courts have supervisory jurisdiction (appointment of arbitrators, interim relief, challenges to the award), and whether the award is domestic or foreign for enforcement purposes. This is the one that matters.

Venue

The physical place where hearings happen. Purely practical. A Singapore-seated arbitration can hold all its hearings by video with participants in Bengaluru and San Francisco. Say so expressly, so nobody argues that the venue implicitly changed the seat.

Never write "the arbitration shall be held in Singapore" and leave it at thatAmbiguity about whether a reference to a place designates the seat or merely the venue has generated an enormous volume of Indian litigation. Use the words "the seat of the arbitration shall be X" and "the venue of hearings may be anywhere agreed, including by video conference, without affecting the seat". Two sentences; years of argument avoided.

Reading a Contract Fast

A repeatable thirty-minute method beats an unstructured three-hour read, because it is the same thirty minutes every time, which means you stop missing the same things. Here is the method, the redline conventions, and a risk-rating scheme you can apply consistently.

Same five passes, same order, every time. Consistency is what stops you missing things.

Pass 2 in detail — the questions to ask of each of the big five

ClauseThe six questions
IP(1) What exactly is assigned — read the definition, not the clause. (2) Is there a Background IP carve-out, and is there a schedule listing yours? (3) Is the assignment conditional on payment in full? (4) Is there a licence-back of your background IP, and is it limited to use as embedded in the deliverables? (5) Is there a residual knowledge / general skills reservation? (6) Are third-party and open-source components dealt with?
Limitation of liability(1) Is there a cap at all? (2) Is it aggregate or per claim? (3) What is the measure — 12 months' fees, total fees, a fixed sum, a multiple? (4) Is loss of profit, revenue, data and goodwill excluded whether direct or indirect? (5) What are the carve-outs, and do they swallow the cap? (6) Is it mutual?
Indemnity(1) Who indemnifies whom, and is it mutual? (2) What triggers it — IP infringement, confidentiality, data, breach of law, "any breach"? (3) Is it carved out of the cap or super-capped? (4) Are there carve-outs for client-supplied materials, modifications, combinations and open source, and do they say "to the extent"? (5) Who controls the defence and settlement? (6) Does it cover patents, and have you done any freedom-to-operate work?
Termination(1) Can either side terminate for convenience, and on what notice? (2) Is it mutual? (3) Do you get paid for work performed and non-cancellable commitments? (4) Is there a kill fee on fixed-price work? (5) What survives? (6) What are your obligations on exit — transition assistance, data return, and is it paid?
Governing law and disputes(1) Which law? (2) Courts or arbitration? (3) If arbitration: institution, seat, number of arbitrators, language, expedited procedure? (4) Can you afford to use it for a claim the size of this contract? (5) Is there a carve-out for urgent interim relief? (6) Is there an escalation or mandatory mediation step, and have you diarised the timelines?

A risk-rating scheme you can apply consistently

RatingDefinitionAction
REDCould cause loss materially exceeding the contract value, or is uninsurable, or removes a protection you rely on in every engagementMust change before signature. Escalate to an advocate if the counterparty refuses. Be prepared to walk away.
AMBERUnfavourable but survivable; increases cost, administrative burden or risk within tolerable boundsAsk for the change. Trade it away if you must. Record the decision.
GREENMarket standard, or favourable, or immaterialAccept without comment. Do not redline green items — it dilutes your red ones and makes you look inexperienced.
The single most effective negotiating disciplineRedline only the red and amber items, and never more than about eight points on a first pass. A document returned with ninety tracked changes signals that you have no priorities, triggers a full legal review on their side, and adds three weeks. A document returned with six precisely reasoned changes gets signed.

Redline conventions

  • Use tracked changes in Word. Never send an untracked redraft — it reads as an attempt to slip something through, and it is the fastest way to lose trust.
  • Add a comment explaining the reason for each change in one sentence. "Aligning the cap with our insurance limit" is persuasive. A silent deletion is not.
  • Propose replacement language, not just a deletion. "Delete clause 14.3" invites a fight; offering a drafted super-cap gives them something to accept.
  • Send a short covering email that lists your changes in order of importance, flags which two are essential, and explicitly says you are flexible on the rest. This frames the whole negotiation.
  • Keep every version. Name them Counterparty-MSA-v3-YourName-markup-2026-03-04.docx.
  • When you accept a change, accept it cleanly and say so, so the other side can see progress.
  • Produce a clean final with all changes accepted before signature, and read it once more in clean form. Tracked-change documents hide errors.

Negotiating Contracts Without a Lawyer in the Room

Most of your contract negotiations will be conducted by you, by email, against a counterparty who has a lawyer. That is survivable if you prepare properly, know your three walk-away points, and trade rather than concede.

Preparation

  1. Know your walk-away points before the first email.Write them down. For most consultants they are: a liability cap at or near contract value, no assignment of background IP, and payment for work performed on termination. Three is the right number — more than that and you cannot hold the line on any of them.
  2. Know your insurance limits.They are an objective, verifiable constraint and the single most persuasive argument available to a small supplier. "I cannot accept a cap above my cover" is a fact, not a position.
  3. Know what the deal is worth to you.If it is 60% of next year's revenue you will concede more than if it is 8%. Be honest with yourself about that before you start, because the other side will sense it either way.
  4. Know what the deal is worth to them.Who inside the client needs this done, and by when? A sponsor with a deadline is your strongest ally against their own legal team.
  5. Have your own template ready.If you move first with your paper, you negotiate from your positions. The party whose template is used wins roughly 70% of the marginal points by default.
  6. Decide your concession ladder.List, in advance, what you will give in exchange for each of your three essentials.

The "I can agree to that if…" formula

TEXT
NEVER SAY "NO". SAY "YES, IF".

"I can agree to [their ask] IF [your condition]."

WORKED EXAMPLES

Ask:  "We need a 3x fees liability cap."
Say:  "I can agree to a 3x cap IF it is aggregate across all
       SOWs and the carve-outs are limited to fraud, wilful
       misconduct and death or personal injury - so the cap
       is real rather than nominal."

Ask:  "We need you to indemnify us for IP infringement with
       no cap."
Say:  "I can agree to an uncapped IP indemnity IF it is
       limited to my original contributions, excludes
       anything arising from your specifications, your data,
       modifications by others, combinations, and
       open-source components, and excludes patents. If you
       need patents included, I'd need a 2x super-cap
       instead of uncapped."

Ask:  "Net 60 payment terms, it's our policy."
Say:  "I can work with net 60 IF we add a 4% premium to the
       rate to cover the financing cost, or IF there's a
       30% mobilisation payment up front. Either works."

Ask:  "We need you exclusive to us for the engagement."
Say:  "I can commit to 30 hours a week and to not working
       with the three competitors you name IF the engagement
       is a minimum of six months with 30 days' notice.
       Full exclusivity would make me an employee in
       substance, which creates classification risk for you
       as well as for me."

Ask:  "We own all IP including your tools."
Say:  "I can agree that you own everything built for you IF
       my pre-existing tools in Schedule 3 are carved out
       and you get a perpetual royalty-free licence to them
       as embedded in the deliverables. You get everything
       you actually need; I keep the ability to work."

Ask:  "Our MSA cannot be changed."
Say:  "Understood - I can sign the MSA as drafted IF we put
       the two changes I need into the SOW, with an express
       statement that the SOW varies clauses 10.2 and 9.1
       for this engagement. That way your template is
       untouched."

WHY IT WORKS
  - It never triggers a defensive reaction.
  - It makes every concession a TRADE, so you never give
    something away for free.
  - It puts the decision back on them.
  - It demonstrates that you understand their constraint,
    which buys goodwill for the point you actually care
    about.

Email etiquette for redlines

  • Keep emails short. A long email reads as anxiety.
  • Number your points and state how many there are up front: "Six points, two essential."
  • Give a reason for every change, framed as a problem you face, not a preference you hold.
  • Never use the words "unacceptable", "non-negotiable" or "deal-breaker" more than once in an entire negotiation. Save it.
  • Never cc more people than necessary. Escalating by audience is a tool; use it deliberately, not reflexively.
  • Reply within one business day even if the reply is "received, I'll come back to you Thursday". Silence is read as a problem.
  • Switch to a call after the second round of email. Two rounds of email is the point at which written exchange stops being efficient.
  • Confirm every call in writing within an hour, with what was agreed. "To confirm what we discussed: 1)… 2)… Let me know if I've misunderstood anything."
  • Be unfailingly pleasant. You are going to work with these people. The lawyer on the other side is doing their job, not attacking you.

Protecting Your Brand and Content

For a small software business the brand is often worth more than the code. It is also cheaper to protect and easier to lose. Three or four decisions made early — the right trade mark classes, the right domains, a clear copyright notice policy — prevent almost all the expensive problems.

Trade mark strategy for a small company

  1. Search before you name.Run the full search described in the IP section: Indian register across your classes and adjacent ones, company names on MCA, domains, common-law use, and foreign registers if you sell abroad. Four hours now, or a rebrand in year three.
  2. File the word mark first.A word mark protects the name in any font or styling. A device (logo) mark protects only that visual presentation. If you can afford one filing, file the word mark.
  3. Pick classes by what you actually sell, plus one.For a software business: Class 42 (SaaS, software design and development, IT consultancy) is almost always essential. Add Class 9 if you sell downloadable software. Add Class 35 if you sell business consulting or data processing for business. Add Class 41 if you sell training. Each class costs another filing fee.
  4. File as an individual, startup or small enterprise if eligible.The concessional fee is roughly half. You will need Udyam registration or DPIIT startup recognition as proof. Verify the current fees and eligibility at ipindia.gov.in.
  5. Use ™ from day one; use ® only after registration.Falsely representing a mark as registered is an offence under Section 107 of the Trade Marks Act 1999.
  6. Keep evidence of use.Dated invoices, website archives, marketing materials, press mentions. You need this to defend against a non-use rectification and to establish goodwill for a passing-off claim.
  7. Watch the Journal.Set a reminder to search for confusingly similar marks quarterly. The opposition window is four months from advertisement and it does not reopen.
  8. Diarise renewal.Ten years feels far away. Put it in a calendar that will still exist, with a reminder twelve months before.
  9. File abroad where you actually sell.The Madrid Protocol allows a single international application designating multiple countries, based on your Indian application or registration. Economical if you need three or more countries.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a post-employment non-compete enforceable in India?
Generally no. Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act 1872 provides that every agreement restraining a person from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business is void to that extent, and Indian courts have consistently declined to enforce restraints operating after the employment ends. The only statutory exception is Exception 1, which saves a restraint agreed on the sale of goodwill, within reasonable local limits. Two important qualifications. First, a restraint operating during employment — an exclusivity or "whole time and attention" clause — is enforceable as a legitimate term of service. Second, if your contract is governed by another country's law, Section 27 gives you nothing; some US states, for example, enforce reasonable non-competes. What remains enforceable in India post-employment is confidentiality, and a narrow, reasonable non-solicit may be partially enforced on the facts. Take advice on your specific clause and governing law.
My client paid me to build software. Do they own the copyright automatically?
No, and this surprises most people. Under Section 17 of the Copyright Act 1957, the author is the first owner, subject to provisos. Proviso (c) vests ownership in the employer where the work is made in the course of employment under a contract of service — that is, an employee. A consultant works under a contract for services, which is different. Proviso (b), which vests ownership in a person who commissions a work for valuable consideration, is limited by its terms to photographs, paintings, portraits, engravings and cinematograph films, and does not extend to software. So absent a written assignment the consultant is the first owner of copyright in commissioned software. Section 19 then requires any assignment to be in writing signed by the assignor, identifying the work, the rights, the duration and the territory — and if duration is not stated it is deemed five years, and if territory is not stated it is deemed India only. The practical consequence: a US-style "work made for hire" clause alone may not do the job under Indian law. Insist on an express assignment clause drafted for Section 19. Confirm with an advocate for your facts.
Can I patent my machine-learning algorithm in India?
Probably not as such. Section 3(k) of the Patents Act 1970 excludes "a mathematical or business method or a computer programme per se or algorithms" from being inventions. The words that create room are "per se" — the Indian Patent Office's Computer Related Inventions Guidelines ask whether the claimed invention makes a genuine technical contribution or produces a technical effect beyond the normal interaction between a programme and a computer, and look at the substance of the claim rather than its drafting. A model that merely makes a better prediction will usually be refused; a method that measurably improves the functioning of a computer or a connected technical system, or that controls a physical process, may be allowable. For most pure-software businesses in India, copyright plus trade secrecy plus speed is a better investment than an Indian patent — though the position differs in the US and Europe, so a cross-border filing strategy is a separate question. Verify the current CRI Guidelines at ipindia.gov.in and take advice from an IP attorney.
A client's contract makes me liable without any cap. How do I push back?
Use three arguments in order. First, insurance: "my professional indemnity cover responds to ₹2 crore; a cap above my insurance transfers uninsured risk to a sole practitioner and would mean signing something I cannot perform." This is an objective, verifiable fact and it is the most persuasive thing you can say. Second, proportionality: a ₹20 lakh engagement cannot support unlimited exposure, and no rational supplier would price it that way. Third, offer a super-cap rather than a flat refusal — an aggregate cap at 12 months' fees for general liability, with a higher multiple (two or three times fees) for the risks they actually fear, namely confidentiality breach, data breach and IP infringement. Keep the standard uncapped carve-outs: fraud, wilful misconduct, death or personal injury, and liability that cannot lawfully be limited. Also check whether the carve-outs swallow the cap — a cap with seven carve-outs covering every realistic breach is decorative.
Does an unstamped agreement mean my contract is invalid?
No. An insufficiently stamped or unstamped instrument is not void; it is inadmissible in evidence under Section 35 of the Indian Stamp Act 1899 until the defect is cured by paying the deficit duty plus a penalty, and it can be impounded under Section 33. That is expensive and embarrassing, not fatal. Specifically on arbitration, a seven-judge bench of the Supreme Court held in In Re: Interplay Between Arbitration Agreements under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 and the Indian Stamp Act 1899 (December 2023) that non-stamping renders an agreement inadmissible but not void or unenforceable, and that the defect is curable — so an arbitration can proceed. Stamp duty is a state subject, rates vary by state and by the nature of the instrument, and an assignment of intellectual property may attract ad valorem duty as a conveyance in some states, which is materially more than the fixed duty on a services agreement. Check your state Stamp Act and confirm the applicable Article before executing a high-value assignment.
Can I sign contracts electronically in India?
Yes, for almost everything. Section 10A of the IT Act 2000 provides that a contract is not unenforceable merely because it was formed by electronic means, Section 4 gives legal recognition to electronic records, and Section 5 to electronic signatures. The strongest forms are a Class 3 Digital Signature Certificate under Section 3 and Aadhaar eSign under Section 3A and the Second Schedule; DocuSign-style click-to-sign with an audit trail is widely used for cross-border commercial contracts and relies on Section 10A. The exceptions are in the First Schedule to the IT Act: a negotiable instrument other than a cheque, a power-of-attorney, a trust, a will, and any contract for the sale or conveyance of immovable property. Those must be executed on paper. Verify the current First Schedule at indiacode.nic.in.
What is the difference between the seat and the venue of an arbitration, and why does everyone go on about it?
The seat is the legal home of the arbitration. It determines the procedural law, which country's courts have supervisory jurisdiction over appointment, interim relief and challenges to the award, and whether the award is treated as domestic or foreign for enforcement. The venue is merely where hearings physically happen, and it can be anywhere, including a video call. Ambiguity about whether a reference to a place designates the seat or the venue has generated a very large volume of Indian litigation, with parties fighting for years about which court can supervise. The fix is two sentences: "The seat of the arbitration shall be Singapore" and "The venue of hearings may be any location agreed by the parties or determined by the tribunal, including by video conference, without affecting the seat." Never write only "the arbitration shall be held in Singapore".
My client wants to use AGPL-licensed software in their SaaS product. What do I tell them?
Stop and get legal advice before writing any code around it. The AGPL-3.0 is a strong copyleft licence with a network trigger: section 13 provides that if users interact with a modified version of the programme over a network, you must offer those users the corresponding source of the whole work. For a SaaS product that can require publishing the source of your client's proprietary business logic — exactly the outcome the plain GPL avoids, because merely running software on your own server is not "distribution". The SSPL goes further still. The practical answer is usually one of: use a permissively licensed alternative (MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD); buy a commercial licence from the same vendor, which most AGPL projects offer and which typically costs a few thousand dollars a year; or architect so that the AGPL component is genuinely separate and unmodified — though "separate" is a legally loaded word and should not be assessed by an engineer alone. Put a clause in your contract requiring written consent before any reciprocal-licence component is incorporated, and deliver a bill of materials with every deliverable.
What is the practical difference between an NDA and a confidentiality clause in an MSA?
Functionally very little — both create an obligation not to use or disclose. The difference is timing and scope. An NDA covers the pre-contract period when you are exploring whether to work together and there is no other agreement in place; a confidentiality clause covers the engagement. Once an MSA with a confidentiality clause is signed, it usually supersedes the earlier NDA by virtue of the entire-agreement clause — which is fine unless the NDA was better than the MSA clause. Check two things when signing an MSA: whether the confidentiality clause is at least as protective as the NDA it replaces, and whether information disclosed before the MSA is expressly brought within it. A single sentence does it: "Information disclosed under the Non-Disclosure Agreement dated 14 February 2026 shall be treated as Confidential Information under this Agreement."
A US client sent me Standard Contractual Clauses to sign. What are they and do I have to?
You have probably received EU SCCs rather than anything American — the EU Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses adopted by Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914, which are the main lawful mechanism for transferring personal data out of the European Economic Area to a country without an adequacy decision. India does not have an EU adequacy decision, so any EU-origin personal data reaching you needs a transfer mechanism, and SCCs are it. You will normally sign Module Two (controller to processor) and complete Annex I (parties, description of transfer), Annex II (technical and organisational measures) and Annex III (sub-processors). Read Annex II carefully — it is a binding description of your actual security controls, and overstating them creates real exposure. Annex II is where most people simply paste a vendor's boilerplate; do not. If the data originates in the UK you will additionally need the UK International Data Transfer Addendum or the UK IDTA. Have a privacy practitioner review your first set; after that you can reuse the annexes.
My employer is asking me to sign a bond for ₹3 lakh if I leave within two years. Is that enforceable?
It depends on what it actually compensates. A bond that is simply a penalty for leaving faces two problems: it looks like a restraint, and Section 74 of the Indian Contract Act 1872 means the stated sum operates as a ceiling, with the employer entitled only to reasonable compensation which they must generally establish. A bond that recovers genuine, quantifiable training costs actually incurred — a specific certification, an overseas deputation, a paid programme — is more likely to be enforced, to the extent of the proven cost, and ideally on a pro-rata reducing basis over the bond period. Retention of your original educational certificates is widely considered improper and has attracted judicial criticism. If you are being asked to sign one: ask for the actual cost in writing, insist on pro-rata reduction, and refuse to hand over originals. If you are drafting one: state the real cost, attach evidence, and make it proportionate. The case law is not uniform; take advice on the specific bond.
How long should confidentiality obligations last?
Three to five years from the date of disclosure is the normal commercial range for ordinary business information, with an indefinite obligation for anything that constitutes a trade secret, for as long as it remains secret. Perpetual confidentiality over everything sounds protective and is actually counterproductive — it is unmanageable in practice, it is sometimes read down, and it makes the clause harder to negotiate. Distinguish three things in your drafting: the term of the agreement (during which disclosures are covered), the survival period of the obligations (running from each disclosure, not from the end of the agreement), and the trade secret carve-out (indefinite). Note that India has no trade secrets statute — protection rests on contract, the equitable action for breach of confidence, and the controls you actually implement.
What should I do in the first hour after discovering a data breach?
Act on a pre-written plan; do not improvise. (1) Contain — isolate affected systems, revoke credentials, preserve evidence rather than destroying it. (2) Start a written log with timestamps of everything you know and do; this becomes your evidence. (3) Call your lawyer and your cyber insurer — notification deadlines are statutory and short, and many policies require insurer notification before you incur response costs. (4) Notify affected clients within the window in your DPA, commonly 24 hours, even if the picture is incomplete; phased notification is expressly contemplated. (5) Consider the CERT-In directions, which require reporting of specified cyber incidents within six hours of noticing them. (6) Support your client's obligations to the Data Protection Board under Section 8(6) of the DPDP Act 2023 and, where EU data is involved, their 72-hour GDPR notification. (7) Do not make public statements naming a client without their consent. Write this sequence down now, with phone numbers, and keep it somewhere you can reach without your corporate systems.
What is the single most common contract mistake engineers make?
Reading the clauses and not the definitions. The dangerous drafting almost always lives in a definition rather than in an operative clause. A one-line clause saying "all Deliverables shall vest in the Customer" is unremarkable until you read that "Deliverables" is defined as "all work product, materials, inventions and developments created, conceived or reduced to practice by the Supplier during the Term" — at which point it has assigned your entire year of creative output, including your reusable libraries and your unrelated weekend project. The same pattern recurs with "Confidential Information" (catch-all with no exclusions), "Losses" (including settlements you never approved), "Affiliate" (extending a liability cap across twelve entities) and "Services" (open-ended obligations). Read the definitions first, every time. It takes four minutes.
When is it worth paying a lawyer versus using a template?
Pay once for a template set — MSA, consulting agreement, SOW template, mutual NDA, sub-contractor agreement, DPA — drafted or reviewed by an advocate for Indian law and your actual business. That is typically ₹40,000–₹2,00,000 and you will use it for years. After that, use the templates for routine work and set a value threshold — commonly 15–25% of annual revenue — above which every contract gets a paid review. Always pay for: anything with uncapped liability or an uncapped indemnity you cannot negotiate away; any assignment of pre-existing IP; founders' and shareholders' agreements and term sheets; any legal notice received; an employment termination; a data breach; and any cross-border tax characterisation. Brief tightly with specific questions, state your budget, and ask for a fixed fee and a one-page priority list you can use in negotiation. A well-briefed hour is worth more than five unfocused ones.
