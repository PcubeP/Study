How to Use This Guide

This is an operational manual for a software engineer in India who is about to stop being only an employee. It walks the entire path from "I have a side project and one paying customer" to "I have a registered entity, a bank account, a GST number and a compliance calendar" — naming every form, every portal, every document, every fee and every realistic failure mode along the way. It is written for the tech/consulting/product case specifically: a solo consultant billing a US client, two engineers bootstrapping a SaaS, or a team raising a seed round.

This is educational material, not legal or tax adviceIndian company law, tax law and labour law are statutory, fact-specific and change with every Union Budget and every notification. Nothing here is advice on your facts. Before you incorporate, sign anything, claim a deduction, or choose a tax regime, engage a qualified Chartered Accountant (CA), Company Secretary (CS) or advocate and have them confirm the current position for your situation. Every rate, threshold, fee, form number and due date in this guide is stated as at FY 2025-26 / early 2026 and must be re-verified against the official portal before you act on it.
Four large changes are in flight right now — verify current status (1) Income-tax Act, 2025 is scheduled to replace the Income-tax Act, 1961 with effect from 1 April 2026, introducing a unified "tax year" concept in place of "previous year"/"assessment year". Section numbers in this guide follow the 1961 Act; map them before relying on them. (2) GST rate rationalisation of September 2025 moved the structure to principally 5% and 18% slabs with a 40% demerit rate. Confirm the rate for your exact HSN/SAC. (3) The four Labour Codes (Code on Wages 2019; Industrial Relations Code 2020; Code on Social Security 2020; Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code 2020) were brought into force from late 2025, but state rules are still landing — your obligations depend on your state. (4) The DPDP Act 2023 Rules were notified in late 2025 with phased compliance deadlines. If you touch personal data, confirm which phase binds you. Treat all four as "verify current status before acting".

Before You Incorporate — Do You Even Need an Entity Yet?

The single most expensive early mistake an Indian engineer makes is incorporating a Private Limited Company before there is a customer. A company is not a trophy; it is a recurring liability of roughly ₹25,000–₹60,000 (₹25,000–₹60,000) a year in professional fees and filings, enforced by penalties that accrue at ₹100 per day per form with no upper cap, and backed by director disqualification under Section 164 of the Companies Act, 2013. Earn first, then structure.

The "first customer first" principle

You do not need a registered entity to find out whether anyone will pay you. You need a conversation, a scope, a price and an invoice. In India, an individual can lawfully provide professional and technical services, raise an invoice, receive payment into a personal savings or current account, and pay tax on that income as "Profits and gains of business or profession" under the Income-tax Act, 1961 — without registering anything. The business comes into existence the moment you start carrying it on. Registration is what you do when a specific statute compels it, or when a specific commercial benefit justifies it.

The 30-day clockGST registration is due within 30 days of becoming liable (Section 25(1), CGST Act). The liability starts on the day you cross the threshold, not on the day you notice. When the maths flipsThe company becomes clearly worth it when (a) you are raising external money, (b) you need to issue ESOPs to hire, (c) your contracts carry real liability exposure (you touch client production data, you sign indemnities), (d) your profits exceed roughly ₹25–30 lakh and the 22% company rate under Section 115BAA plus dividend planning beats your personal slab, or (e) your buyers simply will not contract with a proprietorship. Below that, a proprietorship or an LLP is usually the rational answer.

What "unregistered" still obliges you to do

  • File an income tax return declaring business income (ITR-3 or ITR-4), by 31 July or 31 October depending on audit applicability
  • Pay advance tax in instalments if your total tax liability exceeds ₹10,000 (₹10,000) for the year — Section 208
  • Maintain books under Section 44AA if income exceeds ₹2.5 lakh (₹250,000) or turnover exceeds ₹25 lakh (₹2,500,000) — unless you are on a presumptive scheme
  • Deduct TDS where applicable — individuals become liable once Section 44AB audit applied in the preceding financial year
  • Comply with contracts you sign — personally and without limited liability
  • Comply with FEMA for any foreign-currency receipts, and obtain FIRC / e-BRC for export of services
Unlimited liability is not a theoretical risk for engineersAs a sole proprietor, you and the business are the same legal person. If you sign a consulting agreement with an uncapped indemnity and then push a migration script that deletes a client's production table, the claim reaches your personal savings, your car and your flat. Indian SaaS and services contracts routinely contain indemnities for IP infringement and data breach. Read every limitation-of-liability clause before you decide that incorporation is "premature".

The Entity Decision

India offers six realistic vehicles for a technology business: Sole Proprietorship, Partnership Firm, Limited Liability Partnership, Private Limited Company, One Person Company and Section 8 Company. They differ on liability, taxation, compliance load, cost, and — decisively for startups — the ability to issue shares to investors and employees. Choose on the basis of what you will need in 18 months, not what you need this week, because converting later is possible but costs time, fees and sometimes tax.

Decision flowchart

A first-pass decision tree. It is deliberately biased against incorporation: the burden of proof is on the company, not on the proprietorship.

Five worked scenarios

Scenario 1 — Solo consultant billing a US client ₹60 lakh/year

Facts. Anita is a backend engineer who left her job to consult. One US client, one Indian client, no employees, no plans to raise money. Expected revenue ₹60 lakh (₹6,000,000). Expenses modest.

Answer: Sole Proprietorship with GST registration and an LUT.

WhyDetail
Section 44ADAAs an eligible professional she can declare 50% of gross receipts as income. On ₹60 lakh that is ₹30 lakh taxable. No books of account required under Section 44AA(1) proviso, no tax audit if cash receipts are ≤5%.
No auditA Pvt Ltd would force a statutory audit every single year regardless of turnover — roughly ₹25,000–₹40,000 a year of pure cost.
Export of servicesShe registers for GST (compulsory in practice for clean FIRC/BRC and because exports are zero-rated supplies), files an LUT in Form RFD-11, and exports without paying IGST.
LiabilityShe negotiates a liability cap equal to 12 months' fees in her consulting agreement and buys professional indemnity insurance. That handles the exposure more cheaply than incorporation.

When Anita should reconsider: the moment she hires her second employee, signs an uncapped indemnity, or starts building a product she intends to fund.

Scenario 2 — Two co-founders bootstrapping a dev-tools SaaS

Facts. Vikram and Sneha are building a developer tool. Revenue ₹0 today, maybe ₹15 lakh next year. They intend to bootstrap for at least two years but "might" raise later. They want to split 60/40.

Answer: it depends on honesty about "might".

If they will genuinely never raiseIf raising is plausible within 24 months
LLP. Lower compliance, no mandatory audit below ₹40 lakh turnover / ₹25 lakh contribution, profit-sharing written into the LLP Agreement, limited liability, and partners' remuneration deductible to the LLP under Section 40(b).Private Limited. Converting an LLP to a Pvt Ltd under Section 366 of the Companies Act is slow, requires newspaper advertisements, creditor consents and fresh incorporation, and can trigger capital-gains questions. Doing it while a term sheet is live is a nightmare.
Practical ruleIf the product is a SaaS product rather than a services practice, choose Pvt Ltd from day one. SaaS almost always ends up wanting ESOPs for the first three hires, and an LLP cannot issue them. The extra ~₹60,000/year is the price of not having to restructure mid-fundraise.

Either way, before a single line of code: a written founders' agreement with 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff, IP assignment, and a departure/buyback clause. See the pre-incorporation section below.

Scenario 3 — VC-track startup, three founders, pre-seed in six months

Facts. Three engineers building an AI infrastructure product. Talking to angels. Expect a ₹3 crore (₹30,000,000) pre-seed on a SAFE/CCPS within six months.

Answer: Private Limited Company, no alternative.

  • Investors subscribe to Compulsorily Convertible Preference Shares (CCPS) or equity — only a company can issue these
  • Foreign investors need FDI under the automatic route — only into a company (or LLP, with constraints that VCs will not accept)
  • An ESOP pool (typically 8–12% of fully diluted capital) must exist before the round closes
  • Due diligence will test cap table, board minutes, share certificates, statutory registers, PF/TDS compliance, and IP assignment from every founder and contractor
  • DPIIT recognition early, so that the Section 56(2)(viib) "angel tax" exemption position and the Section 80-IAC application route are available
Set authorised capital with the round in mindIncreasing authorised capital later needs a shareholders' resolution, Form SH-7, MCA fee and additional stamp duty. If you know a ₹3 crore round is coming, consider authorised capital of ₹10 lakh (₹1,000,000) at incorporation. MCA incorporation fee is nil up to ₹15 lakh authorised capital (verify the current fee schedule on mca.gov.in), so the extra headroom is nearly free apart from stamp duty.

The three questions that decide it

1. Will someone buy shares?

If yes — within 24 months, at any valuation, from anyone other than you — it is a Private Limited Company. Nothing else issues shares to outsiders cleanly.

2. What does my worst contract say?

Open the consulting agreement or MSA you are most likely to sign. Find the indemnity and the limitation of liability. If liability is uncapped or capped above your net worth, you want limited liability.

3. Can I live without presumptive tax?

Section 44AD/44ADA is only available to individuals, HUFs and partnership firms — not LLPs or companies. For a profitable solo consultant it is worth lakhs a year in avoided bookkeeping and tax.

Conversion is possible but never freeProprietorship → Pvt Ltd under Section 366 (Part I company registration), partnership → LLP under Section 55 of the LLP Act, LLP → company under Section 366, OPC → Pvt Ltd under Rule 6 of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules 2014. Each requires fresh registrations (new PAN, new GSTIN, new bank account), consents, advertisements, and sometimes capital-gains analysis under Section 47 of the Income-tax Act for the transfer of assets. Budget 2–4 months and ₹30,000–₹1,00,000 in fees. Choosing right the first time is cheaper.

Pre-Incorporation Groundwork

Everything that destroys a startup in year three was decided, or not decided, in the two weeks before incorporation. Equity splits, vesting, who owns the code written before the company existed, what the company is called, and where its registered office sits — these are cheap to fix now and brutally expensive later. Do this work before you touch the MCA portal.

Vesting — the non-negotiable

Standard Indian startup practice mirrors Silicon Valley: 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff, monthly or quarterly thereafter. Mechanically, in an Indian Private Limited Company, founders usually hold their shares outright but subject to a buyback/transfer obligation on unvested portions, written into the Founders' Agreement and reflected in the Articles. Direct "reverse vesting" of issued equity is done via a contractual call option at nominal price, because Section 68 buyback restrictions make a true company buyback cumbersome.

Acceleration clausesAgree up front on single-trigger (acceleration on acquisition) vs double-trigger (acceleration on acquisition and termination without cause). Double-trigger is market standard and is what acquirers will accept. Single-trigger acceleration for all founders can reduce your acquisition price, because the acquirer must re-incentivise the team from scratch.

Co-founder agreement — minimum contents

  • Parties, effective date, and recital of the business
  • Roles, titles and decision authority — who decides what without a board vote
  • Equity split and the exact number of shares each will subscribe to
  • Vesting schedule, cliff, acceleration triggers, and the buyback mechanic with price
  • Time commitment — full-time, and what outside activities are permitted
  • Compensation now and the trigger for raising it
  • Intellectual property assignment — all pre-incorporation and ongoing work product vests in the company
  • Confidentiality — survives termination indefinitely for trade secrets
  • Non-solicitation of employees and customers
  • Transfer restrictions: right of first refusal, tag-along, drag-along
  • Leaver provisions: good leaver vs bad leaver and the differing buyback prices
  • Deadlock resolution
  • Dispute resolution — arbitration seat and venue under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996, governing law, and jurisdiction
  • Amendment only in writing signed by all founders

Naming the company and the trademark search

Your company name and your brand are two different legal objects. MCA approves the company name under the Companies Act. The Trade Marks Registry grants the trademark under the Trade Marks Act 1999. Having one does not give you the other. Engineers routinely discover this after printing business cards.

Idea→Domain check→TM search (ipindia)→MCA name search→Reserve name→File TM-A
  1. Check the domain firstCheapest and fastest signal. If the .com and .in are both gone and the squatter wants $12,000, pick another name. Do this before you emotionally commit.
  2. Search the Trade Marks RegistryGo to ipindia.gov.in → Trade Marks → Public Search. Search your word mark in Class 42 (software/SaaS/IT services), Class 9 (downloadable software) and Class 35 (business services) at minimum. Also search phonetically similar marks — "Kwik" conflicts with "Quick".
  3. Search MCA for existing companiesMCA portal → MCA Services → Check Company Name. Also check LLP names. The Companies (Incorporation) Rules 2014 prohibit names that are identical or "too nearly resemble" an existing company or LLP name, or a registered trademark.
  4. Check for the obvious landminesNo name suggesting government patronage, no name requiring a sectoral regulator's NOC (bank, insurance, stock exchange, venture capital, mutual fund, chit fund, Nidhi) unless you have that NOC.
  5. Decide the suffix"Private Limited" for a Pvt Ltd, "LLP" for an LLP, "(OPC) Private Limited" for an OPC.
  6. File the trademark application in parallelForm TM-A on ipindia.gov.in. For an applicant with DPIIT startup recognition or Udyam MSME registration, the government fee is reduced — indicatively ₹4,500 per class per mark for e-filing by individuals/startups/MSMEs versus ₹9,000 for others. Verify current fees on ipindia.gov.in. You can use ™ from the day you file; ® only after registration.
Two-part name structureMCA expects a name in the form [Distinctive element] + [Activity/object element] + [Suffix]. "Nimbus Data Systems Private Limited" works. A bare "Nimbus Private Limited" is more likely to be queried. The activity element should align with the main object clause in your MoA.

Resident director requirement

Section 149(3), Companies Act 2013 — verify current wordingEvery company must have at least one director who has stayed in India for a total of not less than 182 days during the financial year. For a newly incorporated company the requirement applies proportionately to the part of the year after incorporation. This is the hardest constraint for a founding team that is entirely overseas: you must find a trustworthy resident director, and that person carries real statutory liability. Confirm the current text on mca.gov.in. Position stated as at FY 2025-26.

Who can be a director

  • An individual only — a company cannot be a director (Section 149(1))
  • Must have a Director Identification Number (DIN); for the first directors of a new company, DIN is allotted through SPICe+ itself for up to three directors, with no separate DIR-3
  • Must not be disqualified under Section 164 — undischarged insolvent, convicted of an offence involving moral turpitude with a sentence of six months or more in the last five years, or a director of a company that failed to file financial statements or annual returns for three continuous financial years
  • Must file DIR-3 KYC every year by 30 September or the DIN is deactivated; reactivation costs ₹5,000
  • A person may hold directorships in a maximum of 20 companies, of which not more than 10 may be public companies (Section 165)

The issuance process

  1. Choose a CA or reseller and payOnline application form; you select class (Class 3), type (Signature), validity and whether you need a token shipped.
  2. Aadhaar eKYC or PAN-based KYCMost Indian applicants complete paperless Aadhaar-based eKYC with an OTP to the Aadhaar-linked mobile. If the Aadhaar mobile is out of date, you fall back to document upload plus attestation, which is slower.
  3. Video verificationA short recorded video in which you state your name, state that you are applying for a DSC, read out a one-time code shown on screen, and display your PAN card. Record in good light, in a quiet room, with the PAN legible. This step is rejected more often than any other.
  4. Verification and approvalThe Certifying Authority reviews. Typically a few hours to two working days.
  5. Download to tokenYou receive a download link and a reference/authentication code. Plug in the USB token, install the token driver and the CA's download utility, and download the certificate onto the token. The certificate is written to the token and cannot be moved.
  6. Register the DSC on MCAMCA portal → login → Associate DSC, selecting the role (Director, Professional, Manager/Secretary, Nodal Officer). The DSC must be associated with the correct DIN or PAN or MCA forms will fail validation at upload.
Keep the token PIN safeIf you forget the token password there is no recovery. The token locks after a set number of wrong attempts — often five or ten — and becomes a paperweight; you then buy a new token and a new certificate. Record the PIN and store it with your other founder documents.

Step 5 — The First 90 Days After Incorporation

Incorporation is the start of the obligations, not the end. Five statutory deadlines bite within the first six months, and missing them carries penalties that dwarf the incorporation cost. The most dangerous is INC-20A: miss it and the company cannot legally commence business, and the Registrar may strike it off.

The first-90-days deadline map

Five statutory clocks start on the date printed on your Certificate of Incorporation. INC-20A is the one that can kill the company.

Opening the current account

  1. Pick the bank before you need itPrivate banks (HDFC, ICICI, Axis, Kotak, IDFC First, RBL) are faster and have better APIs; public sector banks (SBI, BoB) have lower minimum balances and are better if you will deal with government customers. LLP Agreement — what must be in it

If you do not file an LLP Agreement, the First Schedule to the LLP Act 2008 applies by default. The default rules are almost certainly not what you want: they provide for equal profit sharing regardless of contribution, equal management rights, and unanimous consent for admitting a partner. Draft your own.

  • Name, registered office, and business of the LLP
  • Names of partners and designated partners, with DPIN
  • Contribution by each partner — in money, property, or services (if services, the valuation must be certified)
  • Profit-sharing ratio — need not match contribution ratio, and this is a key advantage over a company
  • Remuneration and interest on capital to partners — drafted to fit within Section 40(b) of the Income-tax Act limits so it is deductible
  • Management: who decides what; which matters need unanimous consent

The Partnership Firm

Governed by the Indian Partnership Act, 1932. A partnership comes into existence by agreement — the deed — and registration with the Registrar of Firms is optional. But the consequence of not registering is severe.

Section 69 — an unregistered firm cannot sueSection 69 of the Indian Partnership Act 1932 bars an unregistered firm (or a partner of it) from filing a suit to enforce a contractual right against a third party, and bars a partner from suing the firm or co-partners to enforce a right under the partnership. You can be sued; you cannot sue. If a client refuses to pay a ₹15 lakh invoice, an unregistered firm has no civil remedy to recover it on the contract. Register with the Registrar of Firms of your state.

Partnership deed — mandatory contents

  • Name of the firm and its principal place of business
  • Names, addresses and ages of all partners
  • Date of commencement and duration (at will, or for a fixed term, or for a particular venture)
  • Nature of business
  • Capital contribution by each partner, and whether interest is payable on capital (and at what rate, within the Section 40(b) 12% p.a. ceiling for deductibility)
  • Profit and loss sharing ratio
  • Remuneration to working partners — drafted to satisfy Section 40(b)
  • Drawing rights and limits
  • Duties, powers and restrictions on each partner
  • Bank account operation and signing authority
  • Admission of a new partner, retirement, expulsion, death and the consequences for continuity
  • Accounts, audit and the financial year
  • Dissolution procedure and distribution of assets
  • Arbitration clause
  • Executed on stamp paper of the value prescribed by your state and signed by all partners before witnesses

Registration with the Registrar of Firms

  1. Execute the deed on stamp paperStamp value is state-specific — commonly ₹500 to ₹2,000, sometimes scaled by capital. Sign before two witnesses.
  2. NotariseNot legally mandatory everywhere, but universally expected by banks.
  3. File Form 1 (Statement) with the Registrar of FirmsPrescribed under Section 58 of the Indian Partnership Act. States the firm name, principal place, other places of business, date of joining of each partner, names and addresses of partners, and duration.
  4. Pay the fee and attach documentsOriginal/certified deed, affidavit, address proof of the place of business, PAN of the firm (apply separately via Form 49A if not already obtained), ID proofs of partners. Fees vary by state — typically ₹500 to ₹3,000.
  5. Receive the Certificate of RegistrationTimeline ranges from a week (online states like Maharashtra and Karnataka) to several months in states where the process is still physical.
  6. Apply for firm PAN and TANForm 49A for PAN with the deed as proof of constitution.
  7. Open a current accountDeed + registration certificate + firm PAN + partner KYC + a partnership letter from all partners.

What Udyam actually gets you

1. Section 43B(h) — the 45-day payment protection

This is the most valuable thing on the list for a small vendorSection 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 provides that a payment due to a micro or small enterprise is deductible to the buyer only in the year of actual payment, unless paid within the time limit under Section 15 of the MSMED Act 2006 — which is 45 days where there is a written agreement, or 15 days where there is not. The practical effect: a large customer that delays your invoice beyond 45 days loses the tax deduction for that expense in the year it was incurred. Enterprise finance teams now actively chase MSME vendor payments to avoid this. Put your Udyam number on every invoice. Position as at FY 2025-26; confirm the treatment under the Income-tax Act, 2025.

2. MSME Samadhaan — delayed payment recovery

Under Sections 15–18 of the MSMED Act 2006, a micro or small enterprise can file an online application on the MSME Samadhaan portal against a buyer who has not paid within 45 days. The matter goes to the Micro and Small Enterprise Facilitation Council (MSEFC) of the state, which conciliates and, failing that, arbitrates. The buyer is liable for compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate on the delayed amount. Filing is free, and the mere act of filing frequently produces payment.

3. CGTMSE — collateral-free credit

The Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises guarantees a portion of a bank's exposure to an MSE, which allows the bank to lend without collateral or third-party guarantee. Coverage limits and guarantee fee percentages are revised periodically — verify the current scheme on cgtmse.in. For a services business with no assets to pledge, this is often the only route to a term loan.

The myths

Myth: "Udyam costs money"

It is free. Any charge is a private intermediary's fee for a form you can fill in ten minutes.

Myth: "You need a GST number first"

Not if you are exempt from GST registration. The portal has an option to declare GST exemption. PAN is mandatory; GSTIN is required only where you are liable to be registered.

Myth: "It expires annually"

There is no expiry and no renewal. Classification is updated automatically from your ITR and GST data.

Myth: "Only manufacturers qualify"

Service enterprises have been explicitly covered since 2020. A two-person software consultancy qualifies as Micro.

Myth: "It makes you liable for more compliance"

Udyam itself imposes no filing obligation. The only ongoing item is updating details if the business changes materially.

Myth: "A Pvt Ltd cannot register"

Companies, LLPs, partnerships, proprietorships, trusts, HUFs, societies and co-operatives can all register, subject to the investment and turnover limits.

Foreign Founders, NRIs and Overseas Structures

If any founder is a non-resident, or if the company will have an overseas holding entity, the structure stops being a company-law question and becomes a FEMA question. Get this wrong and you face compounding proceedings with the RBI, blocked funding rounds and failed due diligence. Get it right and it is routine.

The resident director requirement, in practice

You cannot incorporate an Indian company with only non-resident directorsSection 149(3) requires at least one director who stayed in India for at least 182 days in the financial year. For a founding team that is entirely overseas, the options are: (a) one founder relocates and establishes residence, (b) you appoint a trusted Indian resident as a director — who then carries genuine statutory liability for the company's defaults under dozens of sections, or (c) you set up a branch office, liaison office or project office instead of a subsidiary, which has its own RBI approval process. Option (b) using a paid nominee director is common and is a real risk for both sides. Verify the current requirement on mca.gov.in.

Reverse-flipping back to India

Several well-known Indian startups have moved their holding company back to India ahead of a domestic IPO, because listing on Indian exchanges requires an Indian holding company. The mechanisms are an inbound merger under Section 234 of the Companies Act 2013 read with the FEMA (Cross Border Merger) Regulations 2018 and NCLT approval, or a share swap, or a sale of the offshore entity's holding to a new Indian holdco. The process takes 12–24 months, needs NCLT approval, and can trigger substantial tax. If an Indian IPO is plausibly your exit, think very hard before flipping out in the first place.

Closing a Business

Businesses stop. Companies do not. A Private Limited Company that stops trading continues to accrue filing obligations, continues to accrue ₹100-per-day additional fees, and eventually disqualifies its directors under Section 164(2) — which bars them from being a director of any company for five years. Closing properly is cheaper than abandoning.

"Just stop filing" is the most expensive optionIf a company fails to file financial statements or annual returns for three continuous financial years, every person who is or was a director of that company during that period is disqualified under Section 164(2)(a) for five years, and their DIN is deactivated. They cannot be appointed as a director of any other company, including the new one they are about to start. Thousands of Indian directors discovered this when the MCA published disqualification lists. There is no cheap fix; restoration requires an NCLT application or a scheme, and payment of all pending fees.

Frequently Asked Questions

Specific answers to the questions engineers actually ask when setting up in India. Every answer is stated as at FY 2025-26 and should be confirmed against the official source named.

I am employed full-time and want to take on freelance consulting on the side. Do I need to register a business?
Legally, no. You can invoice as an individual and declare the income as business or professional income in ITR-3 or ITR-4. Two things matter more than registration. First, your employment contract — most Indian IT employment agreements contain an exclusivity clause and an IP assignment clause broad enough to capture anything you build on your own time, including with your own equipment. Breaching it is a contractual matter, not a criminal one, but it can cost you your job and your ESOPs. Read Clause "Exclusivity of Service" and "Ownership of Work Product" in your offer letter. Second, GST — you must register within 30 days of crossing ₹20 lakh (₹2,000,000) of aggregate turnover for services, and that aggregate includes all your business receipts under the same PAN. Your salary is not part of aggregate turnover. Keep a separate bank account for consulting receipts from the first rupee, because untangling it at year end is painful.
Should I register a Private Limited Company before I have any revenue?
Only if you are raising money, issuing ESOPs, or carrying real liability. Otherwise it costs ₹45,000–₹1,20,000 a year in audit and filings to administer zero revenue, and it starts statutory clocks — INC-20A within 180 days, first board meeting within 30 days, auditor within 30 days, annual filings from the first financial year end — that you will miss while you are busy building. If you are pre-revenue and pre-funding, consider incorporating when you have a term sheet or a signed customer, not when you have an idea. The one genuine exception: if you need an entity to sign a pilot contract with an enterprise buyer whose procurement will not onboard an individual, incorporate.
LLP or Private Limited for a two-person consulting firm with no fundraising plans?
LLP, in most cases. You get limited liability and perpetual succession, you avoid the mandatory statutory audit until turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh (₹4,000,000) or contribution exceeds ₹25 lakh (₹2,500,000), you file two annual forms instead of six, and — the underrated part — profit share distributed to partners is exempt in the partners' hands under Section 10(2A) of the Income-tax Act, whereas a company's dividends are taxed again at your slab rate. For an owner-operated services business that distributes most of its profit, the LLP is frequently the lower-tax structure. The two reasons to choose Pvt Ltd anyway: you want to issue ESOPs to your first hires, or you are not being honest with yourself about "no fundraising plans". Verify the current position with your CA, especially against the Income-tax Act, 2025.
How much does it realistically cost to incorporate a Private Limited Company in India?
Government and statutory cost is small: ₹1,000 name reservation, zero MCA incorporation fee up to ₹15 lakh authorised capital, ₹500–₹4,000 stamp duty depending on state and capital, ₹131 for PAN and TAN. Add ₹3,000 for two Class 3 DSCs. Professional fees are the bulk — ₹8,000 to ₹20,000 for end-to-end incorporation at metro rates. Post-incorporation you will spend another ₹5,000–₹8,000 on INC-20A, ADT-1, share certificates and registers. So roughly ₹25,000–₹40,000 of cash out, plus a blocked bank minimum balance of ₹10,000–₹1,00,000. The number that matters more is the ₹55,000–₹1,50,000 a year it costs to keep compliant. Verify current fees on mca.gov.in.
Can I use my home address as the registered office?
Yes. You need a utility bill in the owner's name not older than two months, plus a no-objection certificate from the owner permitting use as the registered office. If you own the flat, you give the NOC to yourself. Three practical cautions. First, the address becomes public on the MCA master data and on every invoice and letterhead under Section 12(3). Second, many housing society bye-laws and some state apartment acts prohibit commercial use of a residential unit, and a Shops & Establishment registration may be refused on that basis. Third, GST field verification under Rule 25 will visit, and the officer expects to see a name board and a workspace. A home office with a nameplate and a desk passes; a bedroom does not always. If you move house, file Form INC-22 within 30 days.
Do I need GST registration if all my clients are outside India?
Export of services is a zero-rated supply under Section 16 of the IGST Act, not an exempt one — which is a different thing. Strictly, if your aggregate turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh (₹2,000,000) you are liable to register under Section 22, and exports count towards aggregate turnover. In practice almost every services exporter registers voluntarily even below the threshold, for three reasons: your AD bank's documentation for FIRC and e-BRC is cleaner with a GSTIN; you can file an LUT in Form RFD-11 and export without paying IGST; and you can claim refund of accumulated input tax credit on your domestic expenses (cloud bills, rent, professional fees), which is real cash. The cost is twelve GSTR-1 and twelve GSTR-3B filings a year. Confirm the current threshold and the definition of export of services under Section 2(6) of the IGST Act on gst.gov.in.
What is an LUT and why does everyone say I must file it every year?
A Letter of Undertaking filed in Form RFD-11 on the GST portal is a bond by which you undertake to export without payment of IGST. Without it, you must pay IGST on every export invoice and then claim a refund — which blocks working capital for weeks or months. The LUT is valid for one financial year only and does not auto-renew. It must be filed fresh at the start of each financial year, ideally in the first week of April. The filing is free, takes ten minutes on gst.gov.in, and requires two witnesses' details. Forgetting it in April is one of the most common and most expensive cash-flow mistakes a services exporter makes. Verify the current form and conditions on gst.gov.in.
What exactly do I get from Udyam/MSME registration, and does it cost anything?
It is completely free on udyamregistration.gov.in and takes about ten minutes with Aadhaar and PAN. The most valuable benefit for a small vendor is Section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act: a buyer can deduct a payment due to a registered micro or small enterprise only in the year it is actually paid, unless paid within 45 days (or 15 days absent a written agreement) under Section 15 of the MSMED Act 2006. Large customers' finance teams now actively prioritise MSME vendor payments to protect their own deduction. Beyond that: MSME Samadhaan for free online recovery of delayed payments with compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate; CGTMSE collateral-free credit guarantee; preference and EMD exemption in government procurement; reduced trademark and patent fees. Put your Udyam number on every invoice. Any site charging for Udyam is a private intermediary.
Is DPIIT Startup India recognition worth applying for if I will not get the tax holiday?
Yes. Recognition itself is free, usually quick, and gives you self-certification under a list of labour and environment laws, an 80% rebate on patent filing fees and 50% on trademark fees, access to a government-paid IPR facilitator panel, expedited patent examination, exemption from prior-turnover and prior-experience criteria in central government tenders, EMD exemption, free GeM Startup Runway listing, and fast-track insolvency. For a company filing one trademark and one patent, the fee rebates alone can exceed ₹50,000. The Section 80-IAC three-year tax holiday is a separate application to the Inter-Ministerial Board with a much lower approval rate, and it only helps if you are profitable. Treat it as upside. Verify current benefits on startupindia.gov.in.
My co-founder and I want a clean 50:50 split. Is that a problem?
It is the single most dangerous cap table structure in Indian private companies, because there is no tie-breaker. With 50:50 shareholding and two directors, any disagreement on a matter requiring a board or shareholder resolution — appointing an auditor, approving accounts, issuing shares, borrowing, firing an employee the other hired — simply cannot be resolved. The company freezes, and the only legal route is an oppression-and-mismanagement petition to the NCLT under Sections 241–242, which takes years and destroys the business in the process. Fix it one of three ways: an uneven split such as 51:49; a 48:48:4 structure with the 4% held by a trusted neutral; or a binding deadlock clause in the Articles and the Shareholders' Agreement providing for a casting vote, mediation, or a buy-sell ("shotgun") mechanism. Decide before incorporation.
Do I need a Company Secretary on retainer?
A full-time Company Secretary is mandatory only for companies above the paid-up capital threshold prescribed under Section 203 and Rule 8A of the Companies (Appointment and Remuneration of Managerial Personnel) Rules — well above where a startup sits. But you need a practising CS for discrete work that only they can certify: incorporation forms, FC-GPR, PAS-3, share transfers, ESOP scheme documentation, increase of authorised capital, and strike-off. Most early-stage companies use a CS on a per-transaction basis or on a small annual retainer of ₹24,000–₹60,000 covering routine ROC filings and minutes. If you are taking foreign investment, put a CS on retainer before the round, not after — FC-GPR errors are expensive to compound.
Can a foreign national or NRI be a director or shareholder of my Indian company?
Shareholding by a non-resident is permitted in most sectors under the automatic FDI route, subject to reporting in Form FC-GPR on the RBI FIRMS portal within 30 days of allotment, a valuation report, and the Press Note 3 land-border check on beneficial ownership. Directorship is also permitted — a foreign national can hold a DIN and be a director. The binding constraint is Section 149(3): at least one director must have stayed in India for at least 182 days in the financial year. So you can have an all-foreign board plus one resident director, but not an all-foreign board. Practically, foreign directors also need an apostilled passport and address proof for the DSC and for SPICe+, which adds 2 to 4 weeks. Verify on mca.gov.in and dpiit.gov.in.
I registered a company two years ago, never traded, and never filed anything. What happens now?
You are accruing additional fees at ₹100 per day per unfiled form with no upper cap on the ROC additional fee, and you are one financial year away from director disqualification. Section 164(2)(a) disqualifies every director of a company that has not filed financial statements or annual returns for three continuous financial years, for five years, across all companies — including the new one you want to start. Act now: engage a CS, compute the total pending fee, file all overdue AOC-4 and MGT-7, file the pending income tax returns, surrender the GST registration if any, close the bank account, and then either apply for dormant status in Form MSC-1 if you may revive it, or strike it off in Form STK-2. The cost of regularising is typically ₹25,000–₹80,000. The cost of ignoring it is five years of not being a director anywhere.
Should I set up a US or Singapore holding company to make fundraising easier?
Rarely, and never casually. A "flip" is a reorganisation in which Indian shareholders transfer shares of the Indian company to a new offshore parent in exchange for its shares. That transfer is a taxable event in India for the transferors, engages FEMA's overseas investment and round-tripping rules, requires valuations on both sides, and creates transfer-pricing obligations on the subsequent intercompany services agreement including an annual Form 3CEB. The all-in professional cost runs to tens of lakhs. The historical reasons to flip — investor familiarity with Delaware documents, easier option pools, US acquirer preference — have weakened considerably as Indian VC and growth capital have deepened. If an Indian IPO is plausibly your exit, flipping out means a costly reverse-flip later via an NCLT-approved inbound merger. Do it only with specialist cross-border counsel and a written tax opinion, and verify the current Overseas Investment Rules on rbi.org.in.
What is the one thing most likely to go wrong in my first year?
Three things compete, and all are administrative rather than commercial. One: missing Form INC-20A within 180 days, which carries a ₹50,000 company penalty, ₹1,000 per day per officer, and possible strike-off. Two: not deducting TDS on payments to contractors and freelancers, which disallows 30% of that expense under Section 40(a)(ia) and adds interest and penalty — on a ₹25 lakh outsourced development spend this is a six-figure hit. Three: losing input tax credit because you did not reconcile GSTR-2B monthly and the Section 16(4) time limit expired. None of these are hard to avoid; all of them are missed because a founder decided compliance could wait until the product shipped. Set up a compliance calendar in week one and give one founder explicit ownership of it.

Week-by-Week Incorporation Checklist

A twelve-week plan from first conversation to a fully operational, compliant entity. Weeks are indicative; run steps in parallel where you can. This assumes a two-founder Private Limited Company in a metro city with both founders resident in India.

  1. Week 0 — Decide whether to incorporate at allConfirm you have a customer, a term sheet, a liability exposure, or a hiring need that requires an entity. If you have none of the four, defer and operate as a proprietorship. Read the entity comparison table and write down, in one sentence, why you are choosing the structure you are choosing.
  2. Week 1 — Founders' agreementRun the ten-question conversation. Agree the equity split, the vesting schedule and cliff, the full-time commitment date, the leaver provisions and the deadlock mechanism. Have a lawyer draft or review it. Execute it on correctly valued e-stamp paper. Do not skip this to "save time".
  3. Week 1 — Name and trademark searchGenerate five candidate names. Check .com and .in availability. Search ipindia.gov.in public search in Classes 9, 35 and 42, including phonetic variants. Search MCA company and LLP name databases. Shortlist two that survive everything.
  4. Week 2 — Collect documentsEach founder: PAN, Aadhaar, passport-size photograph, proof of present address, mobile number linked to Aadhaar, personal email. Registered office: latest utility bill not older than two months, owner's NOC, rent agreement if rented. Scan everything at high resolution into a shared folder.
  5. Week 2 — Apply for Class 3 DSCsOne per founder. Complete Aadhaar eKYC and video verification carefully — good light, quiet room, PAN legible, read the code slowly. Receive the USB token, install drivers, download the certificate, and record the token PIN somewhere safe.
  6. Week 3 — Engage a CA or CS and file SPICe+ Part AAgree a fixed fee covering incorporation, INC-20A, ADT-1, share certificates and registers. File Part A with two names and the correct NIC code. Simultaneously file the trademark application in Form TM-A.
  7. Week 3–4 — Draft the MoA object clause and the AoADraft a main object clause that covers software development, IT services and platform operation, with a negative covenant excluding banking, insurance, NBFC and chit-fund activity. Review the AoA for share transfer restrictions, board composition and any deadlock mechanism you agreed in week 1.
  8. Week 4 — File SPICe+ Part B with INC-33, INC-34 and INC-35Set authorised capital at ₹10,00,000 and paid-up at ₹1,00,000. Attach the address proof, NOC, DIR-2 consents and ID proofs. In AGILE-PRO-S, take EPFO, ESIC and professional tax; skip GST and register separately later. Pay the stamp duty and fees. Register the DSCs on MCA before uploading.
  9. Week 5 — Receive the Certificate of IncorporationVerify the CIN, the spelling of the company name, the registered office address and the names of directors on the certificate. Download the e-PAN and the TAN allotment letter. Save all three to the shared folder and to a second backup.
  10. Week 5–6 — Open the current account and deposit subscription moneyPass a board resolution naming the bank, branch, signatories and operating mandate. Complete branch KYC. Once the account is live, each founder transfers their exact subscription amount from their own personal account. Download the statement showing both credits.
  11. Week 6 — Hold the first board meetingWithin 30 days of incorporation. Give seven days' notice or obtain consent to shorter notice. Cover: noting the CoI, PAN and TAN; taking MBP-1 and DIR-8 from each director; appointing the first statutory auditor under Section 139(6); approving the bank account and mandate; authorising share certificates; adopting letterhead and name board; fixing the financial year; authorising GST, Udyam and other registrations. Minute it properly and sign it.
  12. Week 6 — File ADT-1 and obtain the auditor's consentObtain written consent and the eligibility certificate under Section 139(1) read with Rule 4 from the auditor before appointment. File ADT-1 within 15 days of the board meeting.
  13. Week 7 — File INC-20AAttach the bank statement evidencing receipt of the full subscription money from each subscriber. Have it certified by your CA or CS. Do not let this slip past 180 days under any circumstances.
  14. Week 7 — Issue share certificates and open statutory registersIssue in Form SH-1 within two months of incorporation, signed by two directors. Pay share-issue stamp duty per your state's requirement. Open the Register of Members in MGT-1, the Register of Directors, the Register of Charges, MBP-4 and the SBO register. Buy a bound minute book.
  15. Week 7 — Udyam registrationTen minutes on udyamregistration.gov.in with the authorised signatory's Aadhaar and the company PAN. Free. Put the Udyam number on the invoice template immediately.
  16. Week 8 — GST registrationFile REG-01 on gst.gov.in with the CoI, PAN, board resolution authorising the signatory, bank proof, address proof and photographs of the premises. Complete Aadhaar authentication to reduce the chance of physical verification. Expect 3 to 15 days.
  17. Week 8 — File the LUT in Form RFD-11Immediately after the GSTIN is issued, if you export services. Repeat at the start of every financial year.
  18. Week 9 — Shops & Establishment and Professional TaxRegister on the state labour portal and the state commercial tax portal. Required once you have premises or employees, and useful as business proof regardless.
  19. Week 9 — Set up accounting and the compliance calendarChoose accounting software (Zoho Books, Tally, QuickBooks). Set the chart of accounts. Create a shared calendar with every recurring deadline: GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B monthly, TDS deposit by the 7th, TDS returns quarterly, advance tax quarterly, DIR-3 KYC by 30 September, AOC-4 and MGT-7 after the AGM, LUT in April.
  20. Week 10 — Contracts and IPExecute an IP assignment deed from every founder covering all pre-incorporation and ongoing work product. Prepare a standard MSA and SOW template with a liability cap, an IP clause and a payment term of 30 days. Prepare an employment agreement template and a contractor agreement template with IP assignment and confidentiality.
  21. Week 11 — Apply for DPIIT recognitionOn startupindia.gov.in. Free. Write the four innovation answers with specific numbers and evidence. Attach the CoI, a pitch deck and any IP filings.
  22. Week 12 — Operational readiness reviewVerify: letterhead and invoice template carry name, registered office, CIN, phone, email and website per Section 12(3); name board is up; website landing page carries the same particulars; bank mandate works; payment gateway onboarded; insurance in place if needed; all documents backed up in two locations; one founder explicitly owns the compliance calendar.

Documents to collect — master checklist

From each founder / director / subscriber

  • PAN card — scanned, legible, name noted exactly as printed
  • Aadhaar card, with the linked mobile number confirmed working
  • Passport — mandatory for foreign nationals, apostilled or consularised
  • Voter ID or Driving Licence as an alternative identity proof
  • Proof of present address not older than two months: bank statement, electricity bill, telephone bill or mobile bill in the person's own name
  • Passport-size photograph, recent, plain background
  • Personal email address controlled solely by that person
  • Educational qualification and occupation details for the SPICe+ form
  • Father's name, date of birth and place of birth, exactly as per PAN records
  • DIN or DPIN if already held, and the DIR-3 KYC status
  • Signed DIR-2 consent to act as director
  • Signed MBP-1 disclosure of interest and DIR-8 non-disqualification declaration

For the registered office

  • Latest utility bill — electricity, telephone, gas or water — not older than two months, in the owner's name
  • No-objection certificate from the owner, naming the company and permitting use as registered office
  • Registered rent agreement or lease deed, if the premises are rented
  • Sale deed or property tax receipt, if owned
  • Photographs of the premises, interior and exterior with the name board — needed for GST registration
  • Nearest police station name, for the SPICe+ form

Agreements to have executed

  • Founders' / co-founders' agreement, stamped and signed
  • Intellectual property assignment deed from each founder, covering pre-incorporation work
  • Employment agreement template with confidentiality, IP assignment and notice period
  • Contractor / consultant agreement template with IP assignment and no employment relationship
  • Non-disclosure agreement template, mutual and one-way versions
  • Master services agreement and statement of work template, with a liability cap
  • Shareholders' agreement, once there are external shareholders
  • LLP agreement, if an LLP, filed in Form 3 within 30 days

Post-incorporation documents to generate and store

  • Certificate of Incorporation (Form INC-11) with CIN
  • Memorandum and Articles of Association as filed
  • Company PAN card and TAN allotment letter
  • Bank account details, IFSC, and the board resolution authorising it
  • Bank statement evidencing subscription money from each subscriber
  • Form INC-20A acknowledgement
  • First board meeting minutes, signed and bound
  • Auditor's consent letter, eligibility certificate and ADT-1 acknowledgement
  • Share certificates in Form SH-1, stamped, with counterfoils
  • Statutory registers: MGT-1, Register of Directors, Register of Charges, MBP-4, SBO register
  • GST registration certificate (Form REG-06) and GSTIN
  • LUT acknowledgement in Form RFD-11, filed for the current financial year
  • Udyam Registration Certificate
  • DPIIT recognition certificate, if obtained
  • Shops & Establishment registration certificate
  • Professional Tax enrolment and registration certificates
  • Trademark application acknowledgement and, later, the registration certificate
  • EPFO and ESIC establishment codes issued through AGILE-PRO-S
Store it twice, share it with both foundersKeep a single structured folder — 01-incorporation, 02-statutory-registers, 03-tax, 04-contracts, 05-banking — in a cloud drive both founders can access, plus an encrypted local backup. Every due-diligence process and every CA handover will ask for exactly these documents, and the founder who can produce them in an hour saves weeks.
